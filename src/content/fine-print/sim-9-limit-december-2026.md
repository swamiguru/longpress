applies:
  - who: You have fewer than nine connections
    status: no-change
    text: Nothing to do. You can still apply for new SIMs as normal.
  - who: You have nine connections and want a new one
    status: act
    text: Surrender at least one before 30 November. After 1 December the retailer's terminal rejects the application on the spot.[^1]
  - who: You have connections you don't recognise in your name
    status: act
    text: Report them on Sanchar Saathi before 30 November. An unknown SIM counted against you is now a practical problem, not just a fraud risk.[^3]
  - who: You need to replace a lost or damaged SIM
    status: check
    text: A replacement doesn't add to your count — it swaps the existing record. But you can't port the number for seven days after the swap.
  - who: You're in J&K, Assam or the North-East
    status: check
    text: Your regional cap is six, not nine. Same rules, lower threshold.[^1]
rules:
  - quote: >-
      "No individual subscriber shall have more than nine mobile connections
      across all service providers, except in Jammu & Kashmir, Assam and
      North-East circles, where the limit is six."[^1]
    plain: >-
      DoT set this ceiling in its August 2026 subscriber verification rules.
      Phase 1 (Aug–Nov 2026) operated post-facto: retailers could still issue
      SIMs and operators corrected excess allocations within a day. From
      1 December, that changes.
  - quote: >-
      "Phase 2 (from Dec 1, 2026) moves to real-time blocking at point of
      sale via the Digital Intelligence Platform."[^1]
    plain: >-
      The retailer's terminal now queries the DIP before the transaction
      completes. If you are at the limit, the application is rejected
      immediately. No SIM is issued, and no override is possible at the
      counter.
steps:
  - >-
    Go to sancharsaathi.gov.in, select 'Know Your Mobile Connections', enter
    your mobile number and verify with an OTP. You'll see every connection
    registered to your Aadhaar.[^2]
  - >-
    Tap 'Not My Number' next to any connection you don't recognise. The operator
    runs re-verification; connections that fail get disconnected.[^2]
  - >-
    Tap 'Not Required' next to connections you no longer need, or call your
    operator directly to surrender them. Allow at least a week for the DIP to
    update — do this before 25 November.[^2]
  - >-
    If you're at nine and genuinely need a new SIM, surrender one first. Wait
    for the DIP to reflect the change, then apply.[^1]
finePrint:
  - >-
    Data-only SIMs count — a 4G dongle or hotspot SIM registered to your Aadhaar
    is included in your nine.[^1]
  - Business connections under a company PAN or CIN are separate from your personal count.
  - >-
    A SIM informally given to someone else but never formally surrendered is still
    on your count. That is not DoT's problem; it is yours on 1 December.[^3]
  - >-
    A replacement SIM (same number, new card) does not add to your count — it
    replaces the existing record.
  - Dual-SIM devices don't double-count. Each physical SIM card is one connection.
unknowns:
  - >-
    How quickly a surrendered connection clears from DIP. It is unclear whether
    a retailer can override a rejection if the count hasn't updated by 1 December.
  - >-
    Whether enforcement will be uniform across all operators from day one. Phase 1
    had patchy compliance in its first weeks.[^1]
take: >-
  Most people are well under nine. The useful thing to do before December isn't
  about getting new SIMs — it's about fraudulent ones. If someone has taken eight
  or nine connections in your name without your knowledge,
  ==you are the one who gets blocked at the counter on 1 December==. The check on
  Sanchar Saathi takes about two minutes.
