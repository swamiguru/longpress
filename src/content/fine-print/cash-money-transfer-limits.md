  If you hand cash to a bank agent, kiosk or ATM to send money to someone, RBI's new rules from 11 November cap it at ==Rs 5,000 a transaction and Rs 25,000 a month==, while a bank branch will take up to Rs 50,000 in cash for one NEFT transfer.[^1][^2]

applies:
  - who: You send money home by paying cash at a local agent or kiosk
    status: check
    text: Your limit is Rs 5,000 per transaction and Rs 25,000 a month in total, counted against you as the sender. You need a verified mobile number and an ID document, and every transfer needs an extra authentication step.[^1][^3]
  - who: You walk into a bank branch with cash but no account there
    status: check
    text: You can deposit up to Rs 50,000 in cash to be sent by NEFT to any bank account. The directions don't set a monthly cap for this route.[^1][^4]
  - who: You send money from your account to someone who has no bank account
    status: check
    text: They can collect it in cash at an ATM, agent or other approved point, up to Rs 10,000 a transaction and Rs 25,000 a month for that one person.[^1][^2]
  - who: You pay by UPI or bank transfer from your own account to another account
    status: no-change
    text: The caps in these directions are about cash going in or coming out. Your usual app or bank limits stay as they are.[^1]
rules:
  - quote: "RBI (Domestic Money Transfer) Directions, 2026, para 9, dated 11 November 2026: cash remittances through business correspondents, ATMs and similar channels are capped at Rs 5,000 per transaction and Rs 25,000 a month per remitter."
    plain: The monthly Rs 25,000 is counted against you, not against the person you're sending to. Five Rs 5,000 transfers and you're done for the month at every agent, not just one.[^1]
  - quote: "RBI (Domestic Money Transfer) Directions, 2026, para 8: a walk-in customer can deposit cash at a bank branch for a NEFT transfer to a bank account of up to Rs 50,000."
    plain: If you need to send more than Rs 5,000 in one go and you don't have an account, the branch counter is the legal way to do it.[^1][^4]
  - quote: "RBI (Domestic Money Transfer) Directions, 2026, para 20: banks and non-banks may fix reasonable charges for the service."
    plain: There's no fee cap. Each bank and agent sets its own price, so ask before you pay.[^1][^2]

steps:
  - Keep your mobile number active and carry an ID like Aadhaar or a voter ID when you go to an agent. You're registered on that number and the document, and each transfer needs an extra authentication step on top.[^1][^3]
  - Ask the agent for the fee before you hand over cash. RBI lets banks set "reasonable" charges but puts no cap on them.[^1]
  - If you need to send more than Rs 25,000 a month, use a bank branch for NEFT, or open a basic savings account and send from it by UPI or IMPS.[^1][^4]
  - Check that the transfer reached the other side the same day. The rules expect transfers to go through in real time or close to it, so a long delay is a reason to complain.[^1]
  - If the agent or bank doesn't fix a problem, complain to the bank first, then take it to the RBI Ombudsman.[^1]
finePrint:
  - The Rs 25,000 monthly limit for agent cash is per sender, so splitting it across two agents or two receivers doesn't get you more.[^1]
  - Banks have to run checks on accounts that suddenly receive lots of these cash transfers and investigate unusual spikes, so a family account getting money from many senders may get questions.[^1]
  - The person receiving money only needs to give a name and address, and the bank has to record them, but the sender's details travel with every transfer.[^1]
  - Card-to-card transfers are outside these directions entirely.[^1]
unknowns:
  - Whether the limits are new or carried over. Outlook Money and Business Standard say the old limits were kept, but the 2024 circular these directions replace gave no figures of its own, and we didn't check the 2011 circular it pointed to.[^3][^5]
  - How each bank will apply the extra authentication step at agents and kiosks. The directions require it on every transaction but don't say what form it takes.[^1]
  - Whether real-time is a promise. Business Standard says transfers must be processed in real time, while the RBI text says they are expected to be.[^1][^3]
take: >-
  For most of us with UPI on our phones, this changes nothing. The people it does touch are the ones with the least room: a construction worker in Pune paying cash at a kiosk to get money to his family in Bihar. Rs 25,000 a month through an agent is enough for many of them, and the ID and authentication checks are a fair price for keeping mule accounts out of this channel. What bothers me is the line on fees. RBI wrote the limits down to the rupee but left charges at "reasonable", and agents are exactly where small senders get overcharged with no receipt. If you're sending money this way, or helping someone who does, ask the fee every time, and if you're crossing Rs 25,000 a month, a basic bank account and UPI will save you money.
