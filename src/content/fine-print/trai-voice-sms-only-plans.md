applies:
  - who: You use mobile data every day
    status: no-change
    text: Your bundled pack is not touched. This rule adds cheaper options, it removes none.[^1]
  - who: You keep a second SIM only for calls
    status: you-pay
    text: Today you pay for data you never use. A 28-day calls-and-SMS pack now costs Rs 177 on Jio and Rs 185 on Airtel and Vi.[^4][^5][^6]
  - who: A parent or relative uses a feature phone
    status: check
    text: Look under the "TRAI Mandated Packs" tab in the operator's app or website. These packs are meant for people who do not use data.[^1][^3]
  - who: You like to recharge on the same date every month
    status: act
    text: Operators must offer a pack that renews on the same date each month. Jio's costs Rs 197, Airtel's and Vi's Rs 206.[^1][^4][^5][^6]
rules:
  - quote: 'The regulation: operators must offer STVs "with appropriate reduction in tariff, exclusively for Voice and SMS."'
    plain: Wherever a pack with calls, SMS and data is sold for 30 days or less, a matching calls-and-SMS-only pack has to exist too, and it has to cost less.
  - quote: 'The regulation: validity "renewable on the same date of every month."'
    plain: Recharge on the 5th and the pack runs to the 5th of next month. Where that date does not exist, it ends on the last day of the month.
  - quote: 'The regulation: "at least one STV with validity" longer than those tiers.'
    plain: At least one longer calls-and-SMS-only pack must also be on sale, beyond the monthly and 30-days-or-less tiers.
  - quote: 'Explanatory memorandum: the amendment "neither notifies prices nor restricts the commercial flexibility" of operators.'
    plain: TRAI says cheaper, not how much cheaper. Operators decide the number.[^1]

steps:
  - If you use data every day, do nothing.
  - If you have a calls-only second SIM, note what you pay now for 28 days. Then open the operator's app and compare it with the calls-and-SMS-only pack under the "TRAI Mandated Packs" tab.[^3]
  - Move the SIM to the new pack only if the saving is real. Check what the pack includes, because it is meant to carry no data.
  - If an operator does not show these packs, raise it with the operator's complaint channel and keep a screenshot.
finePrint:
  - '"Appropriate reduction" is not a number. The April draft said "largely proportional". The final text dropped that, so the gap between the two packs may be small.[^1][^2]'
  - TRAI says the data component of each pack has to be assessed separately, so one operator can cut a lot on one validity and little on another.[^1]
  - Your 28-day pack does not turn into a 30-day pack. The rule covers calls-and-SMS-only packs, and operators keep their existing ones.
  - The rule is about packs, not your base plan or what happens when your SIM goes without a recharge.
  - TRAI's own notes say these packs were not easy to find in shops and apps. A separate TRAI direction from October now makes operators put them under a "TRAI Mandated Packs" tab, first in the prepaid recharge section of their apps and websites, and display them prominently at their own stores.[^1][^3]
unknowns:
  - The Gazette publication date, so the formal start date. The TRAI document is dated 21 September 2026 and we still haven't seen the Gazette entry. It matters less now, since all three operators have been selling the packs since late October.[^1][^4][^5][^6]
  - How big the discount is. Operators don't show the matching data pack next to each of these, so you have to compare the two yourself.
  - Whether operators follow the display direction at recharge counters as well as in their apps.
take: >-
  This is a modest rule that could save real money for people who use a second SIM only for calls. Because TRAI set no formula, the saving depends on each operator's pricing, and the draft's "largely proportional" wording was dropped from the final text. The first prices are out, Rs 177 to Rs 185 for 28 days, so judge each pack by what it costs per month against the bundled one you pay for today. Some coverage already says the price must be proportional. The final text does not say that.[^1][^2]
