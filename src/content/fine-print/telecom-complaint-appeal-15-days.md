applies:
  - who: You use Jio, Airtel, Vi or BSNL
    status: act
    text: The shorter appeal window applies to you. Once a complaint is marked closed, you have 15 days to push back if it isn't actually fixed.[^1][^2]
  - who: You have home broadband from a local provider
    status: check
    text: Your provider is covered if it had more than 10,000 subscribers at the end of the last financial year. The old Rs 5 crore turnover test is gone.[^1]
  - who: You have a complaint open right now
    status: act
    text: Note your docket number and the date it gets closed. That closing date starts the 15-day clock.[^2]
  - who: You have a disability
    status: check
    text: Providers must run a dedicated desk or helpline that can take your calls, and their apps and websites have to meet government accessibility standards.[^1]
rules:
  - quote: "TRAI Press Release No. 146/2026, 2 November 2026: the window to file an appeal after a complaint is disposed of is reduced from 30 days to 15 days."
    plain: The clock starts when the operator closes your complaint, not when you notice the problem is back. Miss the 15 days and the appeal route inside the company is shut.[^1]
  - quote: "TRAI Press Release No. 146/2026: complaint centres must work round the clock on all days, with human assistance for at least 16 hours a day and any automated or AI tools run with appropriate human oversight."
    plain: A chatbot at 2 am is allowed. For most of the day you can ask for a person, and the operator has to tell you when you're talking to a bot.[^1][^3]
  - quote: "TRAI Press Release No. 146/2026: the Appellate Authority must communicate its final decision within 15 days of receiving the appeal. The advisory committee route is removed."
    plain: Appeals go straight to senior staff with at least five years' experience and get an answer in about two weeks. Under the old route, TRAI found appeals often dragged past 40 days.[^1][^4]
steps:
  - When you complain by call, app, website or at a store, ask for the docket number and the expected fix date, and save them. The operator has to give you both and tell you if it's running late.[^2]
  - Most complaints with no other set deadline should be sorted within 3 days. If the operator closes yours and the problem is still there, appeal the same week. Don't wait out the 15 days.[^2][^3]
  - File the appeal wherever is easiest. The operator's app, website and portal now take appeals, as do email, post, a store visit and the consumer care number. Screenshot the closing message first.[^1]
  - If a chatbot keeps looping, ask for a human agent. One has to be available at least 16 hours a day.[^1]
finePrint:
  - The rules phase in. Reports say parts take effect 30, 60 or 90 days after publication in the Gazette, so not everything is live today.[^2]
  - Round the clock doesn't mean a person round the clock. Eight hours a day can be bots only, as long as a human is overseeing them.[^1]
  - A local broadband provider with 10,000 or fewer subscribers is outside these rules, even if it earns more than Rs 5 crore a year, because the turnover test was dropped.[^1]
  - "Penalties go to the regulator, not to you: Rs 1,000 for each wrongly closed complaint and Rs 5,000 for each wrongly closed appeal, capped at Rs 20 lakh a quarter per service area. You get no compensation out of it.[^3]"
unknowns:
  - Which parts start after 30, 60 or 90 days. TRAI's press release gives no start date, and we couldn't open the regulation text in this check to confirm.
  - The penalty amounts. TRAI's press release doesn't list them. Reports of the final rules say the quarterly cap is Rs 20 lakh, while May's draft proposed Rs 50 lakh.[^3][^4]
  - The 3-day default for fixing complaints and the rule that operators must say when you're talking to AI come from news reports of the final text, not from TRAI's press release.[^2][^3]
take: >-
  Most of this is overdue and good: an app button for complaints, a human for most of the day, a help desk for people with disabilities, and appeals that go to senior staff instead of a committee nobody could reach. The one change that works against you is the appeal window. TRAI sells it as speed, and a 15-day answer beats waiting 40 days, but it also means the operator's "resolved" SMS starts a clock you probably weren't watching. Ten days of patchy signal after a "fixed" ticket is normal in India, and now that's two-thirds of your window gone. So treat every closed complaint as a deadline. If it isn't fixed, appeal that week.
