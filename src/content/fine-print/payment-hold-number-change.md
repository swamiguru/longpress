applies:
  - who: You change the number or email on a wallet or prepaid card
    status: act
    text: Expect online payments from it to stop for at least 12 hours. Plan the change for a night, not the morning of a big payment.[^1]
  - who: Someone else changed your number or email
    status: act
    text: The hold is your window. If you get an alert about a change you didn't make, call the provider and block the account before the 12 hours run out.
  - who: You pay by UPI on Google Pay, PhonePe or a similar app
    status: check
    text: The rule doesn't name UPI. NPCI, which runs UPI, is covered and has to make its participants follow the mobile rules, but nobody has said plainly whether UPI apps apply the 12-hour hold.[^1][^3]
  - who: You pay through your bank's own app or net banking
    status: check
    text: Banks sit outside these directions. Their existing mobile-banking rules still apply, and your bank may run its own wait.[^1]
  - who: You haven't changed your number or email
    status: no-change
    text: Nothing changes for you.
rules:
  - quote: 'RBI Master Directions, 5 November 2026, para 31(g): after a "change in registered mobile number or email ID linked to the payment instrument", there shall be "a cooling period of minimum 12 hours before allowing any payment transaction" through online modes or channels.'
    plain: Twelve hours is the floor, and a provider can make you wait longer. It covers online channels in general, so a web checkout is held as well as the app.[^1]
  - quote: 'RBI Master Directions, 30 July 2024, para 31(7): the same minimum 12-hour cooling period.'
    plain: This rule is two years old. Large operators had to follow it from 1 April 2025 and medium wallet companies from 1 April 2026. The November 2026 directions restate it.[^2]
  - quote: 'Para 30, 5 November 2026: a facility "on its mobile application / website" that lets customers mark a transaction as fraud.'
    plain: Covered apps also have to let you flag a fraudulent payment from inside the app, and their payment alerts must name the merchant and put the OTP at the end of the message.[^1]
steps:
  - Change your number or email at night, or on a day when no payments are due. You can pay online again 12 hours later.
  - If you're switching SIMs, add the new number to your wallets while the old SIM still works, so alerts keep reaching you during the wait.
  - Keep another way to pay for those 12 hours, like a bank UPI app, a card or some cash.
  - If you get an alert about a number or email change you didn't ask for, call the provider's helpline and use the in-app fraud option right away. The hold gives you time to act.
  - If a covered wallet lets a payment go through minutes after a number change, report it to the provider and keep a screenshot. The rule says that shouldn't happen.
finePrint:
  - The directions cover non-bank operators only. RBI lists NPCI, card networks, payment aggregators, bill payment operators and large and medium prepaid issuers such as wallet companies. Your bank isn't on the list.[^1]
  - Small wallet companies don't have to follow any of this until 1 April 2028.[^1]
  - Wallets can add a second wait. RBI asks prepaid issuers to set "a suitable cooling period" for transfers and cash withdrawal after money is loaded electronically, with no number of hours given.[^1]
  - Reports calling this a new rule are off. The 12-hour hold has been due from big operators since April 2025. What's new on 5 November is the consolidated rulebook.[^2]
unknowns:
  - Whether UPI apps apply the 12-hour hold. RBI's text never names UPI. NPCI is covered and must make sure participants follow the mobile rules, and at least one compliance firm reads that as reaching UPI apps, but we found nothing from NPCI or any app confirming it.[^1][^3]
  - Which wallet companies count as large, medium or small. The directions point to RBI's oversight framework and don't name companies.
  - Whether your bank holds payments after a number change. The directions say existing mobile-banking instructions continue but don't say what they require.
  - What happened to RBI's April 2026 proposal for a one-hour delay on transfers above Rs 10,000 over UPI and IMPS. Comments were due by 8 May 2026, and we found no final rule in this check.[^4]
take: >-
  This is a good rule that most people have never heard of. SIM-swap fraud works because the thief is fast: take over your number, then empty the wallet before you notice your phone has lost signal. A forced 12-hour wait breaks that, and gives you time to call and block. The cost to you is small, since you plan a number change the way you'd plan a bank visit. What I want is a straight answer on UPI, because that's where most of our money moves now, and RBI's text leaves it to NPCI without saying so. Until NPCI or the apps confirm it, don't assume your UPI app has the same safety net.
