  From 1 November 2026, when an unsaved number calls you on 4G or 5G, your phone shows ==the name that number is registered under with its operator==. It's on for everyone by default, and names you've saved in your contacts still come first.[^1]

applies:
  - who: You get calls on a 4G or 5G phone
    status: no-change
    text: Nothing to switch on. Unsaved numbers will come with a name from the caller's operator records, and saved contacts keep the name you gave them.[^1]
  - who: You make calls
    status: check
    text: The name on your connection goes out to everyone who hasn't saved your number. If the SIM is in a parent's, spouse's or employer's name, that's the name people will see.[^1]
  - who: You're on 2G, 3G or a landline
    status: no-change
    text: Not covered yet. DoT says these networks will be considered separately, and it hasn't given a date.[^1]
  - who: You run a business or calling line
    status: act
    text: You can show a brand name instead of the registered one, but only a registered trademark or a GST-registered trade name, and your operator has to verify it first.[^1]
rules:
  - quote: 'DoT circular 01/Licensing Policy/2026, 13 October 2026, para 4(a): the service is to be given to every user "without the requirement of any separate request from such user."'
    plain: It's on by default. You don't have to ask for it, and the circular gives the person receiving the call no way to turn it off.[^1][^3]
  - quote: 'Para 4(b): the name field shows the name saved in the receiver''s contacts first; if the number isn''t saved, the caller''s registered name, or "International Call" for calls from abroad.'
    plain: Your phonebook wins. The operator's name only fills the gap for numbers you haven't saved, and foreign calls get a label instead of a name.
  - quote: 'Para 8: the names of users who have opted for calling line identification restriction "shall not be presented."'
    plain: Anyone who has turned on number hiding, called CLIR, won't show a name either.
steps:
  - Check whose name your own connection is in. Your operator's app (Jio, Airtel, Vi or BSNL) shows the account holder. That's the name strangers will see from 1 November.
  - If a family member uses a SIM registered to you, or you use one in someone else's name, decide whether you're fine with that name going out on every call. Fixing it means a transfer of ownership or a fresh KYC at your operator's store.
  - "Business numbers: keep your trademark certificate or GST registration ready and ask your operator for its preferred-name process. New connections need the original documents at the counter."
  - Don't treat a name on screen as proof of who's calling. If a caller claiming to be your bank or the police asks for money or an OTP, hang up and report it on 1930.
  - If names don't show up after 1 November, or yours shows wrong, complain to your operator. The circular requires every operator to record CNAP complaints and resolve them within a set time.[^1]
finePrint:
  - Only 4G and newer networks are covered. A caller on 2G, 3G or a landline won't show a name, and DoT hasn't said when that changes. In October 2025 DoT said those older networks would follow only once the technical problems with circuit-switched cores were sorted.[^1][^2]
  - The name comes straight from the subscriber record. A typo, an old surname or a company name on the connection will show exactly as it's recorded.
  - Calls from abroad never show a name, only "International Call". That label alone helps when a foreign call claims to be from an Indian bank or courier.[^1]
  - Operators can't keep your name once the call ends. The circular asks for a lookup built so the name isn't retained after the call.[^1]
unknowns:
  - Whether you'll be able to switch it off as the person receiving calls. The circular is silent. TRAI had recommended CNAP as a supplementary service and only "noted" DoT's default-on approach.[^2][^3]
  - How an ordinary prepaid or postpaid user turns on CLIR to hide their own name, and whether it costs anything. The circular mentions CLIR, but operators haven't published a process yet.[^1][^4]
  - Whether every phone will display the name. The circular says nothing about handsets. TRAI had suggested phones sold after a cut-off date be required to support it.[^2][^3]
  - Whether there's any charge. The circular doesn't mention a fee, and operators haven't said either way.[^1]
  - How fast complaints get fixed. The circular says "time-bound" but sets no number of days.[^1]
take: >-
  This was slow in coming, since TRAI's recommendations date to February 2024, but it's the right default. For years the only way to know an unknown caller was to hand your contact list to a private caller-ID app and trust crowd-sourced guesses. A name from the operator's KYC record is better for most people, especially parents who'll never install one of those apps. I have two reservations. Turning it on for everyone with no clear off switch goes further than TRAI asked for. And the name is only as honest as the SIM's paperwork, so a scammer on a SIM taken out in someone else's name will show that person's real name. Treat the name as a hint about who's calling, not proof.[^2]
