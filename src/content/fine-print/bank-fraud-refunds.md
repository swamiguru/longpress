rules:
  - quote: 'RBI Directions, 24 June 2026: a fraudulent transaction includes one "executed by a third-party using the credentials obtained from the customer through fraudulent means," and one made under coercion or duress.'
    plain: For the first time, a payment you were tricked or pressured into counts as fraud, not just one you never touched.[^1]
  - quote: '"The burden of proving customer liability in complaints involving fraudulent EBTs shall lie on the bank."'
    plain: The bank has to show you were careless. You don't have to prove you weren't.[^1]
  - quote: 'Compensation is "85 per cent of the net loss amount" or Rs 25,000, "whichever is less," for a loss of up to Rs 50,000, once in a lifetime.'
    plain: Small losses get most of the money back even when the bank says it was your fault. RBI itself pays 65% of it.[^2][^1]

steps:
  - Save 1930 and your bank's fraud number in your phone, and in your parents' phones. The five-day clock starts on the day of the fraud, not the day you notice it.
  - Check that your bank has your current mobile number and email. Not updating them counts against you as negligence.[^1]
  - If money goes, report it to your bank and to 1930 or cybercrime.gov.in the same day. Note the complaint number the bank has to give you on the spot.
  - "Credit card fraud: ask for a shadow reversal. The bank has to put the disputed amount back on your card, on hold, within five calendar days while it investigates."
  - If the bank turns you down, it has to give reasons and supporting details. Get them in writing before you escalate.
finePrint:
  - The Rs 25,000 can be claimed once in your life. On a joint account, only one holder can ever use it.[^1]
  - The scheme covers frauds in its first year only, so up to 31 December 2027. RBI hasn't said what happens after that.[^1]
  - Ignoring a specific scam warning from your bank, or downloading a malicious app, is listed as customer negligence. Read the warnings your bank sends.[^1]
  - The 45-day limit has no penalty attached if a bank misses it. Payments banks follow the same refund rules, but their version has no shadow-reversal clause.[^2]
unknowns:
  - How banks will classify a digital arrest case. The rules count a payment made under pressure as fraud, but don't say whether that's customer negligence. Those losses usually run into lakhs, well past the Rs 50,000 limit.
  - What the Supreme Court adds. On 29 October it said banks should compensate victims where a due-diligence lapse enabled the fraud, and asked RBI to notify new KYC rules by 30 November.[^4][^5] Whether any of that changes these rules before 1 January isn't known.
  - Whether the compensation scheme is extended or widened after its first year.
take: >-
  This is the first time RBI has put in writing that a payment you were tricked into making is fraud, and that the bank, not you, has to prove you were careless. That's a real shift, and bank-side failures like a missing SMS alert now mean a full refund with no argument. The compensation scheme is the weaker half. Rs 25,000, once in your life, for losses under Rs 50,000 helps someone who loses a month's rent to a fake delivery link. It does nothing for someone who loses lakhs to a fake police officer on a video call, and those are the cases the Supreme Court is angry about. I'd like RBI to take the court's hint and widen it. Until then, report within five days, because almost everything in these rules hangs on it.[^5]
