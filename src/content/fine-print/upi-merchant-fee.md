  - who: You run a small shop
    status: no-change

  - who: You run a bigger business
    status: you-pay

  - who: You pay other businesses by UPI
    status: check
    text: Shop-to-shop payments, like a retailer paying a wholesaler, may carry a flat Rs 5 per payment. That figure comes from unnamed sources, and NPCI hasn't announced it.[^11]
rules:
  - quote: 'NPCI, 13 October 2026: the fee "shall be implemented immediately post Diwali at a date to be notified separately."'
    plain: Nothing started on 15 October. Diwali was on 8 November, and as of 21 November there's still no date. Business Standard reports the implementation date "is yet to be finalised".[^4][^11]
  - quote: 'Q34: the framework "ensures consumers pay only the posted price."'
    plain: A shop can't add the fee to your bill. The price on the tag is the price you pay.
  - quote: 'NPCI to the Supreme Court, November 2026: the no-pass-on rule is "not merely a policy assurance but a mandatory direction backed by contractual enforcement mechanisms."'
    plain: A bank or UPI app that lets the fee reach you breaks its agreement with NPCI and can lose its UPI membership. Google Pay, PhonePe, Paytm and the rest still can't add a platform fee either.[^7][^8]

steps:
  - Keep paying by UPI exactly as you do now. Nothing on your side changes, before or after the fee starts.
  - If a shop adds a "UPI charge" to your bill, ask them to take it off. The rule doesn't allow it, and right now there isn't even a fee for them to pass on.
  - If they refuse, keep the bill or a screenshot and raise it through your UPI app's help section or your bank.
  - If your UPI app itself shows a fee on a payment, report it in the app and to your bank. NPCI has told the court that's a breach it can act on.[^7]
  - "Shop owners: nothing is deducted until NPCI announces the new date. Use the gap to check your monthly UPI QR total, since three months in a row above Rs 1 lakh moves you into the paid tier. If your bank offers a free QR code or soundbox, the new scheme is likely why."
finePrint:
  - The delay moves the start date only. NPCI's Supreme Court affidavit restates the 0.4% cap and the Rs 2,000 line, so the rates haven't moved.[^7]
  - "The new UPI Vyapaar Saksham scheme, in an NPCI circular dated 11 November, is paid for out of the fee itself: every bank and app puts 5% of its fee share into it. Banks get Rs 300 per QR code and Rs 750 per soundbox they set up at a small shop, and the scheme runs for three years from the day the fee starts. No fee, no money for the scheme.[^10]"
  - A Supreme Court case against the fee is still open. Advocate Anjan Datta's PIL challenges it, and in September the court refused to put the fee on hold while it hears the case.[^9]
  - "By NPCI's own numbers the fee is narrow: about 70% of UPI payments are person-to-person, and 96% of shop payments are Rs 2,000 or less, so it touches roughly 4% of shop payments.[^7]"
  - The small-shop exemption isn't permanent. Cross Rs 1 lakh a month on UPI QR for three straight months and you're reclassified.
unknowns:
  - The new start date. NPCI said "immediately post Diwali" on 13 October. Diwali has come and gone, and no date had been notified as of 21 November.[^4][^11]
  - Whether it slips into 2027. Reports on 18 and 19 November, citing Financial Express and industry sources, say the fee could wait until after the 2027 Assembly elections in five states. An industry official called that a worst case, and nothing official confirms it.[^12]
  - Whether shop-to-shop payments really get a flat Rs 5 fee. Only Business Standard has reported it, from two unnamed people, and NPCI didn't respond.[^11]
  - The government's FAQ still says the fee will "take effect from 15th October 2026". We expect it to be revised once a date is notified.[^1]
  - What happens to a shop that adds a surcharge at the counter. NPCI names consequences for banks and apps, up to losing UPI membership, but reports of its affidavit name none for a merchant.[^7]
  - We haven't been able to read the 11 November circular's own text, so the scheme details above come from reports of it.[^10]
take: >-
  Zero-fee UPI was always being paid for by someone, and a 0.4% charge on big shop payments is a fair way to share the bill. What bothers me is that the silence keeps getting longer. NPCI said "immediately post Diwali", Diwali has passed, and now the talk is of 2027. Meanwhile NPCI has written a whole scheme for small shops that runs on money from a fee nobody is collecting yet, which tells you the plan is still on even if the date isn't. For you at the counter nothing changes: no fee, and push back if a shopkeeper quietly adds Rs 20. In June 2025 the government called talk of a UPI fee "completely false", so a little skepticism about how this rolls out is earned.[^3]
