applies:
  - who: Your WhatsApp number's SIM is in your phone
    status: no-change
    text: You may see a one-time prompt asking WhatsApp to check the phone number on your SIM. Allow it inside the app and carry on.[^3]
  - who: You use WhatsApp Web or the desktop app
    status: no-change
    text: The plan to log everyone out every six hours was dropped in April 2026. Logouts will be "risk-based" instead, which hasn't been explained yet.[^2]
  - who: Your WhatsApp number's SIM is in another phone, or in a drawer
    status: act
    text: Once binding is enforced, the app is meant to stop working when the registered SIM isn't in the device. Move the SIM, or switch the account to the number you actually carry.[^1]
  - who: You live abroad and use an Indian number
    status: check
    text: If the Indian SIM stays active and in your phone, nothing should change. If you've swapped it for a local SIM, watch for prompts. The directions don't spell out what happens to you.
rules:
  - quote: 'DoT directions, via All India Radio (1 Dec 2025): apps must make it "impossible to use the app without that specific, active SIM", and log out web sessions periodically.'
    plain: Your account is meant to follow your SIM, not just your phone number.
  - quote: "Apps covered: WhatsApp, Telegram, Snapchat, Arattai, ShareChat, Josh, JioChat and Signal, under the Telecom Cyber Security Rules."
    plain: It isn't only WhatsApp. Any app that uses your Indian number as your ID is in scope.
steps:
  - Check that your WhatsApp and Telegram accounts use the number whose SIM is in your main phone.
  - If WhatsApp asks to check the phone number on your SIM, allow it. Only do this inside the app, never through a link someone sends you.[^3]
  - Keep that SIM active. A SIM that lapses for non-payment could lock you out of your chats once binding is enforced.
  - Keep your phone, with its SIM, nearby when you link WhatsApp to a laptop or tablet.
finePrint:
  - Android and iOS don't let apps read SIM details freely, so how apps will check the SIM is still unclear. UPI apps do it by sending an SMS from your phone.[^2]
  - The government's case is that accounts which keep working after a SIM is removed help scammers stay anonymous. It cites cyber-fraud losses of more than Rs 22,800 crore in a year.[^1]
  - WhatsApp asked for four to six more months in March 2026, saying it needed to avoid disrupting legitimate users.[^4]
unknowns:
  - What "risk-based" web logouts will mean in practice.[^2]
  - Whether 31 December 2026 is when binding switches on for everyone, or only when apps must file their compliance reports.[^2]
  - How the rule will treat NRIs, dual-SIM phones and eSIMs.
take: >-
  The goal is fair enough: a banned scam account shouldn't be back an hour later on the same number. But this is a blunt tool for a problem that mostly runs out of scam compounds abroad, and the six-hour logout showed how quickly a good intention turns into a daily annoyance for millions of people who use WhatsApp at work. Dropping it was the right call. For now, expect a SIM check prompt, keep your SIM active, and wait to see what "risk-based" turns out to mean.
