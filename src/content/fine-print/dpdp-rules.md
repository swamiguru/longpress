applies:
  - who: You use apps and websites in India
    status: check
    text: From 13 May 2027 you can ask what data an app holds, have it corrected or erased, withdraw consent and name someone to act for you. Replies are due within 90 days.[^1]
  - who: You stopped using a big app years ago
    status: check
    text: Large e-commerce and social platforms (2 crore+ users) and gaming platforms (50 lakh+) must erase your data after three years of inactivity, with 48 hours' notice first.[^2]
  - who: You're a parent
    status: check
    text: Apps will need verifiable consent from a parent before processing a child's data, except for things like healthcare, education and safety.[^1]
  - who: Your data leaks in a breach
    status: check
    text: The company must tell you without delay, in plain language, what happened and what you should do, and file a detailed report with the Data Protection Board within 72 hours.[^1][^2]
  - who: You run a business that handles customer data
    status: act
    text: Consent manager rules start on 13 November 2026 and everything else applies from 13 May 2027. Fines go up to Rs 250 crore.[^2]
rules:
  - quote: 'PIB, November 2025: requests for access, correction, updating or erasure must be answered "within a maximum of ninety days."'
    plain: Three months is the longest an app can take to deal with your request.
  - quote: 'PIB, November 2025: processing a child''s data needs "verifiable consent from a parent or guardian."'
    plain: Expect sign-ups for under-18s to ask a parent to prove who they are, possibly with a DigiLocker token.

steps:
  - There's nothing to do today. The rights you'll use most start on 13 May 2027.
  - After that, use an app's privacy or grievance contact to ask what it holds on you, or to correct or delete it. Keep a screenshot of your request.
  - If there's no reply within 90 days, or the answer doesn't satisfy you, complain to the Data Protection Board. Complaints are filed and tracked online.[^1]
  - Parents should expect apps to ask for proof of age or a DigiLocker token before a child can sign up.[^2]
finePrint:
  - The government can exempt its own agencies from parts of the law, so your rights against state bodies are weaker than against private apps.[^3]
  - Deletion on request isn't absolute. Companies can keep what another law requires them to keep, and must hold certain logs for a year.[^2]
  - Personal data can be sent abroad unless the government later restricts specific countries.[^2]
  - Appeals against the Board's decisions go to the telecom tribunal, TDSAT, not straight to a court.[^1]
unknowns:
  - The Data Protection Board exists in law, but as of September 2026 no chairperson or members had been appointed. MeitY invited applications on 6 May 2026.[^4]
  - MeitY proposed cutting the 18-month runway to 12 months in January 2026. No amendment has been notified, so 13 May 2027 still stands.[^3][^4]
  - How strictly apps will check a child's age in practice.
take: >-
  On paper this is a decent law. You get the right to see, fix and delete what apps hold on you, a 90-day deadline and breach notices written in plain language. In practice it's 18 months from the rules to the rights, and almost a year in, the body meant to enforce them still has nobody in the chair.[^4] Until the Board is staffed and the first fines land, treat this as a promise and keep being careful about what you hand over.
