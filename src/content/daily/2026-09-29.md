  - slot: 2
    kind: news
    heading: "TRAI makes data-free recharge plans mandatory"
    body: >-
      Paying for data on a SIM you only use for OTPs is about to become optional. TRAI's amendment, dated September 21, says every bundled plan with validity up to 30 days needs a voice-and-SMS-only twin with the same validity and a discount, and operators need at least one plan that renews on the same date each month. The 28-day plan survives, and how big that 'appropriate discount' turns out to be will decide whether this matters.
    problem: >-
      Millions of Indians keep a second SIM purely for bank OTPs, calls from family or an old number tied to accounts, and have been forced to buy data they never touch just to keep it alive.
    breakthrough: >-
      The Telecom Consumer Protection (Thirteenth Amendment) Regulations, 2026, dated September 21, require Jio, Airtel, Vi and others to offer a voice and SMS-only version of every bundled plan with validity up to 30 days, at the same validity and an appropriate discount. At least one voice/SMS plan must renew on the same calendar date every month. Operators get 30 days from Gazette publication to comply.
    catch: >-
      TRAI didn't set a price, so 'appropriate discount' could end up being small. And 28-day plans aren't banned, so the extra recharge a year stays unless you pick the calendar-month plan.
    forYou: >-
      Once the new plans show up in your operator's app, check your second SIM first. That's where the saving is most likely.

    heading: "Car sickness while reading? Your iPhone can help"
    body: >-
      Reading in a cab and feeling queasy ten minutes in? Go to Settings > Accessibility > Motion > Show Vehicle Motion Cues and set it to Automatic. Your iPhone then puts small dots along the edges of the screen that shift as the car turns and brakes, which eases the mismatch between what your eyes see and what your body feels. It needs iOS 18 or later.
    problem: >-
      Motion sickness comes from your eyes locked on a still screen while your inner ear feels every turn and pothole, and a lot of people just give up reading on the commute.
    breakthrough: >-
      Since iOS 18, iPhones have Vehicle Motion Cues under Settings > Accessibility > Motion. Animated dots at the screen edges move in step with the vehicle's motion, and on Automatic the phone switches them on when it detects you're in a moving vehicle. You can also add a toggle to Control Centre.
    catch: >-
      It helps, it doesn't cure. If you get properly sick in cars, looking out of the window is still the better fix.
    forYou: >-
      Turn it on to Automatic once and forget it; it only appears when you're actually moving.

    heading: "UPI Lite vs regular UPI: which one when"
    body: >-
      Most of your UPI payments probably don't need a PIN. UPI Lite is a small on-device wallet loaded from your bank, with up to Rs 5,000 in balance, Rs 1,000 per payment and no PIN, and your statement shows one top-up instead of forty small entries. The trade-off is that anyone holding your open phone can spend that balance, so keep it low and use regular UPI for rent and big purchases.
    problem: >-
      UPI made small payments effortless, then buried bank statements under dozens of Rs 20 debits and made you type a PIN at every chai stall.
    breakthrough: >-
      UPI Lite, supported in apps like Google Pay, PhonePe, Paytm and BHIM, lets you load up to Rs 5,000 into an on-device balance and pay up to Rs 1,000 per transaction without a PIN. Your bank statement shows the top-up, not every small spend, and you can set auto top-up. Regular UPI keeps the PIN and much higher limits.
    catch: >-
      No PIN means whoever has your phone open can spend the Lite balance. It's also only for small payments, so it's a companion to regular UPI, not a replacement.
    forYou: >-
      Turn on Lite with a low balance for daily small spends and keep regular UPI for anything you'd hate to lose.
