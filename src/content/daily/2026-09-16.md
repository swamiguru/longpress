    body: >-
      🔥 Techtember phones are flexing 200MP and even 250MP sensors, and the number is doing a lot of marketing work. Here's the truth: almost every shot gets pixel-binned down to roughly 12-16MP anyway, so the full-resolution mode is a headline, not your daily photo. What actually decides image quality is sensor size, image processing, the lens and the light. Chasing the megapixel count is just how brands upsell you.
    image: "/images/daily/2026-09-16/illustration_5.png"
    problem: >-
      Phone marketing has trained buyers to read a bigger megapixel number as a better camera, and the Techtember flood of 200-250MP phones leans hard on that.
    breakthrough: >-
      In practice these sensors combine many tiny pixels into one (pixel-binning), outputting ~12-16MP images; real-world quality is driven by sensor size, processing pipeline, lens quality and lighting — not the peak megapixel figure.
    catch: >-
      High-res modes aren't useless — they can help in bright light or heavy cropping — but they're situational, not the everyday default, and come with bigger files.
    forYou: >-
      Ignore the megapixel headline when choosing a phone; compare real sample photos, sensor size and processing instead, and leave your camera in the standard (binned) mode day to day.
