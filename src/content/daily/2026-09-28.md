      On October 1 Google launches the first Project Suncatcher test satellite, four of its TPU AI chips on SpaceX's Transporter-18 rideshare, built with Planet and powered by a 1kW solar panel.     problem: >-
      AI data centres are running into a power wall. They need enormous amounts of electricity and cooling, and new grid connections take years, so the big labs are looking at every odd source of energy, including the sun above the clouds.
    breakthrough: >-

Google built it with satellite company Planet and says its Trillium TPUs survived a radiation dose greater than a five-year mission in proton-beam tests, plus launch-vibration testing. The plan is a two-satellite constellation by 2027 and, eventually, 81-satellite compute clusters.
    catch: >-
      This flight mostly proves the hardware survives launch and space. Getting heat out of chips in a vacuum, linking satellites with lasers at coin-on-a-distant-target precision, and making launches cheap enough to compete with a shed full of servers are all still unsolved.
    forYou: >-
      Nothing changes for your Gemini queries this year. Watch the October results, though, because if the chips work in orbit, the energy argument around AI gets a lot more interesting.

    body: >-
      Security firm Nebty found DoppelCart, a network of about 119,000 fake shop sites, mostly on .shop domains, cloning 44,182 real brands with copied photos, product pages and prices up to 65% lower. The checkout sends your card details and even your bank OTP straight to the operators. With the big Indian sales starting around October 8, open the brand's own app instead of tapping ads, pay with UPI or a virtual card, and treat a miracle discount as a warning, not a win.
    problem: >-
      Sale season trains everyone to hunt for the lowest price and tap fast. That's exactly the habit fake shops feed on, because a clone of a real brand with a slightly better price looks like a lucky find rather than a trap.
    breakthrough: >-
      German security startup Nebty documented DoppelCart, a fake-shop operation spanning more than 119,000 domains that impersonate 44,182 brands, about two clones per brand. The sites copy catalogues and images, sometimes loading them straight from the real brand's servers, and their checkout code streams card numbers, expiry dates, CVVs, names and addresses to the operators in real time. It can also capture the one-time code your bank sends.
    catch: >-
      Most of the targeted brands are global rather than Indian, and a well-made clone is hard to spot by eye, so checklists only go so far. The safest move is simply not landing on the fake in the first place.
    forYou: >-
      For the Amazon and Flipkart sales starting around October 8, shop inside the official apps, type brand URLs yourself, and never enter an OTP on a store's own page. If a price makes you gasp, close the tab.

    catch: >-
