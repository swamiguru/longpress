    body: >-
      Every sensitive screenshot on your phone belongs in [Google Photos Locked Folder](https://support.google.com/photos/answer/10694388?hl=en). Aadhaar, PAN, cheque photos, UPI PIN reminders, OTP screenshots. Not the camera roll.
      
      In the Photos app, go to Collections or Library, then Utilities, then Locked Folder. Set a biometric unlock. Everything you move in leaves the camera roll and lives behind Face ID or a fingerprint. On-device only by default, so no cloud sync.
      
      It is not a new feature. It is just one of the most underused ones. In a year when half the Daily Five is about phishing and SIM-box fraud, keeping your identity screenshots off the main roll is the quietest two minutes you can spend.
    image: "/images/daily/2026-11-02/illustration_3.png"
    problem: >-
      Most people keep screenshots of Aadhaar, PAN, cheques, OTPs and KYC documents in the regular camera roll. One shared screen, one phone handover, one app with over-generous photo permissions, and sensitive IDs leak the way most leaks actually happen: quietly, and from your own side of the glass.
    breakthrough: >-
      Google Photos Locked Folder, available on Android and iOS, moves selected items off the camera roll into a biometric-locked space that does not show up in search, memories, or shared albums. On-device only by default; Android also offers an optional cloud backup for cross-device recovery.
    catch: >-
      Items in Locked Folder are no longer in your main album, so you have to remember to open the folder when you need them. If you factory-reset your phone and never turned on cloud backup, locally-stored items are gone. Decide the backup trade-off before you start moving things in.
    forYou: >-
      Spend two minutes today: Photos app, then Collections or Library, then Utilities, then Locked Folder. Move everything with your name, number, bank account, Aadhaar or PAN on it. Keep that camera roll clean of anything you would not hand a stranger if they borrowed your phone to make a call.

    heading: "An Indian number does not mean an Indian caller anymore"
    body: >-
      Lucknow police raided four apartments across Sushant Golf City, Para, BBD and Chinhat under Operation Digital Kavach. Inside, they found six 32-port SIM boxes with 192 slots total, three VPN gateways, 21 smart switches, UPS units and 18 batteries. The setup was built to route international calls through local Indian cellular lines, which is why so many digital-arrest scams this year have shown up with +91 numbers.
      
      The suspect, Saugat Asthana, had leased all four properties and is absconding, [The420](https://the420.in/raids-across-four-lucknow-apartments-unearth-192-slot-illegal-sim-box-hub/) reports. Two earlier arrests in Varanasi led police to him.
      
      The old intuition that a 'police inspector' calling from a +91 number must be real is now the con. The number was never the proof. The behaviour is. Any call that forbids you from telling your family is a scam, whatever ID it shows.
    image: "/images/daily/2026-11-02/illustration_4.png"
    problem: >-
      A generation of Indian phone users was trained to trust a +91 number as the signal that a caller is domestic. SIM-box networks like the Lucknow setup turn that trust against them: foreign scammers get Indian caller IDs, and 'ye to local number hai, scam nahi hoga' becomes exactly the hole the fraud exploits.
    breakthrough: >-
      On 2 November 2026, Lucknow police raided four apartments and seized six 32-port SIM boxes with an aggregate 192 slots, VPN gateways and smart switches. The setup was built to route international VoIP calls through Indian SIM cards and had been feeding digital-arrest frauds across multiple states. The lead suspect is absconding; two earlier arrests in Varanasi led to the raid.
    catch: >-
      No arrests were made in the raid itself. The full count of digital-arrest cases this one network fed is still being traced through call records. And one hub down does not take the business model down: more SIM-box farms exist, and more will be set up as long as there is money to steal.
    forYou: >-
      Pass this to anyone over 55 at home. Teach the rule once: 'Agar police kahe kisi ko mat bataana, matlab police nahi hai.' Any threat of arrest over a video call, any request to transfer money to a 'verification' account, any demand for secrecy from family means hang up. Then report the number on Sanchar Saathi's Chakshu portal, and dial 1930 if any money has moved.
    question: "Does a +91 phone number mean a digital-arrest scam call is from inside India?"
    answer: >-
      No. On 2 November 2026, Lucknow police seized six 32-port SIM boxes with 192 slots total that were routing international scam calls through Indian cellular lines, so the +91 number on your screen says nothing about where the caller actually is. Treat any 'police' or 'customs' call that forbids you from telling family as a scam, whatever the number shows.
    qa:
      - q: "What is a SIM box and how does a digital-arrest scam use it?"
        a: "A SIM box is a device that routes voice-over-IP calls from abroad through local SIM cards, so a call that originates overseas arrives on your phone with a local caller ID. In the Lucknow network, 192 SIM slots across six boxes carried international VoIP traffic to victims who then saw +91 numbers and believed the 'policeman' or 'customs officer' was calling from India."
      - q: "What should you do if you get a 'police' call that threatens arrest?"
        a: "Hang up. Do not switch to video. Any genuine Indian police investigation does not demand video calls, bank transfers or secrecy from family. Report the number on the Sanchar Saathi Chakshu portal or app. If any money has moved, call 1930 immediately and file a complaint at cybercrime.gov.in. Secrecy is the con; a request to not tell anyone is the tell."
