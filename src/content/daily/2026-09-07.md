    body: >-
      That blurry WhatsApp photo isn't your camera — it's WhatsApp compressing everything to 'Standard' by default. One setting fixes it forever: Settings → Storage and data → Media upload quality → HD. Now every photo and video sends sharp automatically, with no tapping 'HD' each time. 🔥
    problem: >-
      WhatsApp defaults every image and video to 'Standard' quality to save data, so the photos you send land softer and blurrier than the original — and most people never realise there's a switch.
    breakthrough: >-
      WhatsApp has a default media-quality setting: go to Settings → Storage and data → Media upload quality and choose HD. From then on, everything you send goes out at the higher quality automatically — no per-photo tapping.
    catch: >-
      HD media uses more mobile data and storage, so on a tight plan you'll notice bigger uploads. It also doesn't magically improve a photo that was low-res to begin with.
    forYou: >-
      Flip it to HD once tonight and every photo you share from then on looks noticeably sharper — especially screenshots, documents and group memories people actually zoom into.

    heading: "5,000 Dropbox accounts hacked — none had 2FA"
    body: >-
      Around 5,000 Dropbox accounts were just breached, with hackers viewing and downloading people's files. The one thing every compromised account had in common? No two-factor authentication. The fix is a 5-minute weekend job: turn on 2FA on your email, cloud storage and socials — and use an authenticator app, not SMS. ✅
    problem: >-
      Passwords alone keep failing. Reused logins, leaked emails and single-sign-on flaws mean one weak link can hand attackers your files — and most people still rely on just a password.
    breakthrough: >-
      Between August 4 and 21, ~5,000 Dropbox accounts were accessed via a flaw in Lenovo ID single sign-on: attackers registered a Lenovo ID against a victim's email because verification was skipped, then walked in. Dropbox has since secured the accounts and notified users and regulators.
    catch: >-
      The crucial detail: none of the breached accounts had multi-factor authentication enabled, which is exactly what would have stopped this. SMS codes help but can be SIM-swapped, so an authenticator app is stronger.
    forYou: >-
      Spend five minutes today turning on 2FA — ideally an authenticator app — for your email, cloud storage and main social accounts. It blocks the vast majority of these takeovers, even if your password leaks.
