    kind: myth-buster
    heading: "Stop force-closing your apps to 'save battery'"
    body: >-
      The myth: swiping away every background app saves battery and speeds up your phone. The truth: both iOS and Android already freeze apps in the background — they're not sitting there draining power. Force-quitting means the phone has to cold-launch the app from scratch next time, which uses more battery and CPU than simply resuming it. The only time to force-close is when an app is genuinely frozen or misbehaving. Swipe-killing everything is busywork that quietly costs you battery.
    problem: >-
      Millions of people habitually swipe away all their apps believing it saves battery and makes the phone faster, treating it as basic phone hygiene.
    breakthrough: >-
      Modern iOS and Android suspend background apps automatically, using little to no resources while frozen. Reopening a force-quit app triggers a full cold start, which draws more CPU and battery than resuming a suspended one.
    catch: >-
      The exception is real but narrow: force-close an app that's actually crashed, frozen, or clearly malfunctioning — that's genuine troubleshooting, not routine cleanup.
    forYou: >-
      Leave your apps alone. Skip the swipe-kill ritual and only force-quit the odd app that's misbehaving — you'll get slightly better battery, not worse.
