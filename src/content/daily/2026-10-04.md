    heading: "Apple is locking down your Mac's master key because of AI agents"
    body: >-
      Apple is tightening Full Disk Access on macOS and named AI agents as the reason. That permission lets an app read your Mail, Messages, Safari data and Time Machine backups, and plenty of us granted it to some helper app years ago and forgot. There's no date or macOS version yet, but you don't need to wait for one: open System Settings > Privacy & Security > Full Disk Access and switch off anything you don't recognise.
    image: "/images/daily/2026-10-04/illustration_1.png"
    problem: >-
      Full Disk Access is the broadest permission a Mac app can get. It skips the per-folder prompts and reaches Mail, Messages, Safari, Home data and backups, and a lot of us clicked it once for a utility and never looked again. Hand that to an autonomous agent and the blast radius is your whole digital life.
    breakthrough: >-
      On October 2 Apple posted "Updates to Full Disk Access in macOS" on its developer news site, saying some developers use the permission in ways that put users at risk without their full knowledge, and that the risk will grow as AI agents get more capable. Granting it will now take very explicit user action. Coverage pointed to recent worries around Meta's Muse agent and a reported flaw in ChatGPT's Mac app, though Apple itself named nobody.
    catch: >-
      No timeline, no macOS version, and no detail yet on what the new consent flow looks like. Apps that genuinely need deep access, like backup tools, will have to adapt, and some will be annoying about it.
    forYou: >-
      Do the audit now instead of waiting for Apple. Anything in that list you can't name, or haven't opened in months, gets switched off; if it breaks something, you'll find out fast and can turn it back on.

    body: >-
      One WhatsApp setting worth turning on before the sales start on October 8: Settings > Privacy > Calls > Silence unknown callers. Calls from numbers outside your contacts stop ringing, but they still land in your Calls tab and notifications, so the delivery guy isn't lost. You call back when it suits you. While you're in Privacy, run Privacy checkup too; it takes two minutes.
    image: "/images/daily/2026-10-04/illustration_3.png"
    problem: >-
      Festive sale season brings a spike in fake delivery, refund and KYC calls, and WhatsApp calls are a favourite route because they feel more personal than a random SIM call.
    breakthrough: >-
      WhatsApp has a built-in switch at Settings > Privacy > Calls > Silence unknown callers. Turn it on and calls from numbers not saved in your contacts don't ring at all, but they're still logged in the Calls tab and shown as a notification.
    catch: >-
      Genuine calls from new numbers, like a courier or a new colleague, go quiet too. You'll need to glance at the Calls tab now and then.
    forYou: >-
      Turn it on now, before October 8. Then open Settings > Privacy > Privacy checkup and tighten who can add you to groups and see your last seen.

    heading: "The festive sale price is not always the lowest of the year"
    body: >-
      Myth: the Big Billion Days or Great Indian Festival price is the lowest you'll see all year. Both start October 8. The headline price often needs one bank's card (Amazon's is SBI, 10% instant), the struck-out MRP is rarely what anyone paid, and lots of deals come back at Diwali. Check the price history, work out what you'd pay without the card offer, and set your max before a countdown timer sets it for you.
    image: "/images/daily/2026-10-04/illustration_5.png"
    problem: >-
      Sale banners are designed to make you feel you'll never see this price again. The struck-out MRP, the countdown timer and the 'lowest ever' tag all push the same way, and most people don't stop to check.
    breakthrough: >-
      Amazon's Great Indian Festival starts October 8, and Flipkart's Big Billion Days opens early access the same day with the wider sale from October 9. Amazon has tied up with SBI for a 10% instant discount on eligible cards. The headline prices you'll see usually bake in offers like that.
    catch: >-
      Some sale prices really are the year's best, especially on older models being cleared out, so this isn't a reason to skip the sale entirely. It's a reason to check before you click.
    forYou: >-
      Look up the product's price history on any tracker, note the price without bank offers, and write down the most you'll pay before the sale starts. If the number doesn't beat that, close the tab; Diwali is only a few weeks later.
