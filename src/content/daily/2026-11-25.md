    heading: "RBI's Stop Switch freezes your bank in one tap"
    body: >-
      The RBI has drafted a Stop Switch that would let any bank customer freeze all outgoing debits, new payment mandates and new UPI app registrations in one go, with the rules applying from 1 July 2027 if they are finalised. It's one of the few fraud rules that assumes the victim will act, not just the bank. That's a sensible idea, but July 2027 is a long wait for a problem costing people lakhs today. Comments are open till 14 December.
    image: "/images/daily/2026-11-25/illustration_1.png"
    problem: >-
      Digital arrest and UPI scams work because the victim moves the money themselves, usually while someone on the phone is rushing them. Once a transfer you authorised under pressure has gone out, getting it back is a fight.
    breakthrough: >-
      The RBI floated draft amendments to its Commercial Banks (Responsible Business Conduct) Directions, as [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/rbi-proposes-stop-switch-to-block-digital-payment-frauds/) reported on 23 November. The headline is a Stop Switch: one action that disables all outgoing debits, new payment mandates and new UPI app registrations. Incoming credits and existing standing instructions keep working. You could trigger it through branch, ATM, app, net banking or IVR, and switching it back on needs at least the same authentication. There's also an optional Trusted Person Mechanism, where a nominated adult approves high-value transfers within one hour. Comments close 14 December.
    catch: >-
      It's a draft, and the start date is 1 July 2027, so none of this helps anyone during this wedding-and-sale season. The Trusted Person threshold isn't stated yet, and a freeze only works if you remember to hit it before the scammer's call ends.
    forYou: >-
      Nothing to switch on today. Keep your UPI daily limit low, and find out which channel your bank would let you freeze from. If your parents get scam calls, the Trusted Person idea is the part to watch.
    question: "What is the RBI's proposed Stop Switch for bank accounts in India?"
    answer: >-
      The RBI has drafted a Stop Switch that would let bank customers instantly block all outgoing debits, new payment mandates and new UPI app registrations. Comments are due by 14 December 2026, and if the draft is finalised, the rules would apply from 1 July 2027.
    qa:
      - q: "When would the RBI Stop Switch take effect for bank customers?"
        a: "If the draft is finalised, from 1 July 2027. The RBI is taking public feedback on its Commercial Banks (Responsible Business Conduct) Fifth Amendment Directions, 2026 until 14 December 2026. Nothing is switched on today, and the final rules could still change, including the Trusted Person threshold."
      - q: "Does the RBI Stop Switch block incoming UPI payments to my account?"
        a: "No. The draft blocks outgoing debits, new payment mandates and new UPI app registrations. Incoming credits and existing standing instructions keep working. Turning it back on would need at least the same authentication as turning it on, through branch, ATM, app, net banking or IVR."
      - q: "What is the Trusted Person Mechanism in the RBI draft?"
        a: "It is a voluntary option where an adult you nominate approves high-value transfers above a threshold within one hour. The trusted person has no legal authority over your account, and changes to the setup face a 24-hour cooling-off period. The threshold amount has not been stated."

    heading: "Check which laptops are still logged into your WhatsApp"
    body: >-
      India's Home Ministry has warned about WhatsApp Web misuse and advised against sensitive work on it, and the simplest response is a check you can do from your phone in under a minute. On Android, tap the three dots, then Linked devices; on iPhone, open Settings, then Linked devices. Each entry shows when it was last active, so anything you don't recognise, or any shared office or cyber-cafe PC, can be logged out right there.
    image: "/images/daily/2026-11-25/illustration_3.png"
    problem: >-
      Most people scanned a WhatsApp Web QR code once, on some laptop, and never thought about it again. That session stays logged in until somebody ends it.
    breakthrough: >-
      India's Home Ministry has [warned about WhatsApp Web misuse](https://inc42.com/buzz/home-ministry-warns-of-whatsapp-web-misuse-advises-against-sensitive-work/) and advised against sensitive work on it, Inc42 reported on 23 November. The check takes under a minute: on Android tap the three dots, then Linked devices; on iPhone open Settings, then Linked devices. Each device shows when it was last active. Tap any you don't recognise, or any shared or old office PC, and choose Log out. WhatsApp's own [help page](https://whatsapp.com/faq/en/web/28080003) walks through the same steps.
    catch: >-
      This only ends sessions on linked devices. It does nothing about someone who already saw your messages, and it won't help if a scammer has your SIM or OTP. You'll also need to scan the QR again next time you use that device.
    forYou: >-
      Do the check today, then repeat it after any time you've used WhatsApp Web on a computer that isn't yours. If you only ever use WhatsApp on your phone, the list should be empty.
    question: "How do I log out of WhatsApp Web on all linked devices?"
    answer: >-
      Open WhatsApp on your phone and go to Linked devices: the three-dot menu on Android, Settings on iPhone. Tap each device you don't recognise or no longer use and choose Log out. Your phone stays logged in and your chats stay put.
    qa:
      - q: "What did India's Home Ministry warn about WhatsApp Web?"
        a: "As reported on 23 November 2026, the Home Ministry warned about misuse of WhatsApp Web and advised against doing sensitive work on it. The practical fix for ordinary users is to end any session you left open on a shared or old computer, which takes under a minute from the phone."
      - q: "Does logging out of a linked device delete my WhatsApp chats?"
        a: "No. Logging out only ends that device's session. Your chats stay on your phone and in your account, and you can link the device again later by scanning a QR code. The browser or desktop copy simply stops showing your messages until you sign in again."

    body: >-
      Maharashtra's FDA ordered on 24 November 2026 that influencers, supplement sellers, gyms and e-commerce platforms face fines up to Rs 10 lakh for misleading health claims, and that saying a supplement treats a disease or that doctors recommend it is barred. About time. Self-regulation had 60 out of 60 ads breaking the code with zero consequences, so this is the first rule with teeth. Creators should read it as the start of something, not a Maharashtra quirk.
    image: "/images/daily/2026-11-25/illustration_5.png"
    problem: >-
      Health supplements are the easiest thing for an influencer to sell and the hardest for a viewer to check. 'My doctor recommended it' costs nothing to say.
    breakthrough: >-
      On 24 November, Maharashtra FDA commissioner Tukaram Mundhe issued a compliance order for supplement makers, sellers, gyms, pharmacies, hospitals, e-commerce platforms and influencers, per [MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/11/223-maharashtra-fda-influencers-health-supplement/). Fines run up to Rs 10 lakh for misleading ads, false health claims, unlicensed operation or harmful adulteration, and up to Rs 5 lakh for other violations. Claiming a supplement treats a disease, or that doctors recommend it, is now barred. The drive covers 17 establishments across six divisions, with products worth over Rs 1.38 crore seized.
    catch: >-
      It's one state's order, and enforcement against individual creators is untested. ASCI reviewed 60 supplement ads and found all 60 broke its code, but it can't fine anyone, which is a fair explanation for why a state regulator stepped in.
    forYou: >-
      If you make content, take 'doctor recommended' out of your supplement scripts, in every state. If you watch it, treat any health claim in a sponsored post as an ad, because it is one.
    question: "Can Maharashtra fine influencers for misleading supplement claims?"
    answer: >-
      Yes. Maharashtra FDA commissioner Tukaram Mundhe issued a compliance order on 24 November 2026 covering influencers among others, with fines up to Rs 10 lakh for misleading ads or false health claims. Claiming supplements treat diseases, or that doctors recommend them, is barred. It applies in Maharashtra only.
    qa:
      - q: "What are the Maharashtra FDA fines for supplement violations?"
        a: "Up to Rs 10 lakh for misleading ads, false health claims, operating without a food safety licence or harmful adulteration, and up to Rs 5 lakh for other violations. The drive covers 17 establishments across six divisions, with products worth over Rs 1.38 crore seized so far, according to the report."
      - q: "Does the Maharashtra influencer supplement order apply across India?"
        a: "No. It is a Maharashtra FDA order, so it applies within the state. Nationally, ASCI's code still has no power to penalise, even though it found all 60 supplement ads it reviewed breached its rules, so enforcement elsewhere depends on whether other states copy the order."
