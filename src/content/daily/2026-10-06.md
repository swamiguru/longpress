    heading: "India puts Rs 130 crore behind its own CCTV AI chip"
    body: >-
      India's Technology Development Board has committed Rs 130 crore to Bengaluru's BigEndian Semiconductors for Project VeerAI, a Rs 260 crore effort to build an AI vision chip for cameras. It starts with CCTV and surveillance, then defence, automotive, industrial and medical. It isn't a flashy target, which is why I like it: whoever designs the chip inside a camera has a real say in where the footage goes.
    image: "/images/daily/2026-10-06/illustration_2.png"
    problem: >-
      Most smart cameras in India run on chips designed abroad, and the government's stated worry is that this creates risks around data security, privacy and technology sovereignty.
    breakthrough: >-
      On October 5 the TDB, under the Department of Science and Technology, signed a Rs 130 crore agreement with BigEndian Semiconductors, half of the Rs 260 crore total project cost. The goal is a camera-centric AI vision system-on-chip, taken from TRL-5, roughly a validated prototype, to TRL-9, deployment-ready, aimed at India and the Global South.
    catch: >-
      There's no public shipping date, and moving from prototype to a chip that camera makers actually buy at scale is where most hardware projects stall.
    forYou: >-
      Not something you'll buy, but if it ships it shapes the cameras watching your street and office. The milestone to watch is a named camera maker putting VeerAI in a product.

    body: >-
      If you post publicly on X, those posts can be used to train Grok unless you opt out. Go to Settings and privacy, then Privacy and safety, then Grok & Third-party Collaborators, and untick the data sharing box. The path is the same on the app and the web. It doesn't claw back anything already used, but it keeps future posts out.
    image: "/images/daily/2026-10-06/illustration_3.png"
    problem: >-
      X uses public posts and your Grok interactions to train its AI by default, and the switch sits a few menus deep where most people never look.
    breakthrough: >-
      The opt-out lives under Settings and privacy > Privacy and safety > Grok & Third-party Collaborators (on some versions it's grouped under Data sharing and personalisation). Untick the box that allows your public data and Grok interactions to be used for training.
    catch: >-
      It only applies going forward, and if you use the standalone Grok app you need to turn off Improve the model in its own Data Controls too.
    forYou: >-
      Twenty seconds, once, on whichever device you're holding. Worth doing even if you rarely post, since replies count too.

It's not a total ban, and private use stays allowed. Dutch retailer Hans Anders has already paused Ray-Ban Meta sales, and UK cinemas are restricting camera glasses. Meta points to its recording light, which disables the camera if covered.
