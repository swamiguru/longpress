    heading: "Check how many SIMs are registered in your name"
    body: >-
      A lot of scam calls run on SIMs issued with someone else's KYC, and the person whose name is on them usually has no clue. The DoT's Sanchar Saathi app and website list every mobile number tied to your ID. Open Know Mobile Connections in Your Name, enter your number and the OTP, and anything you don't recognise can be reported as Not my number.
    problem: >-
      SIM fraud in India often starts with a connection someone else opened on your documents, and since it never rings your phone, you have no reason to notice it.
    breakthrough: >-
      The Department of Telecommunications' Sanchar Saathi portal and app show all mobile connections registered against your identity. After an OTP check you can mark any number as Not my number, which sends it to the operator for re-verification and disconnection, or mark old SIMs you no longer use as Not required.
    catch: >-
      The list is only as good as the operators' records, and a disconnection request isn't instant. It also can't tell you who opened a SIM, only that it exists.
    forYou: >-
      Spend two minutes on it today, then do the same with your parents' numbers sitting next to them, since older relatives are usually the ones scammers count on.

It finds robots are technically capable of 74% of physical tasks in US jobs, about 34% of total working hours, yet only 0.3% of tasks are cost-competitive today, and at a 3% yearly price decline it would take roughly 40 years to reach 10%. Drivers top the exposure list, thanks to autonomous vehicles.
    catch: >-
      It's US data, and India's far cheaper labour stretches that timeline even further here. Prices could also fall faster than the historical 3% if humanoid production really scales, so 40 years is a baseline, not a promise.
    forYou: >-
      Treat robot videos as entertainment and the cost curve as the real story. If you drive for a living, that's the one area worth keeping an eye on.
