    body: >-
      One setting almost nobody turns on directly blocks a common way accounts get hijacked: set a SIM PIN. If your phone or SIM is stolen, a thief can pop that SIM into another phone and start receiving your OTPs and 2FA codes. A SIM PIN stops that cold — the SIM won't work in any device without the PIN. On iPhone it's Settings > Cellular > SIM PIN; on Android it's Settings > Security > SIM card lock. One caveat: pick a PIN you'll remember, because too many wrong tries locks the SIM and you'll need the PUK from your carrier.
    problem: >-
      If your phone or SIM is stolen, a thief can move the SIM to another phone and start receiving your OTPs and 2FA codes — a common route to account takeover, especially with the recent wave of phishing and credential attacks.
    breakthrough: >-
      Turning on a SIM PIN locks the SIM itself, so it won't work in any device without the PIN. iPhone: Settings > Cellular > SIM PIN. Android: Settings > Security > SIM card lock.
    catch: >-
      Choose a PIN you'll actually remember — too many wrong attempts locks the SIM and you'll need the PUK code from your carrier to recover it.
    forYou: >-
      Spend 90 seconds enabling it today. It's one of the highest-value, lowest-effort protections against SIM-swap fraud.

    heading: "Stop swiping away your apps to 'save battery.'"
    body: >-
      Time to kill a habit: swiping away every app to 'save battery.' On modern iOS and Android, apps you're not using don't run freely in the background — the OS freezes or suspends them and they sip basically zero battery or RAM. When you force-close one and reopen it, the phone has to cold-start the whole thing from scratch, which uses more battery and CPU, not less. What to actually do: leave them alone, and only force-quit an app that's genuinely frozen or misbehaving.
    problem: >-
      Many people compulsively force-close every background app, believing it saves battery and speeds up the phone.
    breakthrough: >-
      On modern iOS and Android, unused apps are frozen or suspended by the operating system and use almost no battery or RAM — there's nothing to 'clear' by swiping them away.
    catch: >-
      Force-closing and then reopening an app actually costs more: the phone has to cold-start it from scratch, using extra battery and CPU.
