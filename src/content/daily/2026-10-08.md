    heading: "Samsung's record $80B quarter is why your laptop costs more"
    body: >-
      Samsung expects about 107.4 trillion won (roughly $80 billion) in operating profit for July to September, around nine times last year and the biggest quarter any tech company has reported. Almost all of it comes from memory chips, which AI data centres are buying as fast as Samsung can make them. The flip side lands in Indian shops: RAM prices are up two and a half to three times, and laptop and phone prices have been climbing all year. If you need a device, waiting for prices to fall is not much of a plan.
    image: "/images/daily/2026-10-08/illustration_2.png"
    problem: >-
      AI data centres need huge amounts of memory, and the companies that make it can't add factories fast enough. That squeeze doesn't stay in the server room; the same DRAM and storage go into every phone and laptop.
    breakthrough: >-
      Samsung's preliminary Q3 numbers, released October 8, point to about 107.4 trillion won in operating profit (around $80.2 billion) on roughly 195 trillion won in revenue, up 127% year on year. It is shifting capacity toward high-margin HBM for AI chips, and has already raised prices on many of its own smartphones and electronics. The full breakdown comes on October 29.
    catch: >-
      These are preliminary figures without a division split, so we don't yet know how hard Samsung's own phone business was squeezed. Price forecasts for India, like IDC's view that relief may not come until late 2027, are estimates and can move.
    forYou: >-
      If your laptop or phone is dying, buy during the festive sales rather than holding out for a drop that probably isn't coming this year. Spend on RAM and storage at purchase, because that's the part getting most expensive and most laptops won't let you upgrade it later.

    kind: tip
    heading: "Add after: to a Google search to skip stale results"
    body: >-
      Google supports two date operators most people never use. Add after:2026-09-01 to the end of a search and you only get pages from after that date; before: does the opposite, and you can combine them to box in a range. It's the quickest fix for searches like best phone under 30000 that keep serving you listicles from three years ago.
    image: "/images/daily/2026-10-08/illustration_3.png"
    problem: >-
      Google often ranks old, well-linked pages above newer ones, which is fine for history and useless for prices, phone recommendations or anything that changes every few months.
    breakthrough: >-
      Type after:YYYY-MM-DD or before:YYYY-MM-DD in the search box alongside your words, for example best phone under 30000 after:2026-09-01. A year on its own, like after:2025, also works. The Tools menu has a time filter too, but the operator is faster and you can save it in a bookmark.
    catch: >-
      Dates come from what Google thinks a page's publish date is, and some sites quietly update old posts with a new date. Treat it as a strong filter, not a guarantee.
    forYou: >-
      Use it for anything price or launch related, especially during sale season. If you're on mobile, it works the same in the Google app and Chrome's address bar.

    body: >-
      ElevenLabs says India is now its second-biggest market, and the numbers behind that are about you. Indian businesses ran 100 million conversations through its voice agents in the past year, and more than 70% were in Hindi, Kannada, Tamil or Telugu. I'm fine with bots taking routine calls, honestly they're often quicker. What isn't fine is a bot that pretends to be a person, or one that loops you forever with no way to reach a human.
    image: "/images/daily/2026-10-08/illustration_4.png"
    problem: >-
      Indian customer support runs on huge call volumes in many languages, and companies have spent years trying to cut that cost. Voice AI that sounds natural in Hindi or Tamil makes replacing human agents a lot easier.
    breakthrough: >-
      At its India summit in Bengaluru on October 6, ElevenLabs said more than 25,000 Indian developers and businesses use its API each month, about 2.5x a year ago, and its Eleven v4 model now covers 14 Indian languages with all 22 official ones planned. CARS24 runs over 400 voice agents that have handled three million call minutes, and Razorpay has handled more than 35,000 Hinglish merchant calls. Karnataka also signed an MoU with the company for Kannada voice apps.
    catch: >-
      These are ElevenLabs' own figures, not audited, and there's no rule in India yet that forces a company to tell you when you're talking to AI. The 22-language target is a plan, not something it has shipped.
    forYou: >-
      If a support call feels oddly smooth, ask whether you're speaking to a person; a decent system should tell you and hand you over. For anything serious, like a bank dispute or a refund, push for a human and get a ticket number either way.
