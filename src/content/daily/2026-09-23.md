    heading: "Force-closing your apps to save battery does the opposite"
    body: >-
      The myth: apps sitting in your recents tray are 'running' and draining your battery. The truth: on both iOS and Android they're frozen, not active. Swipe one away and the phone has to cold-start it next time — which uses MORE battery and CPU than resuming a suspended app. 🔋 Do this instead: leave them be, and only force-quit an app that's genuinely frozen or misbehaving.
    problem: >-
      A persistent habit says you should regularly swipe away all your background apps to save battery and speed up your phone.
    breakthrough: >-
      Both iOS and Android suspend apps you switch away from — they sit frozen in memory, using little to no power. Force-quitting removes them entirely, so the next launch is a full cold start that consumes more battery and CPU than resuming would have.
    catch: >-
      There's a real exception: an app that's actually frozen, crashing, or misbehaving is worth force-quitting to reset it.
    forYou: >-
      Stop mass-swiping your recents tray. Let the system manage memory, and only close an app when it's clearly broken.
