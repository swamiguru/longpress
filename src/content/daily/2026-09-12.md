    body: >-
      WhatsApp vs Telegram vs Signal, the honest privacy verdict. WhatsApp is end-to-end encrypted by default, but Meta still collects metadata (who you talk to, and when). Telegram's regular cloud chats are NOT end-to-end encrypted, only its opt-in 'Secret Chats' are. Signal is E2EE by default and collects almost no metadata. 🛡️ Verdict: for convenience, WhatsApp; for maximum privacy, Signal; Telegram shines for big communities, not secrecy.
    problem: >-
      'Encrypted' gets slapped on almost every messaging app, so most people assume all three protect them equally, when what's actually encrypted, and what data the company still keeps, differs a lot.
    breakthrough: >-
      Line them up on privacy and the picture clears: WhatsApp uses strong end-to-end encryption by default (via the Signal protocol) but Meta retains metadata about your contacts and timing. Telegram's default cloud chats are encrypted in transit and on its servers, not end-to-end, only 'Secret Chats' are E2EE and they're one-to-one and opt-in. Signal is E2EE by default and is built to hold almost no metadata about who you talk to.
    catch: >-
      Metadata isn't the message, but who-you-talk-to-and-when can be revealing on its own. And the 'most private' app only helps if the people you message are actually on it, so real-world privacy often comes down to your contacts, not just the tech.
    forYou: >-
      Match the app to the need: use Signal for anything genuinely sensitive, keep WhatsApp for everyday reach, and if you're on Telegram, remember normal chats aren't E2EE, switch to a Secret Chat when it matters.

    heading: "Lock your SIM in 60 seconds"
    body: >-
      A 60-second security win almost nobody bothers with: set a SIM PIN. If your phone or SIM is stolen, a thief can pop that SIM into another device, catch your bank and email OTPs, and reset your accounts. A PIN-locked SIM is useless to them. 📱 iPhone: Settings > Cellular > SIM PIN. 🤖 Android: Settings > Security > SIM card lock. Set it now, and change the default PIN.
    problem: >-
      Most phone security stops at the lock screen, but your SIM is a separate key: moved into another phone it keeps receiving your SMS OTPs, which is exactly what attackers need to take over bank and email accounts.
    breakthrough: >-
      A SIM PIN locks the SIM itself, so it can't be used in any device until the PIN is entered, even if it's physically removed from your phone. It's a built-in setting: iPhone under Settings > Cellular > SIM PIN, Android under Settings > Security > SIM card lock, and it takes under a minute to turn on.
    catch: >-
      You must change the carrier's default PIN (often 0000 or 1234) or it offers little protection, and entering it wrong too many times locks the SIM until you get a PUK code from your carrier, so save the PIN somewhere safe. It guards the SIM, not app-based OTPs, so keep using an authenticator app where you can.
    forYou: >-
      Set a SIM PIN today on every phone in your household, pick a non-default code, and store it in your password manager so a stolen phone can't be used to hijack your number.
