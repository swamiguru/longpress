    heading: "What Stops Working In January"
    body: >-
      India's telecom department wants messaging apps that identify users by phone number to keep checking that the registered SIM is active, reportedly every 24 hours, by a December 31 compliance deadline ahead of a January 2027 rollout. The problem is how that check actually works. Two of the three verification methods need live cellular network access, and the one that doesn't, an on-device check, still can't confirm a SIM is genuinely active rather than just present. That breaks WhatsApp or Telegram on home WiFi with no signal, access while travelling abroad, shared business numbers, dual-SIM fallback setups, and any WhatsApp API or CRM integration running without a handset at all. None of it, notably, stops someone misusing a SIM that's stolen but still working.
    image: "/images/daily/2026-11-18/illustration_2.png"
    problem: >-
      DoT's Telecom Cyber Security Rules require messaging apps to verify a user's registered SIM, originally without defining how often, leaving telcos and app makers guessing at compliance.
    breakthrough: >-
      A DoT note reportedly clarifies the check must happen every 24 hours, with a December 31, 2026 compliance deadline and January 2027 implementation. Experts outlined three verification methods: an OS check, an operator lookup, or an SMS ping, each with different network and cost requirements.
    catch: >-
      Only the OS-based check works without a cellular signal, and even it can't confirm a SIM is genuinely active. The other two need network access and are billable. None of the three can tell a legitimate traveller apart from someone misusing a stolen but active SIM.
    forYou: >-
      If you rely on WhatsApp or Telegram over WiFi without a working SIM in the phone, including while travelling, budget for disruption from January unless your app's implementation turns out gentler than the rule reads.
    question: "What is DoT's new SIM-binding rule and when does it start?"
    answer: >-
      India's Department of Telecommunications is requiring messaging apps that use phone numbers to identify users to verify a registered SIM is active, reportedly every 24 hours. The compliance deadline is December 31, 2026, with implementation expected from January 2027.
    qa:

      - q: "Does the SIM-binding rule affect Indians travelling abroad?"
        a: "Yes. Access to WhatsApp or Telegram on an Indian-number account while travelling internationally, including for students studying abroad, is one of the use cases experts flagged as at risk under continuous SIM-verification requirements."
      - q: "Does SIM binding actually stop phone and messaging fraud?"
        a: "Experts quoted by MediaNama are skeptical. The rule can't distinguish a genuine traveller from someone misusing another person's active SIM, and it does nothing about fraud carried out on a SIM that is still live and working."
