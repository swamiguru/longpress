  - slot: 4
    kind: security
    heading: "Lock your SIM before someone else uses it"
    body: >-
      Here's a gap most people never close: your SIM has no lock. If your phone is stolen, a thief can eject the SIM, drop it into any other phone, and start receiving your OTPs and UPI/bank codes. The one-minute fix is a SIM PIN — on iPhone it's Settings → Cellular → SIM PIN, on Android it's Settings → Security → SIM lock. Once set, the SIM is dead weight without your code. 🔒
    problem: >-
      Phone lock screens protect the device, but the SIM card itself is usually unprotected — and OTPs and UPI codes are delivered to the SIM, not the phone.
    breakthrough: >-
      Enabling a SIM PIN locks the SIM to a code you set, so it can't be moved into another handset to receive your messages and one-time passwords. It's built into both iOS (Settings → Cellular → SIM PIN) and Android (Settings → Security → SIM lock) and takes about a minute.
    catch: >-
      You'll need to enter the PIN whenever the phone restarts, and too many wrong attempts locks the SIM until you enter the carrier's PUK code — so set a PIN you'll remember and avoid obvious ones like 0000.
    forYou: >-
      Turn on a SIM PIN today so a stolen or lost phone can't be used to hijack your OTP- and UPI-based accounts.

    problem: >-

    breakthrough: >-
      A short 'red-team' prompt reframes the AI as your skeptic: ask it to argue the strongest case against your decision, name the three most likely failure modes, and state the one fact that should change your mind. It works in ChatGPT, Gemini, or Claude and surfaces blind spots your gut is glossing over.
    catch: >-
      The AI's counter-arguments are only as good as the context you give it and can still be confidently wrong, so treat the output as a checklist to investigate, not a verdict.
    forYou: >-
      Save the prompt and run your next big decision — a job switch, a big purchase, a launch — through it before you commit.
