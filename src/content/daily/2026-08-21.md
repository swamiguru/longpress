    kind: myth-buster
    heading: "More WiFi bars does not mean faster internet"
    body: >-
      Full WiFi bars only show the signal strength between your device and the router — not your actual internet speed. You can sit at four bars and still buffer because of your plan, network congestion, or being stuck on the slower 2.4GHz band. The fix is to switch to the 5GHz or 6GHz band and run a real speed test before blaming your ISP. 📶
    problem: >-
      People judge their internet by the WiFi bars icon and get confused when 'full bars' still buffers — then blame the wrong thing, usually their ISP.
    breakthrough: >-
      Bars measure signal strength (device-to-router distance and interference), not throughput. Real speed depends on your broadband plan, network congestion, and which band you're on — 2.4GHz reaches further but is slower and more crowded than 5GHz/6GHz.
    catch: >-
      Switching bands helps only up to your plan's ceiling — no band beats the speed you actually pay for. And 6GHz needs a WiFi 6E/7 router plus a compatible device, so older hardware won't see it.
    forYou: >-
      Next time it's slow, join the 5GHz/6GHz network, move closer to the router, and run a speed test. If the number is low everywhere, it's your plan or ISP — not the bars.
