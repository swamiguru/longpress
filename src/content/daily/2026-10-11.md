    body: >-
      YouTube has a built-in sleep timer that most people never find. Play a video, tap the gear icon in the player, choose Sleep timer and pick 10, 15, 20, 30, 45 or 60 minutes, or end of video, as [Smartprix](https://www.smartprix.com/bytes/how-to-use-youtube-sleep-timer/) walks through. The video pauses when the countdown ends, and you don't need Premium.
    image: "/images/daily/2026-10-11/illustration_3.png"
    problem: >-
      Falling asleep to a podcast or a long video means waking up to a hot phone, a drained battery and half your data pack gone on autoplay.
    breakthrough: >-
      The sleep timer sits inside the player's settings gear on the YouTube app. Options run from 10 to 60 minutes, or until the current video ends, and a countdown shows in the app and in your notifications. It started as a Premium perk and is now open to everyone, per [Smartprix](https://www.smartprix.com/bytes/how-to-use-youtube-sleep-timer/).
    catch: >-
      It pauses playback, it doesn't lock your phone or stop other apps, and if you don't see the option yet, updating the YouTube app usually fixes it.
    forYou: >-

  - slot: 4
    kind: hot-take
    heading: "Ten minutes of AI answers made people give up sooner"
    body: >-
      A set of experiments with 1,222 people found that ten minutes of solving maths with GPT-4 left people worse off once the AI was taken away: they solved fewer problems and skipped more of them, according to the paper on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.04721v1). The people who used the AI for hints showed no real drop. The tool isn't the problem here. Asking it for the answer every time is.
    image: "/images/daily/2026-10-11/illustration_4.png"
    problem: >-
      Everyone assumes AI makes us more productive, and in the moment it does. What nobody measures day to day is what happens to your own ability when the AI isn't there, in an exam, an interview, or a meeting where you can't open a chat window.
    breakthrough: >-
      In the first experiment, people who used GPT-4 on fraction problems for about ten minutes and then worked alone solved 57% of the next problems, against 73% for a group that never had AI, and skipped nearly twice as many (20% vs 11%). A reading-comprehension version showed the same pattern, per the summary on [alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2604.04721v1). Around 61% of the AI group mostly asked for direct answers and took the biggest hit, while the roughly 27% who asked for hints or clarifications showed no significant impairment.
    catch: >-
      These were short online tasks, fractions and SAT-style reading, not months of real work, and the paper measures a short-term dip rather than permanent damage. Still, if ten minutes moves the needle this much, I'd be wary of what a year of copy-paste does.
    forYou: >-
      Use AI the way you'd use a good tutor: ask what you're missing, not what the answer is. Slot 5 today has a prompt that does exactly that.

    heading: "A ChatGPT prompt that gives hints instead of answers"
    body: >-
      If you use ChatGPT to learn anything, paste this before your question: "Don't give me the answer. Ask what I've already tried, then give me one small hint at a time. Show the full solution only when I type 'show me', and tell me where I went wrong." In the 1,222-person study on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.04721v1), people who used AI for hints kept their skills while answer-seekers slipped. This prompt forces you into the first group.
    image: "/images/daily/2026-10-11/illustration_5.png"
    problem: >-
      Chatbots are built to be helpful, which in practice means they hand you the full answer instantly. That's great for getting unstuck and bad for actually learning the thing.
    breakthrough: >-
      The prompt flips the default: the AI asks what you've tried, drips out the smallest useful hint, and only reveals the solution on request. It lines up with the study's finding, summarised on [alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2604.04721v1), that hint-seekers showed no significant drop when the AI was removed, while answer-seekers solved 57% vs 73% for a no-AI group.
    catch: >-
      It's slower, and some days you just need the answer. Most chatbots also drift back to their helpful defaults in long chats, so repeat the instruction if it starts solving things for you.
    forYou: >-
      Save it in your notes or set it as a custom instruction for study sessions. It works for maths, code, Excel formulas and exam prep.
