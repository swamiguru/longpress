    heading: "AI is paying Big Tech. Everyone else is still waiting."
    body: >-
      Apollo chief economist Torsten Slok said in an October 2026 note that AI has delivered "zero financial payoff to corporate America outside of tech", pointing to flat revenue per worker and flat margins across the S&P 500 once you strip out its seven tech giants, as [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/25/ai-delivers-zero-financial-payoff-to-most-companies-according-to-apollo-economist/) reported. I think he's right about where things stand, and it isn't much of a mystery. Most companies bought licences, ran a few pilots and left the actual work exactly as it was. That's a software bill, not a productivity plan.
    image: "/images/daily/2026-10-26/illustration_1.png"
    problem: >-
      Companies have spent two years being told AI will make every worker more productive. The money flowing to Nvidia, Microsoft and the cloud giants is easy to see; the return for the companies paying them is much harder to find.
    breakthrough: >-
      In a note published by [Apollo](https://www.apollo.com/wealth/insights-news/insights/daily-spark/no-signs-of-ai-payoff-outside-the-tech-sector), Torsten Slok looked at the S&P 493, the index without Alphabet, Amazon, Apple, Meta, Microsoft, Nvidia and Tesla. Revenue per worker and profit margins were flat, and he called AI "a great calculator" in a world where business intelligence is mostly judgment.
    catch: >-
      Flat averages can hide companies that are getting real gains, and productivity data usually lags a new technology by years. This is one economist reading aggregate numbers, not a company-by-company audit.
    forYou: >-
      If your team's AI rollout is a chatbot licence and a training video, expect the same flat line. Pick one process, redesign it around the tool, and measure the hours it saves before buying more seats.
    question: "Has AI improved profits for companies outside Big Tech in 2026?"
    answer: >-
      Not yet, according to Apollo chief economist Torsten Slok. In an October 2026 note he found flat revenue per worker and flat profit margins across the S&P 500 minus Alphabet, Amazon, Apple, Meta, Microsoft, Nvidia and Tesla, and said AI's financial payoff outside tech has been zero.
    qa:
      - q: "What is the S&P 493 in Apollo's AI payoff analysis?"
        a: "It's the S&P 500 with seven tech giants taken out: Alphabet, Amazon, Apple, Meta, Microsoft, Nvidia and Tesla. Torsten Slok used that group to check whether AI is helping ordinary companies. In his October 2026 note, its revenue per worker and profit margins were both flat, with no visible AI productivity boost."
      - q: "Why does Apollo's Torsten Slok think AI isn't paying off for companies?"
        a: "Slok calls AI a great calculator but argues that intelligence in business is mostly judgment. He says AI doesn't understand accountability, trust, culture, unwritten rules or fairness, so he doubts companies outside tech can turn cheaper intelligence into faster revenue growth and better margins. TechCrunch reported his note on October 25, 2026."

    heading: "Indian IT is hiring again, mostly freshers and mostly for AI"
    body: >-
      India's five biggest IT services firms added 16,055 employees net from April to September 2026, up from 3,097 a year earlier, with TCS alone adding 13,537 and Wipro cutting 5,828, according to [Angel One](https://www.angelone.in/news/stocks/indian-it-stocks-in-focus-top-five-firms-add-16-055-employees-in-h1fy27-but-demand-recovery-remains-uncertain). It reads like a recovery and it isn't one yet. Freshers were 60 to 65 percent of the net adds, cheap to hire and quick to retrain, and the only demand that's actually growing is AI, cloud and security work. The jobs are coming back with a different job description.
    image: "/images/daily/2026-10-26/illustration_2.png"
    problem: >-
      Indian IT spent two years barely adding people while clients paused spending and AI started eating the routine coding and testing work that used to fill junior benches. Freshers and mid-level engineers have been left guessing whether the hiring will ever return.
    breakthrough: >-
      In H1 FY27, TCS added 13,537, Infosys 3,948, HCLTech 3,158 and Tech Mahindra 1,240, while Wipro shrank by 5,828. Infosys alone added 4,480 in the September quarter to reach 332,542 people and still plans 20,000 fresher hires this year, per [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/business/companies/infosys-hires-4480-employees-in-q2fy27-4193101).
    catch: >-
      Analysts don't call it a demand recovery. Overall IT hiring fell 3 percent while AI and cloud roles grew about 16 percent, and the full-year total may stay under 30,000 to 40,000.
    forYou: >-
      If you're job hunting in IT, the door is open wider for freshers than for generalists with five years of maintenance work. Put AI, cloud or security work on your CV, even a small project, before the next campus cycle.
    question: "How many employees did TCS, Infosys, Wipro and HCLTech add in H1 FY27?"
    answer: >-
      India's top five IT firms added 16,055 people net between April and September 2026, against 3,097 a year earlier. TCS added 13,537, Infosys 3,948, HCLTech 3,158 and Tech Mahindra 1,240, while Wipro cut 5,828. Freshers made up 60 to 65 percent of the net additions.
    qa:

Overall IT hiring fell 3 percent while AI and cloud roles grew."
      - q: "How many freshers will TCS and Infosys hire in FY27?"
        a: "TCS expects to hire 25,000 freshers this year. Infosys hired 8,000 in the first half and expects 20,000 for the full year, after adding 4,480 net employees in the September quarter to reach 332,542. Wipro has hired about 1,000 engineering graduates so far and plans to step up campus hiring."
      - q: "Which IT skills are in demand in India right now?"
        a: "AI, cloud, cybersecurity and specialised engineering. Demand for those skills grew about 16 percent year on year in the first half of FY27, according to figures reported by Angel One, while overall IT hiring fell 3 percent. That split matters more than the headline headcount number for anyone planning a career move."

    forYou: >-
      Use incognito for one-off sensitive questions you don't want shaping future answers, and keep anything you can't share with Anthropic out of every chat, incognito or not.
    question: "How do I start an incognito chat in Claude that isn't saved?"
    answer: >-

        a: "No. The ghost icon appears only outside a project, so you have to start a fresh chat from the main screen. Close the incognito chat when you're done and Claude goes back to saving conversations to your history and memory as usual. The feature is available on Free, Pro, Max, Team and Enterprise."

    heading: "Your bank is not your scam filter"
    body: >-
      Goa police on October 25 asked banks to hold senior citizens' large transfers to unfamiliar accounts until they're verified, after about Rs 100 crore was lost to cyber fraud in the state this year, as [Daijiworld](https://daijiworld.com/news/newsDisplay?newsID=1333416) reported. That police have to ask tells you the check isn't happening by default. And sometimes the bank side is part of the problem: in Delhi a CSB Bank manager is among six arrested over 37 mule accounts. The safety net that works is a family rule, not a bank's fraud desk.
    image: "/images/daily/2026-10-26/illustration_4.png"
    problem: >-
      A lot of people, especially older account holders, assume a bank would stop or question a sudden transfer of their life savings to a stranger. Digital arrest and fake investment scams depend on that assumption.
    breakthrough: >-
      Goa's cybercrime police asked banks to hold seniors' big transfers to unfamiliar accounts, flag sudden large deposits and reply to police faster. The same week, Delhi Police arrested six people including a CSB Bank manager over 37 mule accounts tied to 99 complaints and Rs 7.36 crore, per [Delhi News Live](https://www.delhinewslive.com/delhi/7-36-crore-cyber-fraud-delhi-police-arrest-bank-manager-and-five-others-trace-37-mule-accounts/).
    catch: >-
      Banks do run fraud checks, and most staff aren't involved in anything. But a request from police isn't a rule, and any hold has to work within RBI norms and the customer's right to their own money.
    forYou: >-
      Agree one rule at home: nobody moves a large amount to a new account without calling a family member first. If money is going to several accounts, it's fraud. If it's already gone, call 1930 straight away.
    question: "Will my bank stop a digital arrest scam transfer in India?"
    answer: >-
      Don't count on it. On October 25, 2026, Goa police had to ask banks to hold senior citizens' large transfers to unfamiliar accounts, after about Rs 100 crore was lost to cyber fraud in Goa this year. In Delhi, a CSB Bank manager is among six arrested over 37 mule accounts.
    qa:
      - q: "What did Goa police ask banks to do about cyber fraud?"
        a: "At a meeting chaired by IGP Keshav Ram Chaurasia, Goa's cybercrime police asked banks to hold large transfers by senior citizens to unfamiliar accounts until verified, flag sudden big deposits, never share customer data, and answer police requests quickly. Police say Goa has lost about Rs 100 crore to cyber fraud in 2026, against Rs 101 crore last year."
      - q: "What happened in the Delhi CSB Bank mule account case?"
        a: "Delhi Police's North-West district team arrested six people, including a bank manager, over 37 mule accounts linked to CSB Bank's Saraswati Vihar and Tri Nagar branches. The accounts are tied to 99 cybercrime complaints across India and about Rs 7.36 crore. Police seized 63 Aadhaar cards and 60 PAN cards."
      - q: "How can I tell if a money transfer request is a scam?"
        a: "Goa police offered a simple test: if you're being told to send money to several accounts, it's fraud, so stop. Real police and agencies never collect money over a video call. If you've already paid, report it on 1930 or cybercrime.gov.in as fast as you can, since early reports give the best chance of freezing funds."

    body: >-
      Sweden's Government Offices block public ChatGPT and Claude on staff computers from Monday, October 26, and point civil servants to Microsoft Copilot, the one tool they approved after a security review, as the [Daily Northern](https://www.dailynorthern.com/36084/swedish-government-offices-block-chatgpt-and-claude/) reported from Swedish radio. I think it's the right call, and it says nothing about which model is smarter. Copilot sits inside a Microsoft contract and security setup the government already audits. For AI startups chasing public-sector deals, that's the real competition: paperwork, not benchmarks.
    image: "/images/daily/2026-10-26/illustration_5.png"
    problem: >-
      Civil servants handle sensitive material, and public chatbots make it easy to paste it into a service the employer has never vetted. Governments have struggled to say yes to AI without losing track of where that data goes.
    breakthrough: >-
      From October 26, Sweden's Government Offices block the public ChatGPT and Claude services on work computers and direct staff to Copilot, which they reviewed and hardened, according to [Yahoo News](https://www.yahoo.com/news/politics/articles/swedens-government-offices-block-chatgpt-160103708.html). Some AI systems stay available through controlled internal services, and Sweden's Tax Agency had already blocked AI services.
    catch: >-
      No detailed official reason has been published, and concentrating a whole government on one vendor's assistant carries its own risk if that vendor slips.
    forYou: >-
      If your company hasn't approved an AI tool, assume the one you're using privately is the one that gets banned first. Ask IT which tool is approved before pasting anything confidential.
    question: "Why did Sweden's government block ChatGPT and Claude on work computers?"
    answer: >-
      From October 26, 2026, Sweden's Government Offices block public ChatGPT and Claude on staff computers and direct civil servants to Microsoft Copilot, the tool that passed their security review. No detailed official reason was published, but the approval process, not model quality, decided it.
    qa:
      - q: "Which AI tool can Swedish government staff still use?"
        a: "Microsoft Copilot. Swedish media reported that the Government Offices reviewed Copilot, tightened its security and approved it, and staff are being directed to it instead of ChatGPT and Claude. Some other AI systems remain available through controlled internal services rather than the public websites."
      - q: "Have other Swedish agencies blocked AI chatbots?"
        a: "Yes. Before the Government Offices' block took effect on October 26, 2026, the Swedish Tax Agency and the National Board of Institutional Care had already blocked AI services, and the Enforcement Authority was reported to be considering the same step, according to Daily Northern's summary of Swedish public broadcaster reporting."
