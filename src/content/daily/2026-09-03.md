    body: >-
      India may become the first country where AI agents pay from your UPI automatically. NPCI's new Unified Agent Protocol — set to debut at the Global Fintech Fest in Mumbai — would let a registered AI agent spend on your behalf under rules you set: spending caps, audit trails and identity checks, built on the existing UPI Circle (delegated payments) and Reserve Pay mechanisms. The convenience is real; so is the need for airtight guardrails before an app can move your money.
    problem: >-
      Paying manually for every small, repetitive transaction is friction, and the first wave of 'agentic' AI assistants can't actually complete a purchase without a human tapping approve each time — which defeats the point of automation.
    breakthrough: >-
      India's payments operator NPCI is building a Unified Agent Protocol to register, verify and authorise AI agents on UPI, extending the UPI Circle delegated-payment model into agentic commerce. It's expected to be unveiled next week at the Global Fintech Fest in Mumbai, letting agents auto-pay under preset rule-based limits with built-in audit trails and identity checks.
    catch: >-
      It still needs RBI sign-off before launch, and today's delegation limits are modest (full delegation runs up to about Rs 15,000/month and Rs 5,000 per transaction) — caps that may be revisited for agentic use. Handing spend authority to software raises an obvious 'who approved that?' question, so the guardrails matter more than the convenience.
    forYou: >-
      If you use UPI, watch for the Global Fintech Fest announcement and treat agent permissions like you'd treat a standing instruction: set the tightest limits you can, keep audit trails on, and don't switch it on until you understand exactly what the agent can and can't spend.

    body: >-
      One iPhone trick most people never touch: you can pull text straight off paper into any text field without a photo or an OCR app. Tap into a text box (Notes, Messages, an email, a form), then tap the Scan Text camera icon — or long-press and choose Scan Text — point your camera at the words, and hit Insert. Great for Wi-Fi passwords, receipts, serial numbers and book quotes.
    problem: >-
      Copying text off a physical page — a Wi-Fi password on a router, a code on a receipt, a quote from a book — usually means squinting and retyping, or installing a clunky third-party scanner app.
    breakthrough: >-
      iOS has Live Text built into the keyboard: tap any text field, tap the Scan Text (camera) icon or long-press and choose Scan Text, point at the words and tap Insert. It drops recognised text straight into the field — no photo, no separate OCR app.
    catch: >-
      It needs a reasonably recent iPhone on iOS 15.4 or later and clear, well-lit text; messy handwriting or tiny low-contrast print can still trip it up.
    forYou: >-
      Next time you'd normally retype something off paper or a screen, tap the text field and look for the Scan Text icon — it turns a 30-second retype into a one-tap insert.

    kind: myth-buster
    heading: "Delete that cleaner app"
    body: >-
      The myth that won't die: 'cleaner' and RAM-booster apps make your phone faster. In reality, modern Android and iOS already manage memory for you, and force-killing cached apps just makes them cold-reload — which means more lag and more battery drain, not less. Most of these apps are also stuffed with ads and ask for alarming permissions, and iOS won't even allow a genuine RAM cleaner, so that satisfying 'freeing up memory' animation is theatre. The best booster is uninstalling the booster; free up space the real way by clearing big videos and unused apps.
    problem: >-
      Phones slow down over time, and app stores are full of 'cleaner', 'booster' and 'speed up' apps promising a one-tap fix — so people install them believing they help.
    breakthrough: >-
      Modern Android and iOS manage RAM automatically: keeping recently used apps cached in memory is a feature, because relaunching from cache is far faster than a cold start. Killing those cached apps forces slower cold reloads that use more CPU and battery — the opposite of a speed-up.
    catch: >-
      Most cleaner apps deliver no real performance gain and come with ads and broad permissions; on iOS a true system 'RAM cleaner' isn't even possible, so the memory-freeing animation is purely cosmetic.
    forYou: >-
      Uninstall the cleaner apps. To genuinely reclaim speed and space, delete large videos and apps you don't use, clear an app's cache from Settings if it's misbehaving, and restart the phone occasionally — no third-party 'booster' required.
