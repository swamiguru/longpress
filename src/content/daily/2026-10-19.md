    heading: "Skip the GPay helpline search. The ticket is in the app"
    body: >-
      Google Pay already has a complaint path inside the app: Show transaction history, tap the payment, then Having Issues, Payment issue, Create a ticket, as [Google Pay Help](https://support.google.com/pay/india/answer/9780926) lays out. Most people skip it and search for a helpline instead, which is exactly where fake "GPay customer care" numbers live. The ticket is slower to find than a phone number, but it can't ask for your UPI PIN. For actual fraud, call your bank first, then the cybercrime police.
    image: "/images/daily/2026-10-19/illustration_3.png"
    problem: >-
      When a UPI payment fails or goes to the wrong place, the instinct is to search for a helpline, and scammers buy and seed fake customer care numbers for exactly that moment. One call can turn a Rs 500 problem into an emptied account.
    breakthrough: >-
      The [Google Pay Help](https://support.google.com/pay/india/answer/9780926) page lists the in-app route: open the transaction from your history, tap Having Issues, then Payment issue, Create a ticket, and attach the bank statement it asks for. Banks usually add a failed debit back within 3 business days, and you can raise a dispute if that doesn't happen.
    catch: >-
      The ticket handles stuck and failed payments well. It can't claw back money you sent to a scammer yourself, which is a bank and police job.
    forYou: >-
      Next time a payment misbehaves, open the transaction before you open a browser. Nobody genuine will ever need your UPI PIN to send you money back.
    question: "How do I complain about a failed payment in Google Pay?"
    answer: >-
      Open Google Pay, tap Show transaction history, pick the payment, then tap Having Issues, Payment issue and Create a ticket. Google's help page says banks usually reverse failed debits within 3 business days. For fraud, contact your bank first and report it to cybercrime police.
    qa:
      - q: "Does Google Pay have a customer care phone number?"
        a: "Google Pay's India help page sends you to in-app tickets and a report form, and tells you to call your bank, which runs 24x7 fraud support. It doesn't list a phone helpline for disputes, so a \"GPay customer care\" number from a search result or a stranger is a red flag, not a shortcut."
      - q: "What if my Google Pay complaint isn't resolved?"
        a: "Google Pay's help page says that if an issue stays unresolved for more than 30 days, you can write to the Google Pay nodal officer at nodal-gpay@google.com. Keep your ticket details and bank statement ready. For fraud, also report to your bank and the cybercrime police, since Google Pay can't reverse a bank debit itself."

    heading: "Fraudster used your UPI PIN? It isn't automatically your loss"
    body: >-
      A UPI fraud that used your PIN is not automatically your loss. In a ruling reported on October 17, a Punjab consumer commission upheld an order making a bank refund Rs 72,495 plus costs, rejecting the argument that PIN authentication alone proved the customer was careless, as [The420.in](https://the420.in/india-cyber-tech-brief-top-10-cybercrime-ai-security-18-october-2026/) reported. That's not a loophole. The 2017 [RBI circular](https://rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_CircularIndexDisplay.aspx?Id=11040) already says the bank has to prove you were at fault. The part people get wrong is the clock: three working days, starting from the bank's SMS.
    image: "/images/daily/2026-10-19/illustration_4.png"
    problem: >-
      Banks routinely tell UPI fraud victims that a correct PIN means the customer was negligent, case closed. Most people believe it, don't file in writing, and miss the window that actually decides who pays.
    breakthrough: >-
      The Punjab State Consumer Disputes Redressal Commission upheld relief for a Bathinda customer who lost Rs 72,495 across 11 UPI transactions in 2019: a full refund, Rs 5,000 compensation and Rs 5,000 costs, plus interest, [The420.in](https://the420.in/india-cyber-tech-brief-top-10-cybercrime-ai-security-18-october-2026/) reported on October 17. It relied on RBI's 2017 framework, which says the burden of proving customer liability lies with the bank.
    catch: >-
      This took seven years, and it isn't a blank cheque. If you handed the PIN or OTP to a scammer yourself, the [RBI circular](https://rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_CircularIndexDisplay.aspx?Id=11040) says you carry the loss until you report it, and only after that does it shift to the bank.
    forYou: >-
      The moment you see a debit you didn't make, report it to your bank in writing and keep the reference number, then call 1930. Do it within three working days, even if you suspect it was your own slip.
    question: "Will my bank refund a UPI fraud if my PIN was used?"
    answer: >-
      Possibly, yes. RBI's 2017 rules put the burden of proving customer liability on the bank, and a Punjab consumer commission ruling reported October 17, 2026 held that a PIN alone doesn't prove negligence. Report within three working days, though, because a delay raises your liability.
    qa:
      - q: "How fast do I need to report a UPI fraud to get a refund?"
        a: "Within three working days of the bank's SMS or email about the transaction. Under RBI's 2017 circular, a third-party breach reported in that window means zero liability. Reporting in four to seven working days caps your loss at Rs 5,000 to Rs 25,000 depending on the account. Later than that, the bank's policy decides."
      - q: "What if I shared my UPI PIN or OTP with the scammer myself?"
        a: "Then the rules are tougher on you. RBI's circular says that where the loss is due to the customer's negligence, such as sharing payment credentials, the customer bears it until they report the transaction to the bank. Anything debited after you report is the bank's problem. So report immediately even if you think it's your fault."
      - q: "How long does the bank have to credit my money back?"
        a: "RBI's 2017 circular says the bank must credit the disputed amount to your account within 10 working days of your report, as a shadow reversal, without waiting for insurance. It must settle the complaint within 90 days, and if it can't, it has to pay you the prescribed compensation."

    body: >-
      Anthropic says junior hires fell from 27% to 9% of its technical hiring since early 2025, in a report it published on October 16 about its own workforce ([Anthropic](https://anthropic.com/research/employment-at-anthropic)). Agent hours now outnumber human hours six to one. Credit where it's due, no other AI lab has published its own numbers this plainly. But if you're a fresher, read the fine print: they're hiring for judgment and autonomy, not for the stuff an agent already does.
    image: "/images/daily/2026-10-19/illustration_5.png"
    problem: >-
      Every AI company says its tools will create more jobs than they replace, and none of them show their own hiring data. Freshers in India are left guessing whether the entry-level tech job is shrinking or just changing.
    breakthrough: >-
      [Anthropic](https://anthropic.com/research/employment-at-anthropic)'s October 16 study says the junior share of its technical staff dropped from 21% to 14%, and junior technical hires from 27% to 9%. Agent hours first passed human hours in March 2026 and were six times employee hours by August.
    catch: >-
      It's one fast-growing company, and the authors say the junior finding is descriptive, not causal. Indian IT still hires at scale, with Infosys sticking to at least 20,000 freshers in FY27, per [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/corporate/infosys-plans-to-hire-20000-freshers-in-fy27-even-as-quarterly-headcount-dips).
    forYou: >-
      If you're starting out, build proof that you can run and check an agent's work: a project where you directed AI, caught its mistakes and shipped something. That's the line on a CV this report says hiring managers now want.
    question: "Is AI reducing junior tech hiring at AI companies like Anthropic?"
    answer: >-
      At Anthropic, yes. Its October 16, 2026 report says junior hires fell from 27% to 9% of technical hiring since early 2025, while agent work hours now outnumber human hours six to one. Anthropic calls the junior finding descriptive, not proof that AI caused it.
    qa:
      - q: "Does Anthropic's report mean entry-level IT jobs in India are disappearing?"
        a: "Not directly. Anthropic's report covers its own staff, and the authors say many of its practices can't be replicated across the economy. Indian IT is still hiring freshers; Infosys said in April it expects at least 20,000 in FY27. But it's a clear signal of where AI-heavy employers are taking the entry-level bar."
      - q: "What skills does Anthropic now look for when hiring?"
        a: "Anthropic's report says hiring managers increasingly prioritise autonomy and judgment over domain expertise, and that non-technical hiring grew in sales, finance and recruiting. In practice that means being able to direct and check AI agents' work, not just write code that an agent could produce on its own."
