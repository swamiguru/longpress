    body: >-
      If your Android phone keeps screaming "storage full," turn on Auto-Archive instead of deleting apps. Open the Play Store, tap your profile, then Settings > General > Automatically archive apps. Android shrinks the apps you barely open — freeing up most of their space — but keeps the icon on your home screen and all your personal data intact. Tap the icon once and it re-downloads, dropping you back exactly where you left off. No app, no root, completely free.
    problem: >-
      Budget and older Android phones constantly run out of space right when a big update or new app needs to install, and the usual fix — deleting apps — means losing your data and re-setting everything up later.
    breakthrough: >-
      Google Play's Auto-Archive (Play Store > profile > Settings > General > Automatically archive apps) removes the bulk of rarely-used apps while keeping the icon and your personal data, then restores everything with a single tap.
    catch: >-
      It only works for apps published as Android App Bundles, and archived apps need an internet connection to re-download before you can use them again.
    forYou: >-
      Switch it on once and your phone quietly clears space before it ever forces you to uninstall anything — ideal if you're always fighting a nearly-full storage bar.
