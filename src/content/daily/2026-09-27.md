    image: "/images/daily/2026-09-27/illustration_3.png"
    problem: >-
      Your own phone's mic can be great and the call still sounds awful, because the noise is on the other person's end and you can't fix their phone.
    breakthrough: >-
      Pixel's Clear Calling uses on-device processing to strip background noise from the incoming voice. It lives in Settings > Sound & vibration > Clear Calling and is supported on Pixel 7 and later.
    catch: >-
      It's Pixel-only and off by default on some builds. It cleans up noise, but a genuinely weak network will still sound bad.
    forYou: >-

    kind: myth-buster
    heading: "Wi-Fi off won't save battery"
    body: >-
      Switching off Wi-Fi and Bluetooth to save battery made sense years ago; it barely matters now. Idle radios draw very little, and with Wi-Fi off your phone falls back to mobile data, which costs more power, especially indoors on a weak signal. The screen, a bad network, apps pinging your location and heat are what drain it. Leave Wi-Fi on and audit location permissions instead.
    image: "/images/daily/2026-09-27/illustration_5.png"
    problem: >-
      People toggle Wi-Fi and Bluetooth off every night and still end the day at 15%, then blame the battery.
    breakthrough: >-
      Modern Wi-Fi and Bluetooth chips sit in low-power states when they aren't doing anything. Moving data over Wi-Fi generally costs less energy than over mobile data, and a weak cellular signal makes the modem crank up power to hold the connection. Wi-Fi scanning also helps your phone find its location without leaning on GPS as much.
    catch: >-
      If you're somewhere with no known networks at all, constant scanning can cost a little, and Bluetooth paired to a busy accessory does use power. It's just small next to the big drains.
    forYou: >-
      Check Battery settings for which apps are eating power, cut background location access to 'While using', drop auto-brightness a notch, and stop fiddling with the toggles.
