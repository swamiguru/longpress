    heading: "CERT-In Names the Bank Attackers"
    body: >-
      CERT-In has a name now for the AI-driven attacks hitting Indian banks and fintechs: CRAFTY FALCON. The campaign uses tools like ARTEX, PentAGI and CyberStrikeAI, originally built for legitimate security testing, to hunt for weak payment APIs and misconfigured servers. The pattern repeats: a Re 1 test transfer to see if it slips through, then real money moving through mule accounts before anyone notices. It even times its biggest moves for weekends and holidays, when monitoring thins out. None of this is conceptually new. What's new is the speed. Turn transaction alerts on, and if you see a tiny test debit you didn't make, don't wait to report it.
    image: "/images/daily/2026-11-05/illustration_1.png"
    problem: >-
      Indian banks, NBFCs and fintechs are facing a wave of intrusions that move at machine speed, exploiting exposed payment APIs and automated fund-disbursement systems faster than manual monitoring usually catches.
    breakthrough: >-
      On November 4, 2026, CERT-In and the I4C named the campaign CRAFTY FALCON and issued critical-severity guidance, CIAD-2026-0046 and CIAD-2026-0049, describing how AI-assisted tools probe for vulnerabilities, run small test transactions, and route stolen funds through mule accounts via IMPS and NEFT before deleting records to cover their tracks, [The 420.in reported](https://the420.in/cert-in-warns-of-ai-driven-cyberattacks-targeting-indian-banks-and-fintech-firms/).
    catch: >-
      The advisory doesn't name every affected institution or put a number on total losses, and it notes these AI tools have legitimate security uses too, so not every intrusion was necessarily fully autonomous.
    forYou: >-
      Turn on transaction alerts, treat a tiny unexplained test payment as a warning sign rather than a glitch, and report any unauthorised debit to your bank and to 1930 immediately rather than waiting to see if more follow.
    question: "What is CRAFTY FALCON, the AI cyberattack CERT-In flagged on Indian banks?"
    answer: >-
      CRAFTY FALCON is the name CERT-In gave on November 4, 2026 to an ongoing AI-assisted campaign exploiting payment APIs at Indian banks, NBFCs, fintechs and crypto exchanges. Attackers test a weak API with a Re 1 transfer, then move real money through mule accounts via IMPS and NEFT.
    qa:
      - q: "What should banks do about the CRAFTY FALCON advisory?"
        a: "CERT-In's advisories CIAD-2026-0046 and CIAD-2026-0049 tell banks to set daily transfer limits, verify beneficiaries, flag unusually fast transactions, separate test environments from live systems, and run heightened monitoring on weekends and holidays, when the campaign strikes most often."
      - q: "What should a bank customer do if this affects them?"
        a: "Turn on transaction alerts so you see every debit immediately, and if you spot an unauthorised transfer, report it to your bank and to the national cybercrime helpline at 1930 right away, since mule-account withdrawals move fast."
      - q: "Is every bank fraud case linked to CRAFTY FALCON?"
        a: "No. CERT-In's advisory does not name every affected institution or give a total amount stolen, so recent banking fraud losses should not be attributed to this specific campaign without further evidence from investigators."

    heading: "Apple's 18+ Label Isn't Enforced"
    body: >-
      Apple is giving the App Store new 13+, 16+ and 18+ ratings, and starting November 16, developers of dating apps and romantic AI companions have to apply them. Good in theory. In practice, the age that decides who can download these apps is whatever birthdate someone typed in once, and India isn't one of the countries where Apple actually blocks an unconfirmed 18+ download. A 16-year-old with a fake birth year gets the same access as before. The label changed. The gate didn't.
    image: "/images/daily/2026-11-05/illustration_2.png"
    problem: >-
      Dating apps and AI companion apps with sexual or romantic content are reaching minors in India, and the App Store's age system has no real check behind it in this market.
    breakthrough: >-
      Apple told developers in early November 2026 that any app with dating or romantic relationship features, including AI companions like Character.AI and Replika, must carry an 18+ rating by November 16, building on its broader age-rating overhaul, [MediaNama reported](https://www.medianama.com/2026/11/223-seven-problems-apple-18-rating-dating-apps/).
    catch: >-
      The 18+ label only reflects a self-entered birthdate. Apple blocks unconfirmed 18+ downloads in Brazil, Australia and Singapore, but India isn't on that list, so the new rating changes what's listed, not who can actually download it here.
    forYou: >-
      If a child in your house has an iPhone, the App Store's new 18+ tag won't stop them on its own. Check Screen Time under Settings, set Content & Privacy Restrictions, and cap app ratings there instead of relying on Apple's label.
    question: "Does Apple verify age for its new 18+ dating app rating in India?"
    answer: >-
      No. Apple's new 18+ rating for dating and AI companion apps, which developers must apply by November 16, 2026, relies on a self-entered birthdate. India isn't among the countries, like Brazil, Australia and Singapore, where 18+ downloads are actually blocked until age is confirmed.
    qa:
      - q: "What apps need Apple's new 18+ rating?"
        a: "Dating apps, and AI companion apps such as Character.AI and Replika where romantic or sexual roleplay is one of the features, now need an 18+ rating under Apple's updated App Store policy announced in early November 2026."
      - q: "How does Apple decide who is old enough to download these apps?"
        a: "It checks the birthdate tied to a user's Apple ID, entered once at account setup. There's no government-ID check or ongoing verification in most markets, so a user who typed in a false birth year keeps that age permanently."
      - q: "Could India get the same download block as Brazil or Australia?"
        a: "Apple hasn't announced plans to extend the 18+ download block to India. Indian matrimonial sites have required ID verification since 2016, showing a working model already exists, but Apple's new rule doesn't draw on it."
