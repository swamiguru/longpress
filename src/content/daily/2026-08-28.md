    heading: "More megapixels doesn't mean better photos"
    body: >-
      Stop judging phone cameras by megapixels. A 200MP sensor can genuinely lose to a 48MP one. What actually decides photo quality is sensor size (more light), individual pixel size, image processing and computational photography, and lens quality. Most sky-high MP numbers just pixel-bin down to 12–16MP anyway, so the giant figure is mostly a spec-sheet flex.
    problem: >-
      Phone marketing leans hard on megapixel counts because a bigger number is easy to sell — and it nudges people to buy cameras by a single spec that doesn't reflect real-world photos.
    breakthrough: >-
      Image quality is driven by sensor size, pixel size, processing and lens — not raw megapixels. High-MP sensors typically 'pixel-bin,' merging many tiny pixels into fewer larger ones (often a 12–16MP final image) precisely because pixel size matters more than count.
    catch: >-
      Megapixels aren't useless — they help when you crop heavily or print large. The point is they're one factor among several, not the headline number to shop by.
    forYou: >-
      Next upgrade, ignore the MP race. Compare real sample photos, sensor size and processing (and read camera reviews) instead of the biggest number on the box.
