    body: >-
      Everyone knows Cmd + Tab to switch between apps on a Mac. Far fewer people use Cmd + backtick (the key just above Tab), which cycles between windows of the app you're already in. Three Finder windows, four Chrome windows or two Word documents open at once? Instead of hunting with the mouse, tap Cmd + backtick to flip through them, and add Shift to cycle backwards. It works system-wide across almost every Mac app.
    image: "/images/daily/2026-09-22/illustration_3.png"
    problem: >-
      Power users constantly juggle several windows of the same app, and Cmd + Tab only jumps between apps — not between those windows — so people fall back to slow mouse-hunting.
    breakthrough: >-
      Cmd + backtick (the key above Tab) cycles between the open windows of the current app, and Cmd + Shift + backtick cycles the other way. It's a built-in macOS shortcut that works across Finder, browsers, Office and most apps.
    catch: >-
      It only cycles windows within the currently active app, and a few apps that manage their own windows differently may not honour it.
    forYou: >-
      Next time you have multiple windows of one app open, tap Cmd + backtick instead of reaching for the mouse — it's an instant daily time-saver.

    heading: "Password manager vs your browser: where should your logins live?"
    body: >-
      After the Gemini story, the practical question is where your passwords should actually live. Saving them in your browser is convenient, but they sit right next to where malware operates, a badly-behaved extension can reach your autofill, and anyone with your unlocked laptop is often one click from seeing them. A dedicated password manager keeps everything in an encrypted vault, generates a unique password for every site and warns you the moment one leaks. The honest verdict: if you have a handful of logins and live entirely inside Apple or Google's ecosystem, the built-in manager (Apple Passwords or Google Password Manager) is genuinely enough — and free. If you have lots of logins or bounce across platforms, get a dedicated manager. The one thing nobody should do is reuse a single password everywhere — that's exactly the door the AI walked through.
    image: "/images/daily/2026-09-22/illustration_4.png"
    problem: >-
      Most people either reuse passwords or let their browser remember them, without knowing whether that's actually safe.
    breakthrough: >-
      Dedicated password managers use strong encryption, generate a unique password per site, sync across platforms and alert you to leaked or reused logins — protections browser autofill mostly lacks, since browser-saved passwords sit close to malware and can be exposed by a bad extension or physical access.
    catch: >-
      Password managers aren't flawless — researchers found vulnerabilities across major managers in early 2026 — and for a few logins inside one ecosystem, Apple's or Google's built-in manager is already enough, so a paid tool isn't mandatory.
    forYou: >-
      Few logins, all-in on Apple or Google? Use the built-in manager. Lots of logins or cross-platform? Get a dedicated one. Either way, never reuse the same password across sites.
