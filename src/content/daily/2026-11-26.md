It works with text, photos and videos in personal and group chats, and stays end-to-end encrypted. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/25/whatsapp-now-lets-you-schedule-messages-in-group-and-individual-chats/) notes that Telegram and Signal already offer scheduling.
    catch: >-

    forYou: >-
      Update the app and long-press send to check. Once it shows up, use it for early wishes, housing society reminders and the work ping you'd rather not send at 11 pm, and clear the queue if plans change.

    answer: >-

        a: "WhatsApp says the feature is rolling out globally over the coming weeks, so some accounts will get it later than others. Update to the latest version of the app, then press and hold the send button. If the option isn't there, it hasn't reached you."

        a: "Yes. TechCrunch reports that you can view your upcoming scheduled messages and delete them before they go out. The furthest you can schedule is 14 days ahead and the soonest is 10 minutes from now, so longer reminders still need another tool."

    body: >-
      As of 26 November 2026, Telegram's official tips show you can hold a message until the other person comes online: hold Send, open Schedule Message and choose Send When Online, provided you can see their Last Seen. With WhatsApp only now rolling out clock-based scheduling, I think this is the smarter idea. A time picker guesses when someone's free, whereas this one simply waits for them.
    image: "/images/daily/2026-11-26/illustration_3.png"
    problem: >-
      Scheduling by the clock assumes you know when the other person will look at their phone. With someone who's travelling or on night shifts, you usually don't.
    breakthrough: >-
      [Telegram Tips](https://t.me/TelegramTips/216) says you can schedule messages or media to be sent as soon as your recipient comes online, and they're delivered even if you're offline. A [companion tip](https://t.me/TelegramTips/217) puts it in the normal scheduling flow: hold Send, select Schedule Message, and the option is there if you can see their Last Seen.
    catch: >-
      If their Last Seen is hidden from you, the option doesn't show. [Telegram's FAQ](https://telegram.org/faq) says you also won't see it for people you don't share your own with, so a strict privacy setting costs you this.
    forYou: >-
      Try it on the next message that isn't urgent but should land while they're around, like a payment nudge to a friend or a file for a colleague abroad. Keep fixed-time scheduling for anything with a deadline.
    question: "How do I send a Telegram message when someone comes online?"
    answer: >-
      In Telegram, type your message, hold the Send button, choose Schedule Message and pick the option to send when the person is next online. It only appears if that person lets you see their Last Seen time, according to Telegram's official tips.
    qa:

        a: "Telegram says the option only appears if the other person allows you to see their Last Seen time, so their privacy settings decide it. Telegram's FAQ also says you won't see Last Seen timestamps for people you don't share your own with."
      - q: "Does a Telegram Send When Online message need me to stay online?"
        a: "No. Telegram's official tips say the message will be delivered even if you're offline. You set it once, close the app and get on with your day, and it goes out the next time the other person comes online. Nothing needs to stay open."
      - q: "Can I schedule a Telegram message for a fixed time instead?"
        a: "Yes. Hold the Send button in any chat and select Schedule Message to deliver text or media automatically at a later time. Use that when the clock matters, and Send When Online when what matters is that the person is actually around."
