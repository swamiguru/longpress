    heading: "Supreme Court wants banks to pay for digital arrest losses"
    body: >-
      The Supreme Court, hearing its own case on digital arrest scams, has asked RBI to examine making banks compensate victims at least the amount lost where inadequate due diligence let the fraud through, according to [The420](https://the420.in/legal/supreme-court-rbi-bank-liability-digital-arrest-victims/). The bench led by CJI Surya Kant also floated pausing unusual transactions and alerting the local police. Banks have spent years telling customers to be careful. This turns the instruction around and points it at the bank.
    problem: >-
      Victims of digital arrest and similar frauds are usually told the transfer was authorised, so the loss stays with them. The CJI cited an estimated net loss of about Rs 800 crore to the economy this year.
    breakthrough: >-
      The Court asked RBI to examine its proposed KYC Amendment Directions, 2026, expected to be notified by November 30 and operational in December, with institutions indemnifying victims where due diligence failed. It also floated suspending unusual transactions, auto-notifying the local police and physically verifying the transaction, per [The420](https://the420.in/legal/supreme-court-rbi-bank-liability-digital-arrest-victims/). RBI must submit a list of all authorised financial institutions at the next hearing.
    catch: >-
      Nothing is binding yet. The Attorney General argued that liability belongs in RBI regulation, not judicial directions, and the next hearing date wasn't reported.
    forYou: >-
      Until a rule exists, assume the bank will not cover you. Never move money for a caller claiming to be police or CBI, and if it happens, report it at once on 1930 or cybercrime.gov.in.
    question: "Will banks in India have to compensate digital arrest scam victims?"
    answer: >-
      Not yet, but the Supreme Court has asked RBI to consider it. A bench led by CJI Surya Kant proposed that banks compensate victims at least the amount lost where weak due diligence let fraud through. The Attorney General says RBI should handle liability through regulation instead.

      - q: "What other safeguards did the Supreme Court suggest for banks?"
        a: "The Court floated temporarily suspending unusual transactions, automatically notifying the local police station, and physically verifying whether a transaction is genuine. The CJI also cited an estimated net loss of about Rs 800 crore to the Indian economy from these frauds this year, according to The420's report of the hearing."

      The buyer waited from October 2021 to July 2026, and the commission didn't examine the E-Commerce Rules. NCH is a pre-litigation channel, so it forwards your complaint rather than guaranteeing a refund.

      Save 8800001915 now, and keep photos or a video of the unboxing plus every order and reference number. Use the helpline first, and go to the commission only if it stalls.

    body: >-

Free UPI was always paid for by somebody. Now the bill may move to the shop, and shops have a long habit of passing costs along to you.
    problem: >-
      UPI has been zero-cost for merchants, and every payments app has run on cashbacks, float and hope. A fee changes who pays and who profits.
    breakthrough: >-
      NPCI notified a 0.4% MDR on P2M UPI transactions above Rs 2,000, per [MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/10/223-upi-mdr-windfall-paytm/). On the Q2 FY27 call, Paytm's CFO Madhur Deora said a 2.5 basis point margin uplift on Rs 28 lakh crore of GMV works out to about Rs 700 crore a year, maybe Rs 900 crore by FY28.
    catch: >-
      Reports don't say who bears the charge, and the start date is unclear: one report ties it to after Diwali, another says NPCI delayed the rollout. Paytm itself calls the number an upside, not part of its model.
    forYou: >-
      Watch how shops handle bills above Rs 2,000 once this starts, since a cash discount or a UPI surcharge is the likely first sign. Small purchases are untouched.

    answer: >-

    heading: "China wants to sell AI access and keep the models"
    body: >-
      China's commerce ministry released a draft on October 16, 2026 that would add 23 AI technologies to its export restriction list, including models at 10^26 training compute, 2 trillion parameters, or a top-10 global ranking, per [Reuters](https://whbl.com/2026/10/28/analysis-chinas-new-export-curbs-draw-a-line-around-its-ai-crown-jewels/). The design is simple: overseas users keep using Chinese AI through websites and clouds, but the weights and know-how stay home. An analyst quoted by Reuters calls it a counterpart to US restrictions on China. The open question is whether labs can still run their best models on foreign-owned servers abroad.
    problem: >-
      Frontier AI is now treated like a strategic export, and the rules for who gets the model, not just who gets the chatbot, are being written fast.
    breakthrough: >-
      The draft lists 23 technologies, including advanced models, autonomous agents, chip-design technology and methods for coordinating more than 10,000 AI chips, per [Reuters](https://whbl.com/2026/10/28/analysis-chinas-new-export-curbs-draw-a-line-around-its-ai-crown-jewels/). A model is covered if it meets any one of three tests: 10^26 training compute, 2 trillion parameters, or a top-10 global ranking. Comments close November 15 and adoption is expected by the end of 2026.
    catch: >-
      It's a draft. The ranking test doesn't say which ranking counts, and law firm Han Kun questioned how size counts for models that activate only some components per task. China's commerce ministry did not comment.
    forYou: >-
      If you build on Chinese models, hosted APIs look like the route the draft protects. Self-hosting open weights is the part to watch, and the rules could still change before the comment window closes.
    question: "What do China's draft AI export restrictions cover?"
    answer: >-
      China's commerce ministry draft, released October 16, 2026, would add 23 AI technologies to its export restriction list, covering models with 10^26 training compute or 2 trillion parameters. Overseas users could still reach Chinese AI via websites and clouds, while some transfers or deployments abroad would need approval.
    qa:
      - q: "When will China's AI export rules take effect?"
        a: "Public comment on the draft closes November 15, 2026, and adoption is expected by the end of 2026, with implementation guidelines later. A temporary registration requirement for certain transfers that put Chinese technology under foreign ownership or control took effect on October 16, including deals already in progress."
      - q: "Will DeepSeek, Moonshot and Z.AI models still be available overseas?"
        a: "Per Reuters, overseas customers currently reach DeepSeek, Moonshot and Z.AI models through websites or cloud platforms, and the draft would generally keep that access. What's unclear is whether labs can train or run top models on foreign-owned overseas servers, and developers may need separate domestic and overseas versions."
