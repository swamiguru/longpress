      eSIM vs physical SIM — which should you actually use in 2026? Pick eSIM if you travel (buy a local data plan in seconds), want two numbers on one phone, or want theft protection — a thief can't yank out an eSIM to dodge tracking. ✈️ Stick with a physical SIM if you swap phones often, lend a SIM to a backup device, or don't want a carrier interaction every time you change handsets. Reality check: most 2026 flagships are going eSIM-first anyway, and Indian carriers now activate eSIMs in minutes. My verdict: eSIM for most people, physical if you're a frequent phone-swapper.
    problem: >-
      People treat SIM type as an afterthought, then get stuck — locked into a plan abroad, unable to quickly move their number to a spare phone, or surprised their new flagship has no SIM tray at all.
    breakthrough: >-
      eSIM has matured: instant remote activation, dual-number support on one device, and a real security edge (it can't be physically removed to defeat find-my-phone tracking). Physical SIMs still win on effortless phone-swapping and broad support on cheaper handsets. Many 2026 flagships are shipping eSIM-only.
    catch: >-
      eSIM support is still patchy on budget phones and with some smaller carriers, and moving an eSIM between devices can mean a re-activation step. Physical SIMs remain the more universal option for now.
    forYou: >-
      For most people — especially travellers — eSIM is the better default in 2026. If you regularly hop between phones or lend your SIM to a backup device, keep a physical SIM.
