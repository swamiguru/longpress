    heading: "From November 1, unknown calls in India show a KYC name"
    body: >-
      The Department of Telecommunications has told every Indian telecom operator to switch on caller name display, called CNAP, across the country from November 1, 2026, [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/govt-mandates-caller-name-display-from-november-1-heres-what-you-need-to-know-4183670) reports. Incoming calls will show the name on the caller's KYC records, free, and business callers have to show their GST or trademark name. It's the right fix, arriving about four years after TRAI first floated it in 2022, years we spent trusting a crowd-sourced app to tell us who was calling. It won't end scam calls, because plenty of fraud runs on SIMs with someone else's real name attached, but it makes 'I'm calling from your bank' a lot harder to say with a straight face.
    image: "/images/daily/2026-10-17/illustration_1.png"
    problem: >-
      Indians get a steady flood of calls from numbers they don't know, and the only way to guess who's on the line has been a third-party app built on whatever names other users happened to save. Scammers live in that gap.
    breakthrough: >-
      From November 1, 2026, the DoT wants every operator to show the KYC-verified name of the caller on incoming calls, pulled from dedicated operator databases, at no charge, per [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/govt-mandates-caller-name-display-from-november-1-heres-what-you-need-to-know-4183670). Telemarketers must show the name registered with GST or the Trade Marks Registry, international calls get an 'International' tag, and anyone who chose Calling Line Identification Restriction stays hidden.
    catch: >-
      A KYC name is only as honest as the KYC behind it. The order also doesn't spell out which phones or networks will show names on day one.
    forYou: >-
      From November, treat the name on screen as a hint, not proof. A call tagged International that claims to be your bank, or a name that doesn't match the company it says it is, is your cue to hang up and call back on the official number.
    question: "When will caller name display (CNAP) start in India?"
    answer: >-
      The Department of Telecommunications has ordered all Indian telecom operators to switch on caller name display, called CNAP, nationwide from November 1, 2026. Incoming calls will show the caller's KYC-registered name for free. People who opted for Calling Line Identification Restriction stay hidden.
    qa:
      - q: "Will CNAP caller name display replace Truecaller in India?"
        a: "Partly. CNAP shows the name tied to the SIM's KYC records, pulled from operator databases, so it is harder to fake than the crowd-sourced names in third-party apps. It doesn't promise spam scores or community reports, so a spam-blocking app still adds something for now."
      - q: "What name will business and telemarketing calls show under CNAP?"
        a: "Telemarketers must display the name registered with GST or the Trade Marks Registry, so a sales call should show the company rather than a random person's name. International calls get an 'International' tag, which helps because a call claiming to be your local bank shouldn't be coming from overseas."
      - q: "Does CNAP caller name display cost extra on Indian mobile networks?"
        a: "No. The DoT order says the name display is free for users. The Deccan Herald report doesn't say which handsets or network types will show names on day one, so don't be surprised if an older phone takes longer to start showing them after November 1, 2026."

    heading: "One ChatGPT setting stops your old chats picking your ads"
    body: >-
      If you use ChatGPT on the Free or Go plan, open Settings, then Ad Controls, and switch off Personalize ads, per [OpenAI's help page](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001047-ads-in-chatgpt). From then on, ads are picked only from the chat you're in, not your history. Switch off Past chats and memory while you're there, then tap Delete ads data. It takes two minutes, and that three-month-old conversation about your loan EMIs stops deciding what you get sold.
    image: "/images/daily/2026-10-17/illustration_3.png"
    problem: >-
      ChatGPT started showing ads to Free and Go users in India in August, per [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/technology/artificial-intelligence/story/chatgpt-ads-arrive-in-india-as-openai-targets-free-go-users-in-major-revenue-push-ahead-of-ipo-551655-2026-08-27), and with personalization on, your past chats and memory can feed which ads you see. Most people never open the setting.
    breakthrough: >-
      Settings, Ad Controls has three things worth touching: Personalize ads (off means only the current chat counts), Past chats and memory (off keeps your history out of ad selection), and Delete ads data, which [OpenAI](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001047-ads-in-chatgpt) says stops use immediately and clears its servers within 30 days.
    catch: >-
      None of this makes ChatGPT ad-free, and [Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/heres-how-chatgpt-is-targeting-you-with-ads-2000826457) notes the toggle may not block advertisers who match you through an uploaded list of emails or phone numbers. Only a paid plan like Plus drops ads entirely.
    forYou: >-
      If you're on Free or Go and you've ever talked money, health or work problems through with ChatGPT, change this today.
    question: "How do I turn off personalized ads in ChatGPT?"
    answer: >-
      Open ChatGPT Settings, go to Ad Controls and switch off Personalize ads. Ads will then use only the chat you're in. You can also switch off Past chats and memory, then tap Delete ads data. Ads show on the Free and Go plans, including in India since August 2026.
    qa:

      - q: "Does turning off personalized ads in ChatGPT stop all ad targeting?"
        a: "No. With Personalize ads off, ads still draw on the chat you're currently in. Gizmodo also notes the setting may not stop advertisers matching you through an uploaded list of emails or phone numbers. What it does cut out is your past chats, memory and ad history."
      - q: "How long does Delete ads data take to work in ChatGPT?"
        a: "OpenAI says deleted ads data stops being used for ads right away, while full removal from its servers can take up to 30 days. It's a one-tap clean slate and worth doing once, straight after you switch Personalize ads off, so old activity doesn't keep shaping what you see."

    body: >-
      Advertisers can target ChatGPT users by uploading lists of emails, phone numbers and Android ad IDs, which OpenAI matches against accounts, [404 Media](https://www.404media.co/how-chatgpt-targets-ads-to-you-based-on-your-prompts-phone-number-and-email/) found by running its own test campaigns. [Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/heres-how-chatgpt-is-targeting-you-with-ads-2000826457) adds that a list needs at least 25,000 entries. My problem isn't ads in a free product. It's that people tell ChatGPT things they would never type into a search bar, and now the same customer-list machinery that made social media ads feel creepy sits right next to those conversations. OpenAI pitched a different kind of ad business, and this looks like the old one with a nicer chat window.
    image: "/images/daily/2026-10-17/illustration_5.png"
    problem: >-
      ChatGPT has roughly a billion monthly users, and plenty of them treat it like a diary, a doctor and a finance advisor rolled into one. OpenAI's promise was that ads would stay politely apart from all that.
    breakthrough: >-
      By running two campaigns, [404 Media](https://www.404media.co/how-chatgpt-targets-ads-to-you-based-on-your-prompts-phone-number-and-email/) showed advertisers can reach users by email, phone number or Android advertising ID, matched by OpenAI to ChatGPT accounts, on top of targeting based on prompts. Per [Gizmodo](https://gizmodo.com/heres-how-chatgpt-is-targeting-you-with-ads-2000826457), audiences need at least 25,000 entries, the data can be raw or hashed, and OpenAI says uploaded files are deleted within 24 hours.
    catch: >-
      OpenAI says advertisers never see your chats or learn when a list entry matches, and being on a list doesn't guarantee you an ad. That's fair, and it's also what every ad platform says.
    forYou: >-
      On Free or Go, switch off Personalize ads in Settings, Ad Controls, knowing it may not stop list matching. If you use ChatGPT for anything sensitive, it's worth asking whether free is really the price.
    question: "Can advertisers target ChatGPT users by email or phone number?"
    answer: >-
      Yes. 404 Media reported on October 15, 2026 that advertisers can target ChatGPT users by uploading lists of email addresses, phone numbers or Android advertising IDs, which OpenAI matches to accounts. Ads can also draw on what you type. It's the old social-media ad playbook, now inside a chatbot.
