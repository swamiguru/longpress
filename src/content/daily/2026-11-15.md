    body: >-
      VIKALP has been running on Indian Railways since 2017, and most people booking a waitlisted ticket still never tick it. The option, officially the Alternate Train Accommodation Scheme, sits below the usual passenger and payment fields at booking, labelled 'Consider for Alternate Train Accommodation.' If your original ticket is still waitlisted close to departure, VIKALP shifts you to a confirmed seat on a different train on the same route and class, no extra payment required. It won't get you your original train, but it turns a likely cancelled trip into a confirmed one, which matters most exactly when you need it, during festival and holiday rushes when waitlists run longest.
    problem: >-
      Passengers booking during high-demand periods often end up stuck on a waitlist with no confirmed seat, and most don't know about a free railway setting that could get them a confirmed seat on a different train instead.
    breakthrough: >-
      IRCTC's VIKALP scheme, running since 2017 per [ixigo](https://www.ixigo.com/vikalp-scheme-for-waitlisted-passengers-10-things-you-need-to-know-story-1129586), auto-shifts a waitlisted passenger to a confirmed seat on an alternate train on the same route and class at no extra cost, if they opt in at booking.
    catch: >-
      You have no control over which alternate train you're shifted to, and you give up your original train and timing entirely once VIKALP reassigns you, so it suits people who need to arrive rather than people attached to a specific journey.
    forYou: >-
      Next time you book a ticket that might go on the waitlist, tick 'Consider for Alternate Train Accommodation' before paying. It costs nothing and only helps if your ticket stays unconfirmed.
    question: "What is IRCTC's VIKALP scheme and how does it help waitlisted passengers?"
    answer: >-
      VIKALP, short for the Alternate Train Accommodation Scheme, is a free opt-in setting at booking that shifts a still-waitlisted passenger to a confirmed seat on a different train on the same route and class, at no extra cost, instead of leaving them stuck on the waitlist.
    qa:
      - q: "How do I opt in to IRCTC's VIKALP scheme while booking a ticket?"
        a: "During booking, tick the option usually labelled 'Consider for Alternate Train Accommodation' below the regular passenger and payment fields. It's easy to miss, especially in a rushed Tatkal booking window, since it isn't a prominent or default setting."
      - q: "Does VIKALP guarantee I keep my original train and seat preference?"
        a: "No. VIKALP moves you to a different train on the same route and class if your original ticket stays waitlisted near departure. You lose your original train choice, but you gain a confirmed seat instead of an unconfirmed one, which is the actual trade-off it offers."
      - q: "Does VIKALP cost anything extra on top of the original ticket price?"
        a: "No. There's no additional payment required to opt in or to be shifted to the alternate train. It uses the same fare and class as your original booking; you're simply reassigned to a different train that has room, at no added cost."

    body: >-
      The CCPA fined Ola Rs 10 lakh and ordered it to kill the 'pay extra, get picked up faster' prompt, the one that surfaces right when a rider is anxious about their pickup. The regulator's own language was blunt: the prompt turns a gratuity into a pre-service toll, with no guaranteed faster ride attached. Rs 10 lakh, about $12,000, barely registers against the revenue a dark pattern like this likely generates at Ola's scale. A fine that small isn't a deterrent, it's a rounding error. Until the penalty costs more than the dark pattern earns, nothing about the underlying incentive changes, for Ola or for any other platform watching how cheaply this one got resolved.
    problem: >-
      Ride-hailing apps have used advance-tip prompts that imply a faster pickup in exchange for extra payment, without being able to show the tip actually changes how fast a driver arrives.
    breakthrough: >-
      The CCPA fined Ola Rs 10 lakh and ordered it to scrap the prompt, ruling it a dark pattern that turns a gratuity into what the regulator called a pre-service toll, per [Bar and Bench](https://www.barandbench.com/news/litigation/olas-extra-pay-for-faster-rides-feature-doesnt-work-ccpa-asks-it-to-scrap-practice-slaps-10-lakh-penalty).
    catch: >-
      Rs 10 lakh is a trivial sum next to what the prompt likely earned Ola in extra tips while it ran, so the fine removes the specific feature without meaningfully punishing the incentive that produced it.
    forYou: >-
      Treat any 'pay more for faster service' prompt in a ride-hailing app as unverified marketing, not a guarantee, and tip based on service received, not a promise made before the ride starts.
    question: "Why did the CCPA fine Ola Rs 10 lakh over its advance-tip prompt?"
    answer: >-
      The Central Consumer Protection Authority fined Ola Rs 10 lakh and ordered it to scrap its 'pay extra for a faster ride' prompt, ruling it a dark pattern. The CCPA found the prompt turns a gratuity into what it called a pre-service toll, without actually guaranteeing a faster pickup.

        a: "Rs 10 lakh is roughly $12,000, a sum too small to function as a real deterrent against a company operating at Ola's scale, where the dark pattern likely generated far more in extra tips than the fine claws back, making it closer to a cost of doing business than a punishment."
      - q: "Is Ola the only ride-hailing app the CCPA has penalised over tipping prompts?"
        a: "No, the CCPA has scrutinised advance-tipping prompts across ride-hailing apps more broadly, fining other platforms like Rapido Rs 10 lakh over similar prompts, while treating some competitors differently depending on how their interface presents the tipping choice."
