    body: >-
      Time to bust one the ads won't tell you: 'Get a VPN and be 100% anonymous online.' What a VPN actually does is hide your IP and browsing from your ISP and encrypt your traffic on sketchy public WiFi — genuinely useful. What it does not do: log you out of Google or Instagram (you're still signed in and tracked), kill cookies and browser fingerprinting, or hide you from the VPN company itself. A shady 'free' VPN can be worse than your ISP. Pick a paid, no-logs provider and use it for what it's actually good at. 🕵️
    problem: >-
      VPN ads sell 'total anonymity', so people trust a VPN to make them untraceable — then behave as if they're invisible when they're still fully logged in and fingerprinted.
    breakthrough: >-
      The honest picture: a VPN reroutes and encrypts your traffic, hiding your IP and activity from your ISP and protecting you on public WiFi. That's real value — but it's about privacy in transit, not anonymity.
    catch: >-
      You're still identified by your logins, cookies and browser fingerprint, and the VPN provider itself sees your traffic — so a free, ad-funded 'no-logs' VPN can be a bigger privacy risk than your ISP.
    forYou: >-
      Use a VPN for what it's good at — public WiFi and hiding activity from your ISP — and choose a reputable paid, audited no-logs provider. Don't rely on it to make you anonymous; log out, clear cookies and mind your fingerprint for that.
