    catch: >-

      Most India riders never switch it on: when you open Directions in Google Maps, tap the two-wheeler (bike) icon sitting next to car and transit. Maps then routes you through narrow lanes and shortcuts that cars legally can't use, and recalculates the ETA for a two-wheeler's actual speed. It has been available in India and several other countries for years, hidden in plain sight — and on Indian roads it genuinely saves time.
    problem: >-
      Default Maps directions assume you're in a car, so they skip the narrow shortcuts and hand you an ETA that doesn't match how a scooter or bike really moves through traffic.
    breakthrough: >-
      Google Maps has a dedicated two-wheeler mode (available in India and select countries): tap the bike icon in Directions and it reroutes through two-wheeler-friendly shortcuts with a bike-specific ETA.
    catch: >-
      It's region-limited — the two-wheeler icon only shows up where Google has enabled it, and shortcut-data quality varies from city to city.
    forYou: >-
      If you ride a scooter or bike, switch to two-wheeler mode before your next trip — you'll often get a faster, more realistic route than the default car directions.
