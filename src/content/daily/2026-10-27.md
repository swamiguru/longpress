    heading: "TRAI wants a licence for the free TV apps on your smart TV"
    body: >-
      TRAI on October 26 recommended that free live-TV apps like Samsung TV Plus, YuppTV and Xiaomi TV+ hold a government licence, with Indian data servers and a one-year transition, as [MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/10/223-trai-live-tv-apps-govt-licence/) reported. Licensing the distributor I can live with; cable operators have done it for decades. The part that bothers me is quieter: internet-only news channels can't register at all, and TV makers would have to pull any app that isn't on the ministry's list. That's the government curating your TV's app store.
    image: "/images/daily/2026-10-27/illustration_2.png"
    problem: >-
      Free ad-supported TV apps now come preinstalled on most smart TVs and stream hundreds of channels, many of which exist only online and sit outside both cable rules and the IT Act.
    breakthrough: >-
      TRAI calls them Application-based Linear Television Distribution services and wants a Rs 1 lakh entry fee for a 20-year licence, Indian-only ownership, data kept in India and approved channels only. Broadcasters' own apps, social media live streams and on-demand services like Netflix are left out. Jio and Sony opposed the framework during the consultation.
    catch: >-
      It's a recommendation, not a rule. The ministry asked for it in December 2025 and still has to accept it, and there's an open question whether the Telecommunications Act even covers internet apps, given the government has said it doesn't apply to OTT.
    forYou: >-
      Nothing disappears from your TV today. If you rely on an internet-only news channel through one of these apps, though, keep an eye on this one, because that's the content the draft shuts out.
    question: "Will free live TV apps like Samsung TV Plus need a licence in India?"
    answer: >-
      Possibly. On October 26, 2026, TRAI recommended that apps streaming linear TV channels, such as Samsung TV Plus, YuppTV and Xiaomi TV+, get a government licence under the Telecommunications Act, 2023. The Information and Broadcasting Ministry still has to accept it before it becomes a rule.
    qa:
      - q: "What would TRAI's live TV app licence cost and require?"
        a: "TRAI proposes a Rs 10,000 processing fee and a Rs 1 lakh entry fee for a 20-year national licence, open only to Indian-registered companies. User, payment and viewing data would have to sit on servers in India, apps could carry only approved channels, and each would need an Indian grievance officer and six-monthly compliance reports."
      - q: "Can internet-only news channels stream on these TV apps under TRAI's plan?"
        a: "No. Internet-only channels could register with the Information and Broadcasting Ministry for Rs 1 lakh a year, but internet-only news channels are barred from registering at all. Industry submissions told TRAI that about 70% of channels on these apps exist only online, so that clause matters more than the fees."
      - q: "Will TRAI's rules remove apps from my smart TV in India?"
        a: "Not yet. TV makers and app stores wouldn't need a licence, but they'd have to check apps against the ministry's master list and remove unlicensed ones. Existing apps would get one year after the rules are notified, and the ministry hasn't accepted the recommendations, so nothing on your TV changes today."

    heading: "Stop strangers adding you to WhatsApp groups"
    body: >-
      WhatsApp's Groups privacy setting, under Settings, Privacy, Groups, lets you choose My Contacts or My Contacts Except, so strangers can't drop you into a group without a private invite that expires in three days, per [WhatsApp's own blog](https://blog.whatsapp.com/new-privacy-settings-for-groups). Most people leave it on Everyone, which is how the fake 'trading tips' groups and stray APK files keep finding them. A fake mParivahan app reportedly reached an 86-year-old in Ahmedabad this week through a WhatsApp group, per [The420](https://the420.in/india-cyber-tech-brief-26-october-2026-hawala-cyberfraud-ai-security). Two minutes, one door closed.
    image: "/images/daily/2026-10-27/illustration_3.png"
    problem: >-
      On default settings anyone with your number can add you to a WhatsApp group, and scammers use that to push fake investment pitches and malicious app files at scale.
    breakthrough: >-
      Settings, Privacy, Groups offers Everyone, My Contacts or My Contacts Except. On anything but Everyone, an admin who isn't allowed to add you gets prompted to send a private invite instead, and it lapses after three days.
    catch: >-
      It does nothing about contacts who are already saved, or about scammers who message you one-to-one, so pair it with WhatsApp's unknown-caller and unknown-message protections.
    forYou: >-
      Set it to My Contacts on your phone, then do the same on your parents' phones this weekend. They're the ones these groups are aimed at.
    question: "How do I stop strangers adding me to WhatsApp groups?"
    answer: >-
      Open WhatsApp Settings, then Privacy, then Groups, and pick My Contacts or My Contacts Except. People outside that list can no longer add you directly; they have to send a private invite, which you can ignore and which expires after three days.
    qa:
      - q: "What happens when someone can't add me to a WhatsApp group?"
        a: "WhatsApp prompts the group admin to send you a private invite in a one-to-one chat instead. You decide whether to join, and if you do nothing the invite expires after three days. The group never appears in your chat list unless you accept, which removes the easiest route for scam trading groups."
      - q: "Can I block specific contacts from adding me to WhatsApp groups?"
        a: "Yes. Choose My Contacts Except under Settings, Privacy, Groups, then select the saved contacts you don't want adding you, or select all of them. WhatsApp replaced its older Nobody option with this, so selecting every contact is now the way to make sure nobody can add you without an invite."

    heading: "That mParivahan app on WhatsApp is not the government's"
    body: >-
      An 86-year-old retired professor in Ahmedabad lost about Rs 10.8 lakh from two bank accounts after a fake mParivahan APK reportedly reached him through a WhatsApp group, [The420](https://the420.in/india-cyber-tech-brief-26-october-2026-hawala-cyberfraud-ai-security) reported on October 26. The myth that keeps this scam alive is simple: if it says mParivahan, it must be official. It isn't, and no real challan ever needs an install. Kerala police warned about exactly this trick in September, and it's still working in October, which tells you the warnings aren't reaching the people who need them.
    image: "/images/daily/2026-10-27/illustration_4.png"
    problem: >-
      Traffic fines feel official and urgent, and the government really does run apps called mParivahan and e-Challan, so a file with that name looks believable, especially to older users.
    breakthrough: >-
      Besides the Ahmedabad case, Kerala police in September described fake e-challan sites that ask for a vehicle number and then push an APK through a fake 'Internal Security Review' prompt, per [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/india/keralam-police-warn-of-malicious-apk-scam-via-fake-e-challan-sms/3053993/). PIB Fact Check had already flagged challan links and APKs that expose OTPs.
    catch: >-
      Police haven't confirmed exactly how the Ahmedabad phone was compromised, so the APK is the reported route, not a proven one.
    forYou: >-
      Check fines only by typing the Parivahan e-Challan address yourself, never install an APK from a chat, and if you already did, go offline, call your bank and dial 1930.
    question: "Is an mParivahan app sent on WhatsApp for an e-challan genuine?"
    answer: >-
      No. Real traffic challans never require installing an app from a message. On October 26, 2026, an 86-year-old in Ahmedabad was reported to have lost about Rs 10.8 lakh after a fake mParivahan APK arrived through a WhatsApp group. Check fines only on the official Parivahan e-Challan portal.
    qa:
      - q: "How does the fake e-challan APK scam work in India?"
        a: "A message on SMS or WhatsApp says a traffic fine is pending and pushes a link or an APK file named like mParivahan. Kerala police said in September 2026 that fake sites even show an 'Internal Security Review' prompt before the download. Once installed, the app can read SMS and OTPs, letting fraudsters move money from bank and UPI accounts."
      - q: "What should I do if I installed a fake mParivahan app?"
        a: "Kerala police advise disconnecting the phone from the internet, avoiding any banking on it, and removing the app if you can. Check your UPI and bank transactions, call your bank straight away if anything looks wrong, change passwords from a different device, and report it on the 1930 helpline or the cybercrime portal."
      - q: "How can I check a traffic challan safely in India?"
        a: "PIB Fact Check says to verify challans only on the official Parivahan e-Challan portal, reached by typing the address yourself rather than tapping a link, and to download apps only from official app stores. Treat any message demanding urgent payment or an app install to clear a fine as a scam."
