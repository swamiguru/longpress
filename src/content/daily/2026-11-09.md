    image: "/images/daily/2026-11-09/illustration_1.png"
    problem: >-
      Digital arrest scams work because they combine the authority of a uniform with the isolation of a phone call: victims are kept on camera, told not to contact anyone, and given no time to think before acting.
    breakthrough: >-
      In this case, fraudsters held a 78-year-old retired executive in Mumbai under fake 'arrest' for 26 days between October 6 and November 1, 2026, accusing him of laundering money tied to the 2025 Pahalgam terror attack, and extracted Rs 1.9 crore in transfers between October 14 and 26 before his nephew, a US-based doctor, intervened, according to [The420](https://the420.in/mumbai-pahalgam-digital-arrest-cyber-fraud-case).
    catch: >-
      The case is now registered with police, but the money moved through a mule account in Uttar Pradesh, which is typically the hardest part of these frauds to recover.
    forYou: >-
      If you or an older relative ever gets a video call claiming to be police, hang up, call a family member, and verify independently through the official helpline, never the number the caller gives you.

    heading: "Silence Unknown WhatsApp Callers"
    body: >-
      WhatsApp has a setting that quietly defangs one of the most common tricks scammers use: the surprise video call. Go to Settings, Privacy, Calls, and turn on Silence Unknown Callers. Numbers outside your contacts stop ringing and move into a list you check on your own time instead of answering in a panic. Most 'digital arrest' and impersonation scams rely on catching someone off guard the moment the phone rings. Take away the ring, and you take away a chunk of the fear. Set this up on your parents' phones this week, not just your own.
    image: "/images/daily/2026-11-09/illustration_3.png"
    problem: >-
      Scammers rely on the shock of a sudden, unexpected video call to keep victims from thinking clearly, and most people have no easy way to filter those calls out before picking up.
    breakthrough: >-
      WhatsApp's Silence Unknown Callers setting, confirmed on [WhatsApp's own blog](https://blog.whatsapp.com/new-privacy-features-silence-unknown-callers-and-privacy-checkup), stops any call from a number outside your contacts from ringing at all, routing it instead to your Call list, found under Settings, Privacy, Calls on both Android and iPhone.
    catch: >-
      It only silences calls, not messages, so a scammer can still text you; the setting is also easy to miss since it's buried a few menus deep and off by default.
    forYou: >-
      Turn it on for yourself and, more importantly, set it up on an older relative's phone this week, that's where these scam calls do the most damage.
    question: "How do I stop scam video calls on WhatsApp?"
    answer: >-
      Turn on WhatsApp's Silence Unknown Callers setting under Settings, Privacy, Calls: any call from a number outside your contacts stops ringing immediately and lands quietly in a list you can review later, which blocks the cold-call shock tactic scammers use in 'digital arrest' and other fraud calls.
    qa:
      - q: "Where is the Silence Unknown Callers setting in WhatsApp?"
        a: "Open WhatsApp, go to Settings, then Privacy, then Calls, and toggle on Silence Unknown Callers. It works the same way on both Android and iPhone, and it only affects calls, your chats from unknown numbers still arrive normally, you're just not interrupted by a ringing call from a stranger."
      - q: "Will I miss important calls if I turn this on?"
        a: "No, calls from unknown numbers aren't blocked, they're just silenced and moved into a separate list under Calls that you can check whenever you want, so a genuine caller still leaves a record you can see and call back, you simply won't be caught off guard by the ring."
