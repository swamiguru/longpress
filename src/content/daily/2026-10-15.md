    body: >-
      WhatsApp has a setting called Block unknown account messages, under Settings, Privacy, Advanced. Once it's on, an unknown account that blasts you with messages gets blocked automatically before it fills your chat list. It doesn't screen every stranger, and WhatsApp doesn't say what counts as high volume, so treat it as a filter for the spray-and-pray scammers rather than a force field. Ten seconds to switch on, and it pairs well with today's digital-arrest warning.
    image: "/images/daily/2026-10-15/illustration_3.png"
    problem: >-
      Scam campaigns on WhatsApp often start with a burst of messages from a number you've never saved, and by the time you block it, the hook is already in.
    breakthrough: >-
      WhatsApp's Block unknown account messages toggle, under Settings, Privacy, Advanced, automatically blocks unknown accounts that send messages in high volume, as [Techloy's walkthrough](https://techloy.com/how-to-block-unknown-account-messages-on-whatsapp) shows.
    catch: >-
      It's a volume filter. WhatsApp doesn't publish the threshold, and one quiet, well-written scam message can still land.
    forYou: >-
      Switch it on today, add Silence unknown callers under Privacy, Calls, and set both up on your parents' phones too.
    question: "How do I block messages from unknown numbers on WhatsApp?"
    answer: >-
      Turn on Block unknown account messages: in WhatsApp go to Settings, then Privacy, then Advanced, and switch it on. WhatsApp will then block messages from an unknown account when it sends you an unusually high volume. It won't stop every single stranger, but it kills the floods.
    qa:
      - q: "Does WhatsApp Block unknown account messages stop all scam messages?"
        a: "No. It's built for high-volume messages from accounts not in your contacts, and WhatsApp doesn't say what volume triggers it. A scammer who sends one careful message can still reach you, so keep treating unknown numbers with suspicion."
      - q: "What else should I turn on in WhatsApp against scam calls?"
        a: "Pair it with Silence unknown callers under Settings, Privacy, Calls. Calls from numbers not in your contacts stop ringing but still show up in your call list. Together the two settings cut most of the noise scammers use to get your attention."

    body: >-
      There is no such thing as a digital arrest, and Hyderabad Police said so again on October 14 after a 72-year-old woman lost Rs 2.20 crore to a fake CBI officer on a video call. The script is always the same: a parcel or your Aadhaar is linked to a crime, an officer appears on camera with a warrant, you're told to stay alone and tell nobody, and then comes the deposit. The newest twist I've seen is in Pune, where scammers name-dropped the Data Protection Board of India to sound current. If someone official wants you alone, on camera and paying, you already know the answer.
    image: "/images/daily/2026-10-15/illustration_4.png"
    problem: >-
      Digital-arrest scams keep working because they hijack fear of authority, and they target older people who are least likely to question a uniform on a screen.
    breakthrough: >-
      On October 14, [Hyderabad Police](https://socialnews.xyz/2026/10/14/hyderabad-police-cautions-citizens-against-digital-arrest-fraud) warned that no government agency demands money over phone or video calls and that digital arrest doesn't exist, citing a Rs 2.20 crore loss. The same week, [The420](https://the420.in/india-cyber-tech-brief-top-10-cybersecurity-ai-news-14-october-2026/) reported a Pune woman losing Rs 68 lakh across nine UPI apps, and Delhi Police arresting 11 people tied to a Cambodia-linked ring.
    catch: >-
      Warnings like this have been going out for two years and losses are still growing, which says awareness alone isn't fixing it. Banks flagging large, odd transfers from seniors would do more.
    forYou: >-
      Have the five-minute talk with your parents this weekend: no officer arrests anyone on video, hang up, call back on a known number, and report on 1930 straight away.
    question: "Can police or CBI put you under digital arrest on a video call in India?"
    answer: >-
      No. There is no such thing as a digital arrest in Indian law. Hyderabad Police repeated on October 14, 2026 that no government agency or police force will demand money over a phone or video call. Anyone keeping you on camera and asking for a deposit is running a scam.
    qa:

      - q: "How much money have people lost to digital arrest scams in 2026?"
        a: "Single cases run into crores. Hyderabad Police cited a 72-year-old woman who lost Rs 2.20 crore after a fake CBI video call. In Pune, a woman sent Rs 68 lakh through nine UPI apps after scammers claimed the Data Protection Board was investigating her."

        a: "No. Scammers in a Pune case used the Data Protection Board's name to sound official, but the board doesn't phone individuals demanding money. Any call that drops a new government body's name and then asks you to pay is the same digital-arrest script with fresh vocabulary."
