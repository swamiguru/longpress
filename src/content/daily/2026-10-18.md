    body: >-
      Google said on October 15 that AI Overviews and AI Mode will show source links higher up and more often, test a section at the top with the one or two official sites for a task, and give related-article links proper thumbnails, per its [Google blog post](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/updates-ai-mode-overviews-explore-web/). Good. It's also a quiet admission that burying links under a wall of AI text was hurting the web those answers are built from. What's missing is the number that matters: there's zero click data, so for now this is a design change, not proof that anyone gets traffic back. If you run a site, watch your Search Console for a month rather than taking the blog post's word for it.

    problem: >-

    breakthrough: >-
      In a [Google blog post](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/updates-ai-mode-overviews-explore-web/) on October 15, VP of product Hema Budaraju said inline links are being surfaced higher, an official-sites section is in testing, news carousels are expanding to more languages and will favour your Preferred Sources, and creator names on AI Mode video results are being tested as clickable profile links.
    catch: >-
      There's no click data anywhere in the announcement, and the official-sites box is only a test. The one number Google did share, Preferred Sources use up more than 7x this year, tells you people like a feature, not that sites are getting visits.

The policy already bans this kind of app, which makes the gap between the rulebook and the listing the real story here. NCPCR's answer is a set of guidelines due by December that won't bind anyone, so the actual pressure has to land on Google enforcing what it already wrote.
    image: "/images/daily/2026-10-18/illustration_2.png"

NCPCR is also drafting child safety guidelines for social, gaming and AI platforms with MeitY, DoT and I4C, due by December.

    body: >-
      Google Search lets you pick your preferred news sources: search any news topic, tap the star next to the Top Stories heading and choose the sites you trust, as [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/google-preferred-sources-rolling-out-in-us-and-india-460631) explained when it rolled out in India. Most people have never touched it, which is why Top Stories feels like whichever outlet happened to rank that morning. It now matters a bit more, because Google's [October 15 post](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/updates-ai-mode-overviews-explore-web/) says the news carousels in AI Mode will lean on those picks too. Two minutes of setup and the outlets you'd open anyway show up first. It only lists sites that publish regularly, so a dormant blog won't appear.
    image: "/images/daily/2026-10-18/illustration_3.png"
    problem: >-
      Top Stories on Google is a lucky draw. You get whichever outlet ranked that hour, including ones you'd never choose to read.
    breakthrough: >-
      Preferred Sources, which [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/google-preferred-sources-rolling-out-in-us-and-india-460631) reported rolling out in the US and India, puts a star icon beside the Top Stories header. Tap it, select the sites you trust, and Google shows more of their fresh coverage when it's relevant. Per [Google](https://blog.google/products-and-platforms/products/search/updates-ai-mode-overviews-explore-web/), AI Mode news carousels will now highlight those picks as well.
    catch: >-
      It's English only for now, and a site has to be publishing fresh content to show up as an option. Your picks get more room in Top Stories; they don't replace everything else.
    forYou: >-
      Search something newsy today, tap the star and add the three or four outlets you trust most. You can change the list whenever you like.
    question: "How do I set preferred sources in Google Search Top Stories in India?"
    answer: >-
      Search any news topic on Google, find the Top Stories section, tap the star icon next to its heading, then pick the sites you trust. Google then shows more of their latest stories when relevant. It works in India in English, and only sites publishing fresh content can be picked.

      - q: "Can I change my Google preferred sources later?"
        a: "Yes.     heading: "Voice-only recharge plans are coming. Don't expect them cheap."
    body: >-
      TRAI's rule forcing Jio, Airtel and Vi to sell voice-and-SMS-only twins of their bundled plans kicks in around October 21, per [Analytics Insight](https://www.analyticsinsight.net/news/trai-new-rule-airtel-jio-vi-must-launch-voice-sms-only-recharge-plans). On paper it's a win for feature-phone users and backup SIMs that have been paying for data they never touch. In practice, TRAI only asked for an 'appropriate reduction' in price and left the number to the operators, and none of them has announced a plan yet. I'd bet the gap ends up small enough that most people shrug and stay on data. The one SIM worth checking is your parents' number that only takes calls and OTPs, the week these vouchers actually show up in the apps.
    image: "/images/daily/2026-10-18/illustration_4.png"
    problem: >-
      If a SIM only makes calls and receives OTPs, you've still been made to buy a plan with data, because operators mostly stopped selling anything without it.
    breakthrough: >-
      TRAI's Telecom Consumer Protection (Thirteenth Amendment) Regulations, published in September, require a voice-and-SMS-only voucher for every bundled plan of 30 days or less, at least one that renews on the same date each month, and one longer-validity option, [The Hans India](https://www.thehansindia.com/tech/trai-mandates-cheaper-voice-only-recharges-what-changes-for-airtel-jio-vi-users-1126876) reported. [Analytics Insight](https://www.analyticsinsight.net/news/trai-new-rule-airtel-jio-vi-must-launch-voice-sms-only-recharge-plans) puts the start date at October 21.
    catch: >-
      There's no price floor. TRAI wants an appropriate reduction in tariff, which the operators get to define, and none of the three has published a voice-only price.
    forYou: >-
      If you pay for a parent's or a backup SIM, wait for the new vouchers and compare them with the cheapest data plan. If the saving is tiny, keeping a little data for emergencies is worth more.
    question: "When do Jio, Airtel and Vi voice and SMS only recharge plans start?"
    answer: >-
      TRAI's Thirteenth Amendment makes Jio, Airtel and Vi sell voice-and-SMS-only vouchers alongside their bundled plans from around October 21, 2026, 30 days after Gazette notification. TRAI only requires an appropriate reduction in price, so expect a modest discount, not plans at half the cost.
    qa:
      - q: "Will voice-only recharge plans be cheaper than data plans in India?"
        a: "Somewhat, but TRAI hasn't fixed a price. The Thirteenth Amendment asks for an appropriate reduction in tariff compared with the data-inclusive plan and leaves the actual number to each operator. As of mid-October none of Jio, Airtel or Vi had announced voice-only prices, so the size of the saving is still unknown."
      - q: "Do Jio and Airtel still sell 28-day plans under TRAI's new rule?"
        a: "Yes, 28-day plans aren't banned. If an operator sells a 28-day plan with data, it must also sell a 28-day voice-and-SMS-only version. It also has to offer at least one voice-and-SMS voucher that renews on the same calendar date each month, plus one longer-validity option."
      - q: "Who should switch to a voice and SMS only plan?"
        a: "Mainly feature-phone users, older parents and backup SIMs that only take calls and OTPs. If a number never touches mobile data, the new vouchers stop you paying for gigabytes nobody uses. For anyone on a smartphone who needs maps, UPI or WhatsApp on the go, a data plan still makes more sense."

      WhatsApp now lets people without an account join your calls: create a call link from the Calls tab, send it, and they join from a computer browser once you let them in, [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/technology/tech-news/whatsapp-introduces-guest-calling-for-non-users-how-the-feature-works-126101500368_1.html) reported on October 15. Calls stay free and end-to-end encrypted, with up to 32 people. It's aimed squarely at Google Meet and Zoom, and for the family call with the one cousin who deleted WhatsApp, or a client who refuses to install it, it's now the easier option. Guests can't see your number or message anyone, which is the right default. Bigger meetings will have to wait for the calendar plugins Meta has only promised for later this year.
    image: "/images/daily/2026-10-18/illustration_5.png"
    problem: >-
      Every group call has one person who isn't on WhatsApp, and the fallback has been a second app, a separate link and five minutes of 'can you hear me'.
    breakthrough: >-
      Hosts open the Calls tab, tap +, create a call link and share it. Guests open it in a computer browser, enter a name and wait in a lobby until admitted, per [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/technology/tech-news/whatsapp-introduces-guest-calling-for-non-users-how-the-feature-works-126101500368_1.html). Calls hold up to 32 people, and guests can screen share, react and raise a hand; [gHacks](https://www.ghacks.net/2026/10/16/whatsapp-rolls-out-guest-calling-so-people-without-an-account-can-join-calls-from-a-browser/) also reported the rollout.
    catch: >-
      Guests join from a computer browser, not a phone, and can't start calls of their own. The Google Calendar and Outlook plugins that would make this a real meeting tool are only due later this year.
    forYou: >-
      Next time someone isn't on WhatsApp, send a call link instead of switching apps. Admit only the names you recognise from the waiting room.
    question: "How do I invite someone without WhatsApp to a WhatsApp call?"
    answer: >-
      Open the Calls tab, tap +, create a call link and send it. Since WhatsApp's October 15, 2026 update, the guest opens the link in a computer browser, types a name and waits for you to admit them. Calls stay free and end-to-end encrypted, with up to 32 people.
    qa:

      - q: "Can WhatsApp guest calls replace Google Meet or Zoom?"
        a: "For small family or client calls, mostly yes, since group calls go up to 32 people and guests install nothing. For proper work meetings, not yet. WhatsApp's Google Calendar and Outlook plugins, which would put call links straight into meeting invites, are only promised for later this year."
