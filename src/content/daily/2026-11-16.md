    body: >-
      Delhi Cyber Police arrested four men across Punjab and Rajasthan for running a mule-account pipeline that drained Rs 15.5 lakh from a retired Army Colonel's family in Munirka Enclave. The scammers used a forged [Enforcement Directorate](https://telanganatoday.com/retired-colonel-wife-held-under-digital-arrest-lose-rs-15-50-lakh-delhi-police-nab-4) warrant, fake court papers and a monitored video call that the family were told never to hang up on. That part you've heard before.
      
      What's new is the back-end. Investigators traced the money to a current bank account in Ludhiana sold on Telegram for a cut. The seller passed it to a middleman, who passed it to a Rajasthan handler, who allegedly supplied accounts to scam compounds in Cambodia and the UAE. That one account alone was linked to 17 separate complaints across several states.
      
      One call, many victims, one rented account. The person on your screen pretending to be a cop isn't the business. The business is a quiet market in bank accounts, with bank-employee help in the mix.
    problem: >-
      Digital-arrest arrests in India have focused on the loudest end of the pipeline, the fake cop on the video call. The middle, where people sell current bank accounts to scammers for a commission, has stayed largely untouched. One compromised account routes money for dozens of victims.
    breakthrough: >-
      Delhi Cyber Police arrested four men across Punjab and Rajasthan on Friday after tracing Rs 15.5 lakh from the Colonel's family to a Ludhiana current account. Sarbjit (38) sold the account; Suraj (30) and Sandeep (32, a bank employee) ran the resale; Naresh alias Lucky in Sikar allegedly supplied accounts to scam compounds in Cambodia and the UAE. That single account was linked to 17 complaints.
    catch: >-
      Four arrests against an industrial pipeline is a dent, not a solution. Most of the money has already moved overseas, and the article doesn't report recovered funds. The bank employee angle is what deserves more weight than it got.

    catch: >-

    forYou: >-
