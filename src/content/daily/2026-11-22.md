    heading: "RBI Just Capped Your Cash Transfers"
    body: >-
      RBI has consolidated its domestic money transfer rules into one direction, effective November 11, mainly aimed at migrant workers and anyone without a bank account. Cash pay-outs to a beneficiary with no account are now capped at Rs 10,000 a transfer and Rs 25,000 a month. Walk-in customers can still deposit cash and send up to Rs 50,000 via NEFT to someone's bank account, but ATM and Business Correspondent agent transfers are capped tighter, at Rs 5,000 a transfer. Banks also have to track unusual transaction patterns, flag sudden credit spikes and send alerts. It's a genuine safeguard against misuse, and also a real slowdown for someone trying to wire a full month's wages home in one shot.
    image: "/images/daily/2026-11-22/illustration_2.png"
    problem: >-
      Millions of migrant workers and unbanked Indians send cash home through informal bank-counter and agent networks that had scattered, inconsistent limits and weak tracking, leaving room for both fraud and confusion over what's even allowed.
    breakthrough: >-
      RBI issued the Domestic Money Transfer Directions, 2026, effective November 11, setting a Rs 10,000 per-transfer and Rs 25,000 monthly cap on cash pay-outs to unbanked beneficiaries, a Rs 50,000 NEFT cap for walk-in cash deposits, and a tighter Rs 5,000 cap on ATM or agent-based transfers.
    catch: >-
      The rules let banks and payment operators charge whatever they decide is a reasonable fee, with no cap specified, so the actual cost of sending money home could still swing a lot depending on which channel you use.
    forYou: >-
      If you or someone in your family sends cash home through a bank counter or agent rather than a UPI transfer, check these new caps before you go, because a single large remittance may now need to be split across more than one trip.
    question: "What are RBI's new Domestic Money Transfer Directions 2026?"
    answer: >-
      The RBI (Domestic Money Transfer) Directions, 2026, effective November 11, cap cash pay-outs to people without a bank account at Rs 10,000 per transfer and Rs 25,000 a month. Walk-in NEFT deposits still allow up to Rs 50,000, while ATM or agent-based transfers are capped tighter, at Rs 5,000 per transfer.
    qa:
      - q: "Who do RBI's new domestic money transfer rules affect most?"
        a: "The rules mainly affect migrant workers and anyone without a bank account who relies on cash pay-out services, remitting through a relative's or friend's bank account, or sending money via ATMs and Business Correspondent agents. Scheduled banks, RRBs, co-operative banks and authorised payment operators must all comply; card-to-card transfers are excluded."
      - q: "Why did RBI tighten domestic money transfer limits now?"
        a: "RBI consolidated scattered small-value transfer rules into one direction and added safeguards: monitoring unusual transaction frequency, flagging sudden credit spikes, sending credit alerts, and including sender details in IMPS and NEFT messages. The stated goal is making remittances more accessible while guarding against misuse, not a specific fraud case."
      - q: "Can banks charge extra fees under the new transfer rules?"
        a: "Yes. The directions let banks and non-bank payment operators set what RBI calls reasonable charges, with no fixed uniform fee specified. That means the cost of sending cash home could vary noticeably between your bank, a Business Correspondent agent, and a UPI app, so it's worth comparing before you send."

    breakthrough: >-
      A simple, repeatable workflow: paste the suspicious message in full into any major AI chatbot and ask it specifically to flag urgency cues, sender mismatches and scripted phrasing against how real banks communicate.
    catch: >-
      This is a second opinion, not a verdict. AI chatbots can miss a convincing fake and can also flag a legitimate message as suspicious, so it's a filter before you act, not a replacement for checking with your bank directly.
    forYou: >-
      Save this as a habit, not a one-time trick: the next odd bank text you get, paste it into whichever AI app you already have open before you tap anything.
