The [Washington Post report](https://www.spokesman.com/stories/2026/nov/26/fcc-approves-1-million-spacex-data-centers-in-spac/) says 500,000 are expected to be operating by November 2038 and the rest by 2040, under a 15-year licence.
    catch: >-

    forYou: >-

    answer: >-

    heading: "Search a seller on the government portal before paying"
    body: >-
      As of 27 November 2026, you can search a suspicious seller's mobile number or UPI ID on India's cybercrime.gov.in portal before paying, under Report & Check Suspect. It isn't a new tool, and I'm surprised how rarely it comes up. It costs you a minute, which is cheap on a Black Friday when a stranger wants money upfront.
    image: "/images/daily/2026-11-27/illustration_3.png"
    problem: >-
      Sale season is when unfamiliar sellers, sponsored ads and too-good prices all arrive together, and a UPI payment to the wrong one is hard to pull back.
    breakthrough: >-
      A [government release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleseDetailm.aspx?PRID=2082765&reg=48&lang=2) says citizens can search the Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre's repository of cyber criminal identifiers through Suspect Search. [Cybermithra's guide](https://cybermithra.in/2025/06/04/report-or-verify-suspected-cyber-fraudsters/) gives the path as Report & Check Suspect, then the check suspect option for mobile, email and similar details.
    catch: >-
      A clean result only means no match was found. Menu labels have differed between published guides, and I couldn't load the live portal today, so expect the wording on screen to vary a little.
    forYou: >-
      Run the search before paying anyone you found through an ad or a forwarded link. The [Telangana cyber bureau's advisory](https://www.siasat.com/black-friday-sale-scams-telangana-cyber-bureau-issues-advisory-3587886/) also says never to share OTPs, UPI PINs or CVVs, and to report fraud on 1930.
    question: "How do I check if a UPI ID is fraud in India?"
    answer: >-
      Go to cybercrime.gov.in, open Report & Check Suspect and search the UPI ID, mobile number, email or bank account. The government's Suspect Search looks through the cybercrime coordination centre's records. A match is a warning, but no match doesn't prove the seller is safe.
    qa:
      - q: "What can I search in the cybercrime suspect check?"
        a: "Published guides list mobile numbers, UPI IDs, email addresses, bank account numbers and social media profile IDs, with a separate option for websites and apps. Use whatever detail the seller has actually given you. If they offer several, check each one, since a report may be attached to only one of them."
      - q: "Does no result mean a seller is safe?"
        a: "No. The search works from data the public and agencies have reported, so a freshly created number or account may show nothing at all. The portal's information may also not be accurate every time. Treat an empty result as unknown, and keep your usual caution about advance payments and unrealistic discounts."
      - q: "What should I do if I already paid a fake seller?"
        a: "Call the national cybercrime helpline 1930 straight away and file a complaint at cybercrime.gov.in. Tell your bank too, and keep screenshots of the listing, the chat and the payment reference. Never share an OTP, UPI PIN or card CVV with anyone offering to recover the money."

    heading: "TRAI's AI rules need a human you can reach"
    body: >-
      TRAI's comment window on its draft AI guidelines for telecom closes on 27 November 2026, and the Internet Freedom Foundation says the draft gives users no right to contest an AI decision. My view is that it should. If software can act against your number, you ought to be told, and you ought to be able to get a person to look at it.
    image: "/images/daily/2026-11-27/illustration_4.png"
    problem: >-
      Networks can use software to make calls about traffic, spam and risk that a subscriber never sees. When one goes wrong, you may not even know a machine made it.
    breakthrough: >-
      [MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/11/223-telcos-third-party-trais-anthropic/) reports the draft asks for board-approved AI policies, risk ratings, human oversight and complaint resolution within a reasonable time. The Internet Freedom Foundation, [as MediaNama reports](https://www.medianama.com/2026/11/223-trai-telecom-ai-guidelines-iff/), says there's no user right to review automated decisions.
    catch: >-
      The draft does give consumers a complaint route and requires human oversight, so the gap is a disputed reading rather than a blank page. These are also draft guidelines that can still change, and a reasonable time isn't a deadline anyone can hold a company to.
    forYou: >-
      If a call, message or connection gets blocked and you can't work out why, complain to your operator in writing and keep the reference. And if you meant to send TRAI a comment, today's the listed last day.
    question: "What do TRAI's draft AI rules mean for phone users in India?"
    answer: >-
      TRAI's draft AI guidelines, released on 30 October 2026, put duties on telecom operators such as risk ratings, human oversight and complaint handling. The Internet Freedom Foundation argues they give subscribers no right to be told about, or seek human review of, an AI decision.
    qa:
      - q: "When is the deadline to comment on TRAI's AI draft?"
        a: "The deadline is 27 November 2026. The Internet Freedom Foundation asked on 23 November for an extension to 18 December and for a round of counter-comments. Elets reported that TRAI hadn't publicly responded as of 25 November, so don't assume extra time unless the regulator announces it."
      - q: "What must telecom companies do under TRAI's draft AI guidelines?"
        a: "Operators would need a board-approved AI policy, an inventory rating each system high, medium or low risk, testing before launch, incident logging and human oversight. They would stay accountable for AI bought from vendors. MediaNama reports the draft adds no new penalties, leaving existing laws and licence conditions to apply."
      - q: "Does the TRAI AI draft cover spam filtering and call blocking?"
        a: "The Internet Freedom Foundation says it doesn't mention AI used for filtering, blocking, traffic management or monitoring users. That is the group's reading of the draft, and the guidelines do include a route for consumer complaints about AI-related harm. The text is still a consultation draft."
