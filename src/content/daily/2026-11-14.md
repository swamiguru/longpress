A grace-period rule quietly removing someone's only buffer gets a lot less attention than it deserves.

    problem: >-
      H-1B holders currently get 60 discretionary days after losing a job to find new sponsorship, and that buffer is the only thing standing between a layoff and an immediate scramble to leave the country.
    breakthrough: >-
      DHS has proposed a rule titled [Eliminating the Discretionary 60-day Grace Period](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/news/us-proposes-new-rule-to-scrap-60-day-grace-period-for-h-1b-workers-after-job-loss), opening a 60-day public comment period that will decide whether this buffer disappears for H-1B, L-1, O-1, TN and related visa categories.
    catch: >-
      The rule isn't finalised and carries no effective date yet, so nothing changes immediately, but a rule this consequential moving through quietly, with comparatively little coverage, is exactly how major shifts land before most affected workers notice.
    forYou: >-
      If you or someone you know is on H-1B, it's worth tracking this specific proposal, not just visa fee news, since a finalised version would change what happens the day a job ends, not just what it costs to get the visa in the first place.
    question: "What is the DHS proposal to end the H-1B 60-day grace period?"
    answer: >-
      DHS proposed eliminating the discretionary 60-day grace period that currently lets H-1B and similar visa holders stay in the US after losing a job to search for new work. If finalised, most would have to leave almost immediately instead, and some could face a Notice to Appear, the first step toward removal proceedings.
