    heading: "Myth: Incognito mode makes you invisible online"
    body: >-
      Incognito (or Private) mode is the most misunderstood button in tech. All it actually does is stop your browser from saving history, cookies and form data on that specific device. Your internet provider, your office or college WiFi admin, the websites you visit, and any account you log into can all still see you. Incognito is genuinely useful — for shared computers or signing into a second account — but it was never anonymity. Don't trust it for the wrong job.
    problem: >-
      Millions of people use Incognito mode believing it hides their activity from their ISP, their employer, or the websites themselves — and make real privacy decisions based on that false sense of security.
    breakthrough: >-
      The truth: Incognito only stops your browser from saving history, cookies and form entries locally on that device. It does nothing to hide you from your internet provider, network admin, the sites you visit, or any account you sign into.
    catch: >-
      Even a VPN — the usual next step — hides your traffic from the network but not from services you log into, and it shifts your trust to the VPN provider. True online anonymity is genuinely hard, and Incognito isn't even the first step toward it.
    forYou: >-
      Use Incognito for what it's good at: shared or public computers, and logging into a second account. For real network privacy use a reputable VPN, and remember that signing into any account instantly ends the 'private' part.
