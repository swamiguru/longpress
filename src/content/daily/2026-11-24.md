    heading: "SIM binding is a tracing tool, not a scam shield"
    body: >-
      DoT's SIM-binding order, with a compliance deadline of December 31, 2026, will tie WhatsApp, Telegram and six other apps to a live SIM, [MediaNama reports](https://www.medianama.com/2026/11/223-questions-dot-must-answer-on-sim-binding/), but it won't stop most scams. One estimate cited there puts the drop in cybercrime at 2 to 3 percent. Think about how a digital arrest scam actually works: a call from a real number, a frightened victim, a transfer the victim makes themselves. Nothing in SIM binding touches any of that. It helps investigators trace an account. That's useful, but it isn't the same as protection.
    image: "/images/daily/2026-11-24/illustration_4.png"
    problem: >-
      Scams run through messaging apps, and the government wants accountability for who is behind each account. SIM binding is being sold as the fix.
    breakthrough: >-
      DoT's directive from November 2025 covers eight apps: WhatsApp, Telegram, Signal, Arattai, Snapchat, ShareChat, JioChat and Josh. Compliance is due by December 31, 2026 and the rules take effect January 1, 2027. Reported details include SIM checks every 24 hours. WhatsApp, Telegram and Arattai have confirmed they will comply.
    catch: >-
      The full order was never published, so what's required is partly guesswork. The 2 to 3 percent figure is one commentator's estimate, so the honest position is that nobody knows the real effect yet. The claim that it can't stop self-authorised transfers is my reading of how these scams work.
    forYou: >-
      Don't wait for the rule. Treat any call that threatens arrest as a scam, cap your own daily UPI limit, and silence unknown callers in WhatsApp.
    question: "Will SIM binding on WhatsApp and Telegram stop cyber fraud in India?"
    answer: >-
      Probably not much. DoT's SIM-binding order ties messaging apps to a live SIM, with compliance due by December 31, 2026, but one expert estimate cited by MediaNama puts the cut in cybercrime at only 2 to 3 percent. Scammers with real SIMs, or victims sending money themselves, aren't stopped.
