      Stop treating 'satellite' or map-style images as automatic proof of anything, especially screenshots shared on social. When a picture is doing a lot of persuading, check the original source before you believe it or reshare it.

    heading: "Stop relying on SMS codes"
    body: >-
      Scams are having a moment — fake IRS letters, malware that steals crypto seed phrases, and OTP-interception attacks all spiked this week. The single biggest upgrade you can make is switching from SMS one-time codes to passkeys. A passkey is a login tied to your device's Face ID or fingerprint, so there's no code to type and nothing for a scammer to phish, intercept, or SIM-swap. Google, Apple, WhatsApp and most banks support them now. It takes about two minutes — do your main email first, since that's the master key to everything else.
    problem: >-
      SMS one-time codes were never built for security. They can be phished on fake login pages, intercepted, or stolen via SIM-swap — and a wave of phishing scams this week (fake IRS letters, seed-phrase malware, OTP interception) is exploiting exactly that weakness.
    breakthrough: >-
      Passkeys are now widely supported across Google, Apple, WhatsApp and most major banks. Instead of a typed code, you approve logins with your device's Face ID or fingerprint, so there's no shared secret for an attacker to steal.
    catch: >-
      Passkeys are tied to your device and ecosystem, so you'll want a backup method and to make sure they sync (via iCloud Keychain or Google Password Manager) — otherwise losing your phone can lock you out. Not every service supports them yet, so you can't ditch SMS everywhere just yet.
    forYou: >-
      Spend two minutes today adding a passkey to your primary email account (Account settings > Security > Passkeys), since that inbox can reset every other login you own. Expand to your bank and socials from there.
