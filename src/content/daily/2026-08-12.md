    heading: "'100x zoom' doesn't mean 100x clearer photos"
    body: >-
      With two camera phones launching today flexing huge zoom numbers, a reality check: only your phone's optical or periscope zoom (usually 3-5x) uses real glass. Everything past that is a digital crop plus AI inventing detail — which is exactly why 'space zoom' moon shots look stunning but fall apart on ordinary subjects. Learn your phone's true optical zoom and use the big numbers to frame a shot, not to trust the detail. 🔍
    problem: >-
      Marketing pushes '30x' and '100x' zoom as if it's all real magnification, so people shoot expecting detail the phone can't actually resolve.
    breakthrough: >-
      The honest breakdown: a phone's optical/periscope lens (commonly 3-5x, up to ~10x on some) is the only true glass zoom; beyond it the phone crops the sensor and uses AI upscaling to guess pixels — which is why extreme zoom looks sharp on the moon (a known shape AI can reconstruct) but mushy on faces or text.
    catch: >-
      AI upscaling keeps improving and can look great for social sharing, so 'fake' zoom isn't useless — just don't expect real, printable detail past the optical limit.
    forYou: >-
      Check your phone's true optical zoom in the specs, stay within it when detail matters, and treat any number bigger than that as framing help, not proof of quality.
