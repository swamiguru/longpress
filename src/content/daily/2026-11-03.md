      Google broke ground this week in Visakhapatnam on what it calls its biggest AI hub outside the US. The commitment is $15 billion over five years, 2026 to 2030, and a 1-gigawatt data centre campus. [AdaniConneX](https://www.deccanherald.com/business/google-breaks-ground-on-15-billion-ai-hub-in-andhra-pradeshs-vizag-3983882) runs the physical build. Airtel's Nxtra lays the fibre and the subsea connectivity. Google Cloud supplies the chips, the Gemini models, and the billing. Andhra CM Chandrababu Naidu laid the foundation stone. Union IT minister Ashwini Vaishnaw attended. The scale is real: 1GW is a city's worth of power inside one campus. But look at who owns what. The buildings are Indian. The energy is Indian. The silicon, the model weights, and the pricing power are not. For a decade, India has had the users and the engineers for AI without the compute. This closes the compute gap, on Google's terms. It's a very large deal. It's also, by design, a landlord deal.
    image: "/images/daily/2026-11-03/illustration_1.png"
    problem: >-
      India has had the users and the engineers for AI for a decade, but the actual buildout has lived on US soil, with US power and US tax. For every Gemini query from a Delhi phone, the latency, the cost, and the IP returned to Oregon or Virginia. That gap has been the real bottleneck for Indian AI, not talent.
    breakthrough: >-
      [Google](https://www.deccanherald.com/business/google-breaks-ground-on-15-billion-ai-hub-in-andhra-pradeshs-vizag-3983882) broke ground this week on a 1-gigawatt AI data centre campus in Vizag, Andhra Pradesh — a $15 billion build phased 2026 to 2030. AdaniConneX constructs the sheds, Airtel's Nxtra lays the fibre, Google supplies the chips, the Gemini models, and the cloud billing. CM Chandrababu Naidu laid the foundation stone, Union IT minister Ashwini Vaishnaw attended, and Google Cloud CEO Thomas Kurian was named on the announcement. The project was first signed off in October 2025 at the [India AI Impact Summit](https://theprint.in/economy/google-to-invest-15-bn-in-ai-hub-in-andhra-to-partner-adani-for-indias-largest-data-centre/2763583).
    catch: >-

Nobody has said whether Indian AI startups get preferential rates, or whether data processed on the Vizag campus stays here under DPDP rules. Hyperscaler data centres sit inside a global network, and ownership of workload data is a contract term, not a geographic one.
    forYou: >-
      If you're an Indian AI startup or developer running on Google Cloud, expect lower latency and better capacity on India regions over the next 18 months. If you're hoping for sovereign Indian AI — Indian models, Indian chips, Indian weights — this isn't it. This is a Google campus with Adani as landlord and Airtel as the fibre guy.

    body: >-
      Most Indian households run their digital payments through one person's UPI, because teens, elderly parents, or non-earning adults don't have their own. The usual workaround is sharing passwords, PINs, or whole phones. That is a scam waiting to happen. Open BHIM or PhonePe, add a family member to your UPI, and set a monthly limit (NPCI caps it at Rs 15,000 per delegate). Pick partial delegation if you want to approve every payment with your own PIN. Pick full delegation if you want them to transact on their own PIN. Teen needs lunch money? Done. Parent paying the electricity bill? Done. Nobody sees your bank login. The feature's been live for over a year and almost nobody has set it up — mostly because it's buried a layer deep in the menus. Fifteen minutes this weekend and you can finally stop sending your PIN over WhatsApp.
    image: "/images/daily/2026-11-03/illustration_3.png"
    problem: >-
      In millions of Indian households, the family's whole digital-payment life runs on one person's phone, because teens, elderly parents, and non-earning adults don't have their own UPI. The usual workaround is sharing passwords, PINs, or whole phones with other family members. That is a scam opening you have voluntarily created.
    breakthrough: >-
      [NPCI's UPI Circle](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/corporate/npci-bhim-services-launches-upi-circle-for-safe-delegated-payments-heres-how-it-works) feature lets you add a secondary user to your UPI with their own identity, approvals, and monthly spending limit. Partial delegation keeps your UPI PIN as the gatekeeper: the secondary user initiates, you approve each payment. Full delegation gives the secondary user their own PIN to transact on their own. Both are capped at Rs 15,000 per month, per delegate. Supported in BHIM and PhonePe today, rolling out across other UPI apps.
    catch: >-
      The Rs 15,000 monthly cap is low — fine for a teen's lunch and small bills, not for a shared household kitty, EMIs, or any serious spending. And the setup flow is buried a couple of layers deep in each app's menus; most people have simply never seen it.
    forYou: >-
      Set this up this weekend. Add one family member, start with partial delegation so you approve each spend, see how it feels for a month, then upgrade to full if it fits. It is the first real alternative to texting your UPI PIN to your spouse.
    question: "How do I let my family pay from my UPI without sharing my bank password?"
    answer: >-
      Use UPI Circle in BHIM or PhonePe. Add a family member, set a monthly spending limit (capped at Rs 15,000 per delegate), and pick partial delegation (you approve each payment with your PIN) or full delegation (they get their own PIN). No password or OTP sharing required.

Full delegation gives the secondary user their own UPI PIN, letting them pay independently without pinging you. Both modes cap each delegate at Rs 15,000 per month, per NPCI rules."
      - q: "Which Indian UPI apps support UPI Circle today?"
        a: "NPCI's own BHIM app supports both partial and full delegation and was the first to roll out full UPI Circle. PhonePe supports UPI Circle for delegated payments across its base. Google Pay has been rolling it out in stages. The feature is being added gradually across the UPI ecosystem. Check your app's UPI settings or Family section to see if it is live for you."
