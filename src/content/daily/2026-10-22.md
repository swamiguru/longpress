    heading: "eMudhra's secret keys were numbers you could guess"
    body: >-
      Google, Mozilla, Apple and Microsoft blocked eight intermediate certificates from Indian certificate authority eMudhra between October 17 and 20, 2026, after a researcher found their private keys were numbers like 4, 56 and 115, [Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/10/21/emudhra-certificates-private-keys-compromised/) reported. That's the kind of mistake you'd expect from a student project, not a company whose whole job is vouching that a website is who it says it is. The browsers reacted within hours, which is the system working. What worries me more is the long tail of apps and servers that never check whether a certificate was revoked.
    image: "/images/daily/2026-10-22/illustration_1.png"
    problem: >-
      Every padlock in your browser depends on a few hundred certificate authorities keeping their private keys secret and properly random. If one of them slips, someone can impersonate a bank or a government site and your browser will happily show the padlock.
    breakthrough: >-
      A researcher called Wayne spotted the flaw on October 17 and signed a message with all eight keys to prove it, per [Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/10/21/emudhra-certificates-private-keys-compromised/). The keys came from an April 2025 ceremony where a faulty script created weak lookalike keys. Chrome and Firefox blocked them the same day, Apple followed on October 19 and Microsoft on October 20, and [The420](https://the420.in/emudhra-shares-plunge-lower-circuit-certificate-keys-compromised/) reports eMudhra's shares fell about 20% to the lower circuit.
    catch: >-
      The keys were effectively public for about 17 months and nobody can say for sure they weren't used. Scott Helme found curl, Python, Node, Go and Java would accept a forged certificate for Stripe's API, according to [CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/tech/2026/10/21/a-company-that-vouches-for-websites-had-secret-keys-so-weak-that-8-of-them-were-cracked).
    forYou: >-
      Update your browser and phone today if you've been putting it off. If you build software, update your language runtime and certificate bundles, because revocation won't reach a static trust store on its own.
    question: "What happened with the eMudhra certificate keys in October 2026?"
    answer: >-
      In October 2026, Google, Mozilla, Apple and Microsoft blocked eight intermediate certificates from Indian certificate authority eMudhra after a researcher found their private keys were tiny numbers like 4, 56 and 115. Anyone could have forged HTTPS certificates with them. No misuse has been reported.
    qa:
      - q: "Why were eMudhra's certificate keys so weak?"
        a: "eMudhra traced it to a faulty script used in an April 2025 key ceremony, which created a second set of weak keys with labels that looked like the real ones, and staff picked the wrong set. It admitted human error at several steps and says it will re-check every key ceremony it has run."
      - q: "Do I need to do anything after the eMudhra certificate compromise?"
        a: "Update your browser and operating system, since Chrome, Firefox, Apple and Microsoft have already blocked the eight certificates. The bigger risk is software that never checks revocation: Scott Helme showed curl, Python, Node, Go and Java accepting a forged certificate, so developers should update trust stores and libraries."
      - q: "How did eMudhra shares react to the key compromise?"
        a: "eMudhra shares fell about 20% and hit the lower circuit on October 21, 2026, according to The420. The company says only one of the eight intermediates went into production, none issued customer certificates, and about 30,000 customer certificates under the affected root will still need replacing."

    heading: "Stop strangers finding you on Telegram by your number"
    body: >-
      Telegram lets you hide your number and block strangers from finding you by it: Settings > Privacy and Security > Phone Number, set it to Nobody, then set 'Who can find me by my number' to My Contacts, as [Bitdefender](https://www.bitdefender.com/en-us/blog/hotforsecurity/control-your-privacy-series-how-to-hide-your-phone-number-on-telegram) walks through. It takes half a minute. With SBI investigating a suspected leak of customer phone numbers this week, it's a good time to shut one of the easiest lookups a scammer has.
    image: "/images/daily/2026-10-22/illustration_3.png"
    problem: >-
      Phone numbers leak constantly, from bank records to shopping sites, and on Telegram a number can be a lookup key for your name, photo and a direct chat.
    breakthrough: >-
      Two settings fix it. Set who can see your number to Nobody, and the extra option 'Who can find me by my number' appears; set that to My Contacts.
    catch: >-
      People already in your contacts can still find you, and a public username works as a way in regardless. Hiding your number doesn't hide your account from Telegram itself.
    forYou: >-
      Do it now, then check your username isn't your full name. If you don't use Telegram much, it's still worth doing, since a dormant profile with your photo is just as easy to look up.
    question: "How do I stop people finding me on Telegram by my phone number?"
    answer: >-
      Open Telegram, go to Settings > Privacy and Security > Phone Number, set 'Who can see my phone number' to Nobody, then set 'Who can find me by my number' to My Contacts. Someone who only has your number can then no longer pull up your Telegram profile or start a chat.
    qa:
      - q: "Can my saved contacts still find me on Telegram after hiding my number?"
        a: "Yes. With 'Who can find me by my number' set to My Contacts, people saved in your phone's contacts can still find you and see your number. Strangers who got your number from a leaked list or a WhatsApp group can't use it to look up your Telegram profile, name or photo."
      - q: "Does hiding my number make me anonymous on Telegram?"
        a: "No. Telegram still needs a phone number to create the account and keeps account data such as your username, IP address and device details, according to Bitdefender. If you share a public username, anyone can still message you through it, so pick one that doesn't reveal your full name."

    heading: "A caller knowing your SBI details doesn't make them SBI"
    body: >-
      SBI is investigating a suspected leak of customer data after alerts on October 16, 2026, with 3,897 records reportedly carrying names, mobile numbers, dates of birth and addresses, [The420](https://the420.in/sbi-dark-web-customer-data-leak-3897-personal-information-investigation/) reported. The myth this feeds is an old one: if a caller knows your details, they must be from the bank. It's the opposite. Leaked details are what make a fake call convincing, and they tell you nothing about who's on the line.
    image: "/images/daily/2026-10-22/illustration_4.png"
    problem: >-
      Fake bank calls work because callers sound informed. When someone reads out your date of birth and branch, most people relax, which is exactly what the script is designed to do.
    breakthrough: >-
      SBI received alerts from its threat intelligence provider, the Defence Cyber Agency and CERT-In on October 16 and filed a disclosure on October 21, per [The420](https://the420.in/sbi-dark-web-customer-data-leak-3897-personal-information-investigation/). A preliminary review of about 375,000 dark web records found 3,897 with personal information, including CIF IDs and loan metadata. SBI has brought in a CERT-In-empanelled agency.
    catch: >-
      SBI hasn't confirmed the records are genuine or where they came from, and it says the exposure hasn't materially affected its operations. That's the honest state of it: suspected, not proven.
    forYou: >-
      Whether or not this particular leak is real, the rule holds for every bank. Hang up, call the number on your card, never share an OTP or PIN, and call 1930 at once if money goes.
    question: "Is SBI customer data leaked on the dark web in October 2026?"
    answer: >-
      SBI said on October 21, 2026 that it is investigating a suspected leak after alerts from October 16. A review reportedly found 3,897 records with names, mobile numbers, dates of birth and addresses. SBI hasn't confirmed the data is genuine and says core operations aren't affected.
    qa:
      - q: "What SBI customer details were reportedly exposed?"
        a: "The records reportedly include registered mobile numbers, dates of birth, names, gender, email addresses and home addresses, plus branch identifiers, Customer Information File IDs and loan or lead metadata, according to The420. SBI says it hasn't yet established the authenticity, source or full extent of the exposure."
      - q: "What should SBI customers do after the suspected data leak?"
        a: "Treat any call quoting your date of birth, branch or account details with more suspicion, not less. Hang up and call back on the number printed on your card or in the official app, never share an OTP, PIN or screen-share, and call 1930 immediately if money leaves your account."
    sources:
      - label: "The420"
        url: "https://the420.in/sbi-dark-web-customer-data-leak-3897-personal-information-investigation/"
