    body: >-
      Meta released Muse Spark Open on October 15, an open-weight model with 816 billion parameters (42 billion active) and a 1 million token context, built for coding and agent work, per [Meta](https://research.meta.ai/blog/muse-spark-open). It's the most interesting thing Meta has shipped in AI this year, mostly because the US has spent two years watching Chinese labs own the open-weight leaderboard. The asterisk sits on the "runs on Macs" headline: [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/15/meta-launches-an-open-weight-model-that-runs-on-macs/) says it needs around 1TB of memory, which in Meta's setup means two M5 Ultra Mac Studios.
    problem: >-
      Most of the strongest open-weight models people actually run come out of Chinese labs, while US companies kept their best work behind paid APIs.
    breakthrough: >-
      Meta's Superintelligence Labs put the weights on Hugging Face and the big clouds, with API access through Groq, Together AI, OpenRouter and others from day one. Apple worked with Meta so it runs on Apple silicon through MLX, and Meta showed it fixing bugs and building features in a Mastodon client using its Muse Code terminal.
    catch: >-
      The launch page has no benchmark numbers and no named rivals, just a claim and a link to a methodology report. The licence is Meta's own with limited commercial terms, so open here means open weights, not open source.
    forYou: >-
      If you build with AI, try it through an API provider this week and judge it on your own tasks. If you were hoping to run it on a laptop, you can't.
    question: "What is Meta's Muse Spark Open model and can it run on a Mac?"
    answer: >-
      Muse Spark Open is an open-weight AI model Meta released on October 15, 2026, with 816 billion parameters, 42 billion active and a 1 million token context. It does run on Macs, but Meta's setup uses two M5 Ultra Mac Studios with about 1TB of memory combined.
    qa:
      - q: "Where can I use Meta's Muse Spark Open?"
        a: "Meta lists API access through Baseten, Fireworks AI, Groq, Modal, Nebius, OpenRouter and Together AI, and weight downloads on Hugging Face, Google Cloud Model Garden, Amazon SageMaker and Microsoft Foundry. It also works with vLLM, SGLang, llama.cpp and Apple's MLX. Pricing is set by each API provider."
      - q: "Is Meta's Muse Spark Open free for commercial use?"
        a: "Partly. The weights ship under Meta's own Muse Model License, which Meta says lets many developers use it for free but carries limited commercial terms. It isn't an open-source licence like Apache or MIT, so a business should read the licence before building a product on Muse Spark Open."
      - q: "Is Muse Spark Open better than DeepSeek or Qwen?"
        a: "Meta says it's the most capable US open-weight model and beats other open models in its own evaluations, but the launch post publishes no benchmark scores and names no rivals. Until the full evaluation report and independent tests arrive, treat any comparison with Chinese open models as Meta's claim."

    heading: "Lock your Aadhaar fingerprints. It takes one tap"
    body: >-
      The new Aadhaar app, which replaced mAadhaar on June 30, lets you lock or unlock your biometrics with a single tap, per [Angel One](https://www.angelone.in/news/personal-finance/new-aadhaar-app-launched-check-7-big-features-every-user-should-know). Most people have never touched it, which is a shame, because a locked fingerprint is one less thing a fraudster can use. Turn it on, unlock it for the few minutes a bank or ration shop needs it, and lock it again.
    problem: >-
      Your Aadhaar fingerprint works like a password you can never change, and fingerprint checks don't ask you for anything else.
    breakthrough: >-
      Once you turn the lock on, [UIDAI](https://uidai.gov.in/en/contact-support/have-any-question/925-english-uk/faqs/aadhaar-online-services/biometric-lock-unlock.html) says your biometrics stay locked until you unlock them temporarily or switch the system off. The new app puts that toggle up front, alongside authentication history, QR sharing and face authentication.
    catch: >-
      You need a mobile number linked to Aadhaar, and if it isn't, that's a trip to an enrolment centre. You'll also have to remember to unlock before any fingerprint check, which gets mildly annoying at a bank counter.
    forYou: >-
      Install the new Aadhaar app, lock your biometrics and look at the authentication history while you're in there. If you spot a use you don't recognise, report it to UIDAI.
    question: "How do I lock my Aadhaar biometrics in the new Aadhaar app?"
    answer: >-
      Open the new Aadhaar app, which replaced mAadhaar in June 2026, find the Biometric Lock option and switch it on. Your biometrics then can't be used for Aadhaar authentication until you unlock them or turn the lock off. You need a mobile number linked to Aadhaar.
    qa:
      - q: "What happens when my Aadhaar biometrics are locked?"
        a: "UIDAI says locked biometrics stay locked until you either unlock them temporarily or disable the locking system, so a fingerprint check for Aadhaar authentication won't go through in the meantime. That's the point: a copied fingerprint is useless to anyone else while the lock is on."
      - q: "Can I unlock my Aadhaar biometrics when I need them?"
        a: "Yes. UIDAI lists its website, Aadhaar enrolment centres, Aadhaar Seva Kendras and the app as places to unlock them. Unlock right before a bank visit, a ration shop or any other fingerprint check, then lock your biometrics again in the Aadhaar app once you're done."
      - q: "Why can't I lock my Aadhaar biometrics?"
        a: "The usual reason is a mobile number that isn't linked to your Aadhaar. UIDAI says a registered mobile number is essential for locking and unlocking, and if yours isn't linked you'll have to visit an Aadhaar enrolment centre or a mobile update point first."

    heading: "Ask AI before you send money to a 'tip'"
    body: >-
      An 83-year-old retired businessman in Pune lost ₹1.57 crore after a fake social media ad used N.R. Narayana Murthy's name and photo, according to [The420.in](https://the420.in/india-cyber-tech-brief-top-10-cybersecurity-ai-news-15-october-2026/), which covered the complaint reported on October 14. The scam ran for months over WhatsApp and calls, and he sold shares and mutual funds to keep paying across 49 transactions. Here's a prompt worth saving for the next too-good offer that lands on your phone, or your parents' phones.
    problem: >-
      Fake ads with famous Indian faces work because they borrow trust, and once a victim is chatting with a friendly 'adviser' on WhatsApp, every payment feels like the next sensible step.
    breakthrough: >-
      Paste the ad, message or screenshot text into ChatGPT, Gemini or Claude with this: "I received this investment offer. List every red flag you see, explain what a genuine registered adviser would never ask for, and tell me what I should verify independently before paying anyone." It costs a minute.
    catch: >-
      A chatbot can't confirm whether a celebrity photo or video is real, and a slick scam can read clean. Treat the answer as a speed bump, not a verdict.
    forYou: >-
      Save the prompt as a note on your parents' phones too. Any famous person selling stock tips in an ad is fake until their verified accounts say otherwise.
    question: "How can I use ChatGPT or Gemini to check an investment scam?"
    answer: >-
      Paste the ad or WhatsApp message into ChatGPT, Gemini or Claude and ask it to list every red flag and what to verify before paying. It won't catch every scam, but it forces a pause, which fake celebrity investment ads like the Narayana Murthy one in Pune count on you skipping.
    qa:
      - q: "What happened in the Pune Narayana Murthy investment scam?"
        a: "An 83-year-old retired businessman in Pune lost ₹1.57 crore after a fake social media ad used N.R. Narayana Murthy's name and photo. Scammers followed up on WhatsApp and calls, he sold shares and mutual funds, and investigators found 49 transactions across several accounts. The complaint was reported on October 14, 2026."
      - q: "What should I ask AI to check in an investment offer?"
        a: "Ask it to flag guaranteed or unusually high returns, pressure to act fast, a move to WhatsApp or Telegram, payments to personal or changing bank accounts, and celebrity endorsements. Then ask what you should verify independently, through official sources, before any money leaves your account."
      - q: "Can AI tell if a celebrity investment ad is a deepfake?"
        a: "Not reliably. ChatGPT, Gemini or Claude can point out suspicious wording and patterns, but they can't confirm whether a photo or video of a famous person is real. Treat any celebrity pushing an investment platform in an ad as fake until their own verified accounts say otherwise."

    heading: "Meta's new terms reach into your website"
    body: >-
      Meta's updated commercial terms take effect October 30 and let it crawl websites associated with business accounts and use that content to train its products, legal experts told [Nieman Lab](https://www.niemanlab.org/2026/10/metas-new-commercial-terms-will-allow-the-scraping-of-news-sites-legal-experts-say/). There's no opt-out except stopping commercial use of Facebook, Instagram, Messenger and WhatsApp, which for most publishers and creators is no choice at all. Meta says it only consolidated old language and that robots.txt still works. If that's true, it costs Meta nothing to write it into the terms.
    problem: >-
      Publishers and creators need Meta's apps for reach, and AI companies want their writing for training. Until now robots.txt was the line most sites leaned on.
    breakthrough: >-
      The new terms authorise Meta and its agents to run scrapers on sites linked to business accounts, train on what they collect, and create 'test credentials' to get access. Cornell's James Grimmelmann called it clear permission to train, and another law professor compared it to antitrust tying.
    catch: >-
      Nobody knows yet whether a court would enforce this, and AI training still rests on unsettled fair-use law. Meta's spokesperson insists its crawling won't change in practice.
    forYou: >-
      If your business page links to your website, assume the clause covers you. Keep your robots.txt blocks for Meta's crawlers, note the date you set them, and push Meta for a written carve-out before October 30.
    question: "Can Meta use my website content to train AI under its new terms?"
    answer: >-
      Yes, according to legal experts reading Meta's commercial terms that take effect October 30, 2026. They let Meta crawl websites associated with business accounts on Facebook, Instagram, Messenger or WhatsApp and train on the content, with no opt-out except stopping business use of Meta.
    qa:
      - q: "Who is affected by Meta's October 30 commercial terms?"
        a: "Any business account that manages a Facebook page, Messenger, Instagram or a WhatsApp channel, which covers almost every news publisher and plenty of creators and small brands. The clause reaches websites 'associated with' those accounts, a phrase Meta's commercial terms don't tightly define."
      - q: "Does robots.txt still block Meta's crawler?"
        a: "Meta's spokesperson told Nieman Lab that publishers can still manage access with robots.txt and that its crawling won't change. Legal experts warned the terms could let Meta argue its contract overrides robots.txt, and Meta didn't say whether its 'test credentials' could get past paywalls."
      - q: "What can publishers and creators do before Meta's October 30 terms?"
        a: "There's no opt-out short of stopping business use of Meta's products. Keep robots.txt rules for Meta's crawlers in place, record when you set them, and watch for a written clarification from Meta. Anyone with paywalled content should ask Meta directly about the test-credentials clause."
    sources:
