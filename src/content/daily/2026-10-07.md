    heading: "Hold Option + Shift for 64 volume steps on a Mac"
    body: >-
      On a Mac, hold Option and Shift while you press the volume or brightness keys and each press moves a quarter of a normal step, so the usual 16 levels become 64. It's the fix for that late-night call where one notch is too loud and the one below it is basically silent, and it works for screen brightness in a dark room too.
    image: "/images/daily/2026-10-07/illustration_3.png"
    problem: >-
      Mac volume and brightness keys jump in 16 fairly big steps. At night, or with sensitive earphones, the gap between two of those steps can be the difference between too loud and inaudible.
    breakthrough: >-
      macOS has a long-standing modifier for this: hold Option + Shift and press the volume up/down or brightness up/down keys, and each press moves a quarter step. The on-screen indicator shows the finer movement as you go.
    catch: >-
      It needs the dedicated volume and brightness keys, so on an external keyboard without them you're back to Control Centre's slider. It doesn't do anything for apps that manage their own volume separately.
    forYou: >-
      Try it next time you're on a call with earphones in. Once you've used it a few times, the regular steps feel clumsy.

    kind: myth-buster
    heading: "Dark mode barely saves battery at normal brightness"
    body: >-
      Dark mode isn't the battery fix people think it is. Purdue researchers measured it on OLED phones and found big savings only at full brightness, while at the 30-50% brightness most of us actually use the gain was a few percent. On LCD screens it does close to nothing. Keep it because it's easier on your eyes at night, and fix brightness and screen-on time if battery is the real complaint.
    image: "/images/daily/2026-10-07/illustration_5.png"
    problem: >-
      Dark mode got sold as a battery hack along with the night-time comfort, and a lot of people keep it on purely for the savings. If your phone is dying by evening, that belief points you at the wrong setting.
    breakthrough: >-
      A 2021 Purdue University study of OLED phones found that switching from light to dark mode at 100% brightness cut display power by 39-47%. At 30-50% brightness, where auto-brightness keeps most phones indoors, the saving dropped to 3-9%. LCD panels light the whole screen with a backlight, so dark pixels save almost nothing there.
    catch: >-
      Your exact number depends on the app, how dark its theme really is and your screen, and if you live at max brightness outdoors the saving is real. The study measured display power, not total battery life, so the effect on your day is smaller still.
    forYou: >-
      Use dark mode because you like it. For battery, turn down brightness or let auto-brightness work, cut screen-on time, and watch for weak-signal areas, which quietly burn more than any theme.
