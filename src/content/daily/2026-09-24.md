      One hidden YouTube setting fixes its most annoying habit — ads blasting your ears, then a quiet video you can barely hear. Turn on Stable Volume from the gear/settings icon in any video, under Additional settings. It evens out the loud and quiet parts within a video and normalizes volume across videos, so you stop reaching for the volume rocker every couple of minutes. 🔊
    image: "/images/daily/2026-09-24/illustration_3.png"
    problem: >-
      Volume swings wildly between YouTube videos and ads, forcing constant manual adjustment.
    breakthrough: >-
      YouTube's built-in Stable Volume toggle (player settings gear → Additional settings → Stable Volume) auto-levels loud and quiet sections and normalizes loudness across clips — no third-party app or headphone setting needed.
    catch: >-
      It's a normalization, not a limiter — genuinely dynamic content like music or films may sound slightly flattened, so toggle it off when audio fidelity matters.
    forYou: >-
      Turn it on once and stop babysitting the volume button; switch it off for music if you want full dynamic range.

      Before your next phone, here's the honest eSIM-versus-physical-SIM breakdown. eSIM wins on instant activation, no tray, brilliant travel and dual-SIM support, and it can't be popped out by a thief — but switching phones means re-activating with your carrier. A physical SIM wins on moving to any phone in seconds and easy backup to a spare, but it's slower to set up and limited to one number per slot.     breakthrough: >-
      The trade-off is clear once you name it: eSIM offers instant remote activation, dual-SIM flexibility, theft resistance and reclaimed internal space; a physical SIM offers device portability, easy backup, and universal compatibility with no carrier re-provisioning.
    catch: >-
      eSIM support and easy transfer still vary by carrier and country, and moving an eSIM between phones can mean a support call — physical SIMs remain the fuss-free option for frequent device swappers.
    forYou: >-
      Heavy traveler or want theft resistance? Choose eSIM. Frequently switch phones or keep a backup device? Stick with a physical SIM.

    kind: myth-buster
    heading: "More RAM does not make your phone faster"
    body: >-
      Time to bust India's biggest phone-spec myth: 'more RAM equals a faster phone.' Once you have enough, extra RAM barely moves real-world speed. For most people 8GB is plenty and 12GB is comfortable headroom; 16GB and beyond is largely a number you'll never feel. What actually makes a phone fast is the chipset, the UFS storage read/write speed, and software optimization. Don't pay a premium for RAM you'll never use. 🧠
    image: "/images/daily/2026-09-24/illustration_5.png"
    problem: >-
      Phone marketing in India leans hard on huge RAM figures, pushing buyers toward pricier 12/16GB-plus variants for speed they may never notice.
    breakthrough: >-
      RAM helps only up to the point where your active apps fit; beyond that it's headroom, not speed. Real-world responsiveness is driven by the SoC, storage (UFS generation), and how well the software manages memory and background apps.
    catch: >-
      Heavy multitaskers, gamers, and people who keep dozens of apps live do benefit from more headroom — and cheaper phones sometimes pair big RAM with slow storage, so specs must be read together, not in isolation.
    forYou: >-
      For most buyers, 8–12GB is the sweet spot. Spend the extra money on a better chipset and faster storage instead of chasing RAM numbers.
