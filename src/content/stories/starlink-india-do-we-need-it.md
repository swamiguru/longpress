In May 2025, people familiar with the matter told [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/05/08/starlinks-launch-in-india-now-a-matter-of-when-not-if) that Starlink's official rollout in India would take six to nine months. It is October 2026. This week the argument went public.

At the India Mobile Congress, Starlink's Lauren Dreyer said the company has built [20 gateway sites with hundreds of antennas](https://www.businesstoday.in/technology/news/story/starlink-nearly-india-ready-20-gateways-in-place-but-one-clearance-still-pending-560176-2026-10-07) and signed MoUs with Airtel and Jio. Musk then wrote that "certain oligarchs" are blocking the launch to protect a "monopolistic chokehold," and the next day [pointed at Ambani](https://www.forbes.com/sites/antoniopequenoiv/2026/10/08/elon-musk-suggests-indias-richest-person-mukesh-ambani-stymying-starlink-launch/). The Ministry of Communications called the claim [baseless](https://www.forbes.com/sites/siladityaray/2026/10/08/baseless-indian-government-rejects-musks-claim-oligarchs-are-blocking-starlink-launch/). Its position is that Starlink, Jio Satellite and OneWeb all hold licences, all are in security assessment, and all are at roughly the same stage.

That last part is checkable, and it is only half the picture. The Digital Communications Commission [approved most of TRAI's spectrum recommendations](https://www.etvbharat.com/en/technology/digital-communications-commission-approves-trai-recommendations-on-satcom-spectrum-enn26090806612) on September 3. Spectrum will be assigned, not auctioned. The fee is 5% of adjusted gross revenue, with [4% for rural and remote areas](https://thewire.in/tech/dot-approves-trais-proposal-for-satellite-broadband-services-in-india), for five years. That still needs Cabinet approval and a second round of security clearance. A [Swarajya report](https://swarajyamag.com/news-brief/satellite-broadband-in-india-gets-push-as-dcc-approves-spectrum-usage-charges) says Starlink will also need FDI approval. So the remaining delay has named steps. Whether any of them is being slowed on purpose is the part nobody has shown.

Now the question the fight skips: do you need it?

India's internet gap is real. At the end of March, rural India had [48.31 internet subscribers per 100 people against 76.59 nationally](https://www.theweek.in/news/biz-tech/2026/06/23/india-telecom-growth-digital-divide.html). But most of those connections are mobile. In July, India had [48.14 million wired broadband connections and 18.84 million fixed wireless ones](https://theprint.in/india/indias-telecom-subscriber-base-crosses-1-35-billion-in-july-broadband-growth-picks-up-trai/3027591/). Of the 13.21 million on 5G home broadband, just over half were rural. Jio and Airtel are already selling the product Starlink would compete with, in villages.

That narrows Starlink's real market to addresses with no fibre and no usable 5G signal, plus ships, border posts and disaster backup. That is a legitimate market. It is not most readers.

![A row of connected houses along a fibre cable and under a cell tower, with the cable cut off short of a lone hilltop house that has only a satellite dish on its roof](/images/known-issue/starlink-india-do-we-need-it-body.webp)

Bangladesh is the closest test. Starlink priced residential at Tk 6,000 a month, against local broadband [starting at Tk 400 for a 5 Mbps plan](https://online91.thedailystar.net/business/news/starlink-bangladesh-blazing-fast-few-can-afford-4150951). Starlink's plan is rated up to 300 Mbps, so the gap is not like for like. Even so, a year in, it had [5,389 customers](https://www.ajkerpatrika.com/en/technology/ajpmhyhwwvpns), mostly corporate. India has no published price to compare. The ₹8,600 a month and ₹34,000 kit that went around were, per Starlink, [placeholder test data](https://www.techloy.com/starlink-says-leaked-india-prices-were-placeholders-as-launch-awaits-regulatory-approval/).

Meanwhile Jio is reported to be planning [about 1,600 satellites](https://the420.in/starlink-india-launch-delay-jio-satellite-network/) of its own. The companies that say Starlink is being held back are also the ones building the alternative, and distributing it.

That is the known issue. The launch has been a few months away for 17 months, and every month the argument is about who is allowed to sell satellite internet. Whether it works for your house is still unanswered, because there is no price to answer it with.
